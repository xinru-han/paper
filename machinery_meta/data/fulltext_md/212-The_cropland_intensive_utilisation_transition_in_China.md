## ABSTRACT

Understanding the transition process of cropland-use intensification can help reconcile the relationship between food demand and ecological conservation. The transition of cropland-use intensification is a trend developing in the continuous reorganisation and optimization of factor inputs with the changes in socio-economics, policies and institutions. From the induced factor substitution perspective, a transition of cropland-use intensification occurs when the agricultural entities adjust the structure of factor inputs in response to changes in the relative prices of production factors. This paper established an analytical framework for identifying the transition of cropland-use intensification and conducted an empirical study based on evidence from China between 1978 and 2019. The empirical study revealed a four-phase transition process of cropland-use intensification in China, named the 'labour-force-driven intensification', the yield-increasing-factors-driven intensification', the 'laboursaving-factors-driven intensification', and the innovation-driven intensification'. This paper also investigated the transition trajectory of cropland-use intensification in nine different agricultural zones and explored the influencing factors that contributed to the heterogeneity of the transition trajectory among different zones. Furthermore, this paper discussed the policy initiative to accelerate the transition of cropland-use intensification to the fourth stage to benefit the ecological environment and farmers' livelihood and ultimately be of great significance in promoting the sustainable development of agriculture in China.

## 1. Introduction

Land-use intensity implies the degree of adoption of land management practices in order to increase land output from a given area of land (Erb et al., 2013)., which has become a critical aspect of land-use science (Kühling et al., 2016; van der Sluis et al., 2016; Liu, 2018), intensification of land-use may help to meet the increasing demand for land products caused by the growing population and economy given the limited land resources (Mauser et al., 2015; Willy et al., 2019). For cropland specifically, intensification of cropland-use refers to increasing yield output on a given area by adding agricultural production factors. With the rapid urbanisation process, intensifying cropland use becomes necessary to maintain the supply of food and other agricultural products (Brussaard et al., 2010; Mahon et al., 2018; Scherer et al., 2018). However, empirical studies suggest that increasing intensification of cropland use often accompanies considerable environmental degradation and biodiversity loss (Bais-Moleman et al., 2019; Folberth et al., 2020; Millard et al., 2021). Long-term and high-intensity cropland utilisation increases pressure on the environment (Phalan et al., 2016), which urges sustainable intensification of cropland-use to coordinate the objectives of securing food supply and environmental protection given the resource scarcity.

In recent years, there has been a shift towards discussing sustainable intensification of cropland use instead of solely promoting land use intensification (Cassman and Grassini, 2020). For instance, Niu et al. (2021) and Lyu et al. (2022) investigated the appropriate level of intensity for cropland utilisation and proposes a development direction to achieve sustainability; Areal et al. (2018) and Liang et al. (2021) proposed a development direction to achieve sustainable intensification of cropland use. Increasing cropland-use intensity by inputting more labour, energy, fertilisers, water, mechanisms, etc., can increase yields without expanding cropland, but this may have diverse environmental impacts (Springmann et al., 2018). For instance, the redundant use of fertilisers and pesticides can cause soil and water pollution (Kopittke et al., 2019), and intensifying mechanical power input may increase carbon emissions (Carlson et al., 2017). However, innovative factors, such as improving cropland management practices, can increase the ecological efficiency of cropland use (Adolwa et al., 2019). Given the merit in evaluating the magnitude of the intensity of cropland utilisation, there is still lacking a systematic knowledge of the mechanisms of the evolutionary process of cropland use intensification in existing studies.

The proposed concept of cropland-use intensification transition refers to the gradual evolution of production factors inputs (e.g. labour, capital, etc.) over an extended period of time, in response to socioeconomic developments, policies, and institutions (Long and Qu, 2018; Song et al., 2021). An in-depth exploration of this transition can shed light on how the intensification of different production factors has changed and provide a detailed account of the process of cropland-use intensification. Additionally, a thorough understanding of this transition is crucial for comprehending the relationship between cropland-use intensity and its environmental impact (Tilman et al., 2011; Garnett et al., 2013; Cassman and Grassini, 2020) and can guide policy-making towards achieving sustainable intensification (Liu et al., 2014; Niu et al., 2021; Liang et al., 2022).

This paper attempts to provide a theoretical explanation of the transition of intensive cropland utilisation based on the theory of induced technological innovation, investigate the evolution process of cropland-use intensification since China's reform and opening in the late 1970 s, and further promote the sustainable utilisation of cropland. Thus, this paper first classified the production factors into four categories, including labour force, yield-increasing factors (i.e. fertiliser and pesticide), labour-saving factors (i.e. machinery inputs) and innovation factors (i.e. agricultural management services). Based on the induced technological innovation theory, this paper establishes a theoretical framework to identify the transition of cropland-use intensification. Finally, the paper conducts an empirical test using agricultural statistics data from China from 1978 to 2019.

## 2. Transition of cropland-use intensification: a theoretical framework

The research on cropland-use intensification originated in economics (Brookfield, 1972; Foley et al., 2005; Moran et al., 2005). The law of diminishing returns is a classical theory in the field of cropland-use intensification and enriches the connotation of intensification (Stone et al., 2010). With the subsequent contribution of von Thünen, the connotation of intensive cropland utilisation has changed from focusing only on the input dimension to both the input and output dimensions (Liang et al., 2021). It has become a common view that intensive cropland utilisation is a strategy to meet the increasing global demand for food. This cropland management practice aims to maximise yields by inputting additional production factors into currently used cropland (Brauman et al., 2018; You et al., 2018).

According to the theory of induced technological innovation, the growth of agricultural productivity is partly the result of the continuous progress of modern agricultural technology (Hayami and Ruttan, 1985). Technological advancement is induced by the fluctuation of the relative price of production factors (Song and Li, 2019), which affect agricultural production and control – for example, changes in crop species, farmland vulnerability to biological disasters and the function of the cropland ecosystem (Song et al., 2020, 2021, 2022). Similarly, substitutions in agricultural factor inputs are affected by variations in their price. Rising prices lead to an increase in production costs. According to the price signals, agricultural business entities tend to replace costly and scarce factors with economical and relatively ample factors and strive to apply more technologies that can conserve relatively scarce factors (Hayami and Ruttan, 1985; Fienup et al., 1986). Price is an ingredient of the socioeconomic system (Song et al., 2021) and shifts the process of intensification, a phenomenon that is coupled with many social and economic factors (e.g. technological progress, rural outmigration and the growing importance in the economy of the industrial and service sectors) (Grainger, 1995; Lambin and Meyfroidt, 2010; Long and Qu, 2018).

A transition of cropland-use intensification occurs when it evolves into a certain pattern in the long term (Song et al., 2015; Chen et al., 2020). The transition of cropland-use intensification can be defined as the shift in patterns of intensive cropland utilisation that are differentiated by the structure of factor inputs, specifically changes in the structure of factor inputs from one state to another. The transition of cropland-use intensification is mainly driven by changes in the relative price of factors. The phasal feature is the crucial difference between the transition of cropland-use intensification and the change in cropland-use intensity. In this paper, we identify four production factors, namely labour force, yield-increasing factors (i.e. input to increase yield, including chemical fertiliser, plastic film, pesticide etc.), labour-saving factors (i.e. input to save the labour force, including mechanism etc.) and innovation.

The theoretical framework for the transition of cropland-use intensification is illustrated in Fig. 1. In the early stage, when the secondary and tertiary industries develop slowly and create limited employment opportunities in the urban area, a large number of labourers remained in the rural areas and are engaged in farming (Chen et al., 2009; Hao et al., 2015). At this time, with a low price for the agricultural labour force, agricultural production is mainly driven by human force and animal power – that is, there is labour-force-driven intensification’ (OA in Fig. 1).

The emergence of advanced technologies leads to a continuous decline in the price of capital factors. At the same time, the demand for the labour force in non-agricultural sectors increases, raising the price of agricultural labour (Long et al., 2012; Chen et al., 2014). When the price of yield-increasing factors is lower than that of the labour force, agricultural operators, rational economic people who seek to maximise profits, tend to purchase additional yield-increasing factors (Liu et al., 2016). At this time, agricultural production is mainly driven by yield-increasing factors – that is, there is yield-increasing-factor-driven intensification'(AB in Fig. 1).

As the economy develops, the price of yield-increasing factors and labour-saving factors declines continuously. Moreover, the greater requirement for ecology and the quality of agricultural products controls the supply of major yield-increasing factors such as pesticides and chemical fertilisers, and the opportunity cost of applying pesticides and chemical fertilisers increases (Song et al., 2020), leading to a rebound in the price of yield-increasing factors. When the price of labour-saving factors is lower than that of yield-increasing factors, agricultural production is mostly driven by labour-saving factors – that is, there is labour-saving-factor-driven intensification' (BC in Fig. 1).

Technological advances have accelerated the decline in the price of innovation factors (Renting et al., 2009). When the price of innovation is lower than that of labour-saving factors, the agricultural production is mostly driven by innovation – that is, there is innovation-driven intensification'(after point C in Fig. 1).

## 3.1.2. Measuring the price of each input factor

Assuming the optimal quantity of factor inputs is achieved, the marginal revenue of this production factor is equal to the marginal costs. To describe the relationship between the cost and revenue of production factors, the revenue and cost curves are plotted in Fig. 2. As the factor inputs increase, their total costs rise at a constant rate while the total revenue growth gradually slows. The crimson lines ①–③ are the tangent lines of the total revenue curve, representing the marginal revenue at different levels of factor inputs. The slope of the crimson lines ①-③ gradually decreases, indicating the property of diminishing marginal productivity. Fig. 2b plots the marginal revenue and cost curves. In a perfectly competitive market, the price of factors is determined by the market (Mankiw, 2015); as a result, the marginal cost curve is horizontal. However, the marginal revenue curve slopes downward because of diminishing marginal productivity (Mankiw, 2015). When the marginal revenue is greater than the marginal cost, additional factors input still result in a profit. On the other hand, when the marginal revenue is less than the marginal cost, additional factor inputs result in a deficit. Therefore, to maximise profit, agricultural entities input production factors up to the point where the marginal revenue and cost curves cross (point A in Fig. 2b).

In a perfectly competitive market, the price of production factors is determined by the marginal revenue of such factors, and the supply of factors equals the demand. At this time the optimal input' in the above analysis is achieved, and the equilibrium price of factors is equal to the marginal revenue under the current input level.

## 4. The transition of cropland-use intensification

## 4.1. Changing intensities and prices of factors

## 4.1.1. Evolution of cropland-use intensity

We calculated the production factor intensities by using Eq. 1 (Fig. 4). From 1978–2019, the intensity of labour force dropped by 73.67%, from 0.75 person/t to 0.20 person/t, while the intensities of yield-increasing factors, labour-saving factors and innovation rose by 161.52%, 249.58% and 2211.91%, changing from 0.02 kg/kg to

0.06 kg/kg, from 0.31 kw/t to 1.08 kw/t, and from 17.64 yuan/t to 407.78 yuan/t, respectively. It is easy to see a fluctuating intensity in labour-saving factors in 2016. The reason may lie in the adjustment of the statistical range.

Considering different units of the intensity of the four factors, we compared their standardised intensities (Fig. 5). The gradual perfection of the market economy and urbanisation accelerated the transfer of the agricultural labour force, leading to a decline in the intensity of the labour force. From 1978–2009, the intensity of yield-increasing factors showed an upward trend, indicating that since the Chinese economic reform, China's agricultural production has become increasingly dependent on pesticides and fertilisers, the main yield-increasing factors. However, the long-term extensive use of agricultural chemicals has led to ecological degradation. China's ecological civilisation strategy was put forward – including a series of measures such as Agricultural Double Reduction' (i.e. reducing the use of pesticides and chemical fertilisers) and an ecologically oriented agricultural subsidy system – and implemented to restrict the use of agricultural chemicals. Consequently, the intensity of yield-increasing factors dropped rapidly from 2010 to 2019. The increase in the intensity of labour-saving factors benefited from a central financial subsidy for the purchase of agricultural machinery as well as technological advances, both of which have lowered the operating costs of agricultural machinery and enhanced the spread of labour-saving factors. Agricultural operators increased purchases and application of labour-saving factors to free up additional labour force for secondary and tertiary industries. There was a rapid increase in the intensity of innovation after 2008, indicating that innovation has been strengthened since 2008. Undoubtedly, advanced agricultural techniques and farming measures will further contribute to grain production.

Considering different price units of the four factors, we compared standardised prices (Fig. 8). The graph shows a rapid decrease in the prices of yield-increasing factors, labour-saving factors and innovation and a remarkable increase in the price of the labour force. The evolution of the prices of the four factors are consistent with the theoretical framework.

The increase in the price of the labour force was mainly due to the impact of national policies on the factor market. A large number of individual economies have emerged in China since implementation of the Chinese economic reform in 1978, providing opportunities for rural labour to migrate to cities. In the same year, the large-scale spread of the household contract responsibility system has promoted higher productivity and increased surplus agricultural products, further encouraging employment transfers of agricultural labourers. The decline in the supply of agricultural labour since 1978 has led to an increase in its price. Since 1999, the establishment of a guide price level system for the Chinese labour market has sufficiently stimulated the fundamental regulatory effect of market mechanisms on wage distribution. As a result, the non-agricultural sector has played a more significant role in the transfer of the agricultural labour force, and the price of the labour force has risen rapidly under the regulation of the invisible hand' of the market.

The sharp decline in the price of yield-increasing factors, laboursaving factors and innovation factors from 1978 to 2019 has mainly been caused by technological advances. A series of incentives reduce the opportunity cost of applying agricultural mechanisms. including a reduced tax rate for enterprises, which produce agricultural mechanism products, a tax exemption for instruments and equipment used for scientific research, and discount loans for technical innovation of large agricultural mechanism, among other factors. Technological advances have facilitated the continuous renewal of innovation factors, a phenomenon that has resulted in the functional devaluation of the original innovation factors, and further degrading their prices.

## 4.2. China's transition to intensive cropland utilisation since 1978

According to the above results, the evolution of the prices and intensities of the four factors are consistent with the theoretical framework. The intensification of different input factors continuously varies in response to fluctuating prices of those factors. This ongoing optimization reveals distinct patterns of intensive cropland utilisation, a phenomenon referred to as the transition of cropland-use intensification. We identified the transition of cropland-use intensification according to the price difference of the four factors (Fig. 10). The price of yield-increasing factors was lower than that of the labour force, becoming the cheaper of the two factors around 1995. Around 2017, the price of yieldincreasing factors rebounded and the price of innovation was lower than yield-increasing factors, becoming the least expensive factor.

While the price of production factors may shift suddenly, the transition of cropland-use intensification is a gradual process. From 1978 to the early 1990 s, China's intensive cropland use was driven by the labour force. A great deal of manpower was input during this period. In the mid-to-late 2010 s, the price of labour-saving factors was very close to the price of yield-increasing factors, without a significant price advantage, so cropland utilisation in China was in the yield-increasingfactor-driven intensification' phase at this time. After that, from the mid-2010 s to 2019, cropland utilisation gradually changed to the innovation-driven intensification'.

## 4.3. Spatial differences in the transition of intensive cropland utilisation among the agricultural zones

China has a diverse geographical environment and human activities that have led to various changes in the characteristics of factor prices and intensities. The time and trajectory of the transition of cropland-use intensification varies across regions. Based on the evolution of factor prices and intensities, we investigated the heterogeneity of the transition of cropland-use intensification (Fig. 10).

Most zones completed the transition from labour-force-driven intensification’ to the next phase during 1994–1997, including SC, HHHP, LP, SBSR and MLYP. For a decade or more afterwards, in most agricultural zones cropland utilisation was driven by yield-increasing factors. At the end of the study period, the pattern of cropland utilisation in NCP, SC, HHHP, LP and MLYP had shifted to the innovationdriven intensification'. Although we cannot be sure whether this shift to ‘innovation-driven intensification’ is complete (based on data availability), the results still indicate that innovation has accelerated in China since 2005. After 2019, cropland utilisation in most agricultural zones is likely to gradually shift to innovation-driven intensification'.

Differences in geographical conditions and socioeconomic have led to early or late transitions in individual regions. With abundant cropland resources and fertile soil, NCP is an essential commodity grain base and supplies a large amount of high-quality agricultural products every year. As early as around 1991, NCP had moved away from labour-forcedriven intensification'. Conversely, YGP, dominated by Karst landform with thin and barren soil and rugged terrain that has hindered agricultural development, had moved away from labour-force-driven intensification' around 2000. With flat terrain, HHHP input mechanisms for grain production and has moved into the labour-saving-factors-driven intensification' since 2011. Conversely, characterized by low mountains and hills that hindered the scaled implication of mechanisms, SBSR moved into the labour-saving-factors-driven intensification’ later in 2017. Regions with better economic condition are inclined to place greater emphasis on the supply of innovation and are more capable of maintaining investment for innovation (Xiong et al., 2020; Dhar et al., 2023). With relatively backward economic levels, QTP and YGP have not yet entered the ‘innovation-driven intensification', while China entered the innovation-driven intensification' around 2017. Due to regional heterogeneity, the four phases did not emerge in each region. Only HHHP has gone through all four phases. Cropland utilisation in most zones of China has not gone into labour-saving-factor-driven intensification', which is closely related to traditional small-scale farming in China. Small-scale farming is an agricultural business mode in which the family is the production unit and relies entirely or mainly on its own labour to meet the demand for agricultural products, which leads to fragmentation of cropland property rights and management and further hinders agricultural mechanisation.

## 5. Policy implications

## 5.1. Policy induced transition of cropland-use intensification in China

The transition of cropland use intensification echoes the policy implemented nationwide. Since the reform and opening-up policy in early 1980 s a series of state policies have increased employment transfers from agriculture and driven up the price of labour force. In 1984, the Chinese government issued the Notice on Rural Work as the No.1 Central Document, permitting rural residents to work and trade in cities and towns. This significantly raised the cost of the labour force in agriculture. In 1999, the Ministry of Labours and Social Security introduced the labour market wage guidance pricing system. This system specified the minimum wage standards for urban residents, reinforced the rural-urban income gap, and further heightened the cost of labour in agriculture. Meanwhile, to enhance the efficiency of agricultural productivity, the State Council introduced the Program of Agriculture Development for the 1990 s. A series of national support measures were implemented to encourage the adoption of yield-increasing factors, effectively reducing their prices. Since entering the 21st century, a series of policies and regulations aimed at improving resource utilisation efficiency and promoting sustainable agricultural development have been successively introduced (i.e. the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Promotion of Agricultural Technology, 2012; the 13th Five-Year Special Plan for Agricultural and Rural Science and Technology Innovation, 2017 etc.), which effectively stimulated the adoption of laboursaving and innovation factors.

Ensuring sufficient food supply to meet high demand (Chen et al., 2016; Zhou et al., 2020) is unrealistic without increasing cropland intensity under the circumstance of high population and scarce land resources. Rapid urbanization and an aging population (Liu and Li, 2017; Liu et al., 2018) will reduce agricultural labourers, making it unlikely to intensify cropland use through increased labour input. China's ecological civilization construction policy, focusing on sustainable development (Li et al., 2018), makes the future production and adoption of yield-increasing and labour-saving factors more costly.

The empirical trend in socio-economic development suggested that cropland use in China will shift towards 'innovation-driven intensification' in the future. Since the 1990 s, China has supported agricultural research and technology adoption (Liu et al., 2021), gradually increasing the supply of innovation factors. With the expected continuous decline in innovation prices, 'innovation-driven intensification' is poised to replace environmentally costly intensification modes. Speeding up this transition is crucial for simultaneously securing food supply and reducing environmental impact.

## 5.2. Policy implications at the nation level

Accelerating the transition towards innovation-driven cropland-use intensification' can be achieved through two approaches: reducing the cost of innovation factors or internalising the externalities of yieldincreasing or labour-saving factors.

To reduce the cost of innovation factors, policies such as tax cuts or subsidies could be implemented. For example, China has reduced tax rates for agricultural machinery producers and offers discount loans for adopting advanced agricultural machinery (Liao et al., 2022). Since agricultural out-sourcing services emerge as one type of innovation factor, reducing loan interest rates for agricultural out-sourcing companies, training professional farmers or providing subsidies for out-sourcing services can also reduce the cost of innovation factors. Besides, technical advancement helps reduce innovation factors' costs and accelerate their spreading (Hayami and Ruttan, 1985). Therefore, encouraging investments in the research and development sector is expected to help accelerate the transition of cropland-use intensification towards the innovation-driven stage.

Fig. 10. The transition trajectory of cropland-use intensification.

On the other hand, introducing taxes on production factors with high environmental impact could increase the costs of yield-increasing factors. Several countries, including Sweden, France, Denmark, Norway, and Finland, have implemented taxes on pesticides and chemical fertilisers to reduce their usage (Böcker and Finger, 2016; Skevas, 2020). Alternatively, a carbon levy or polluting tax could be introduced to internalise the externalities associated with yield-increasing or labour-saving factors, thereby promoting a transition towards innovation-factor-driven stages of cropland use intensification.

Moreover, most regions of China have not yet entered the stage of labour-saving-factor-driven intensification’ due to small peasant ownership hindering the large-scale application of machinery (Wang et al., 2020). Thus, accelerating the circulation of cropland contractual management rights can benefit agricultural mechanisation. Subsidies can also be implemented to encourage small landowners to transfer their cropland and promote large-scale operations.

## 5.3. Region specified policy implications

Considering the heterogeneity of the transition of cropland-use intensification among regions, some regional strategies are proposed as follows.

For regions that have not yet entered the innovation-driven intensification', e.g. YGP, QTP and SBSR, there are two ways to accelerate the transition of cropland-use intensification. In one aspect, to lower the costs for transferring innovation factors from one region to another, e.g. improve transportation conditions for innovation factors, and establish a sharing mechanism (Pitakaso and Sethanan, 2019) for remote and economically underdeveloped areas to reach the innovation factors more conveniently. In another, to give some subsidies for advanced agricultural management skills, including smart agriculture, organic fertilizers, cultivating high-yield seeds, etc.

For regions that are going into or have entered the innovationdriven intensification', especially those with high economic levels, e.g. HHHP, MLYP and NCP, it is significant to maintain the transition trend of intensification and avoid retrograde transition. Since professionalize agricultural management increases the production efficiency and a strict agricultural cultivation standard reduces environmental outcome (Dik et al., 2023), both of them are considered helpful for keeping the transition of intensification sustainable. In fact, China has initiated to foster new-type professional farmers since 2012 (Liu et al., 2022), which deserves to be praised highly.

## 6. Discussion

## 6.1. Study applicability and practical significance

As the negative effect of intensification has attracted wide attention, sustainable intensification has been proposed as an essential measure to achieve sustainable agricultural development (Scherer et al., 2018; Cassman and Grassini, 2020). Nevertheless, previous studies have neglected the complete process of intensification and do not reflect over-use impacts on the environment (Liang et al., 2021, 2022). Based on the theory of induced technological innovation, we have established a complete framework for analysing the transition of cropland-use intensification, looking at the intensification process from a fresh perspective and proposing different intensification modes, which are of particular interest.

From a theoretical perspective, research on land intensification is a crucial component of land change science (van der Sluis et al., 2016; Liu, 2018), and therefore the study of land use intensification refines land change science. From a practical perspective, the intensification transition reflects the characteristics of intensive cropland use at different stages of socioeconomic development. Therefore, an in-depth understanding of the transition processes and mechanisms helps to improve the cropland conservation systems in countries or regions at different levels of development and to develop the food security strategies when there are limited cropland resources, which are crucial land resources relevant to national food security (Liu, 2018; Long and Qu, 2018). Analysis of trends in intensive cropland use in different zones contributes to formulate and refine differentiated land use policies based on local conditions (Ye et al., 2020). Furthermore, the pattern of cropland use driven by yield-increasing factors leads to chemical agriculture, while innovation factors reduce chemical application and increase production efficiency, suggesting that the intensification process in this paper reflects the impact on the environment. Identifying patterns of intensive cropland use provides a theoretical reference for achieving sustainable intensification.

## 6.2. Limitations and prospects

We have described the evolution and dynamic mechanism of cropland-use intensification and have analysed the differences in the transition of cropland intensification in the nine major agricultural zones of China. This paper fills the current gap of focusing only on intensity and neglecting the transition processes and provides an academic reference for land change science. However, there are still some shortcomings. Some metrics may be relatively broad due to the limitations in data accessibility. For example, primary industry workers are not only engaged in farming, and the value of agricultural services, in addition to socialised agricultural services, includes contributions from yieldincreasing and labour-saving factors. The uncertainty associated with the broad metrics may be eliminated by the standardised prices of the four factors we compared in this paper.

In this study, we choose the yield of grain crops as the cropland output as well as the price indicator of the input factors. While the demand and consumption of cash crops could influence cropland intensification (Pretty and Bharucha, 2014), we omitted the output of cash crops in this study for the sake of simplicity and data availability. Due to grains being the most widely cultivated crops in China, using grain output as the price indicator of input factors can effectively avoid the complex influence of long-term socio-economic fluctuations on currency prices in time-series studies. This approach also avoids introducing excessive uncertainty.

Considering the practitioner of cropland use and management, the gender, age and education level of farmers affect their understanding of sustainable intensification and ultimately affect the intensification of cropland utilisation (Niu et al., 2021; Lyu et al., 2022). Due to the limitations of data acquisition, we have not focused on the impact of individual differences among farmers on the intensification transition. This issue is of particular concern and deserves attention in its own study.

## 7. Conclusions

More attention needs to be given to the evolution of intensification for sustainable agriculture. Understanding the transition of cropland-use intensification is crucial to better conceptualise land-use intensification and to achieve sustainable intensification. Corresponding to the stage of socioeconomic development, the transition of cropland-use intensification is a process in which the input structure of production factors (e.g. labour, capital, etc.) is continuously reorganised and optimised. It is the result of the evolution of cropland intensification to a certain status over a lengthy period of time. We have developed a theoretical framework of the transition of cropland-use intensification from the perspective of induced factor substitution, and have conducted an empirical analysis with China's agricultural production data from 1978 to 2019. The empirical study of China indicates that the transition of cropland-use intensification generally includes four phases: the labour-force-driven intensification', the yield-increasing-factors-driven intensification', the 'labour-saving-factors-driven intensification', and the 'innovationdriven intensification'. The development direction of intensification in the nine agricultural zones of China is consistent with the framework, while there are some variances in the time and path of transition due to differences in socioeconomic and resource endowments. Most of the zones have not entered the labour-saving-factor-driven intensification'. The four types of intensified states appeared only in HHHP. The theory of induced technological innovation can be used to analyse the transition of cropland-use intensification. We have provided a complete analytical framework for cropland-use intensification, which is of fundamental scientific value for sustainable agriculture throughout the world.
