## Abstract

Purpose – This study estimates the extent of misallocation in agricultural production and analyzes how distortions drive wedges between the marginal products of capital and labor at the farm level. The study also evaluates potential aggregate total factor productivity and yield gains through the reduction of misallocation, while examining the impact of agricultural machinery outsourcing services (AMS) on misallocation.

Design/methodology/approach – We develop a theoretical framework that incorporates labor and capital distortions. We apply this framework to rice production in China, propose a resource distortion index and introduce sources of agricultural distortions at the smallholder farmer level.

Findings – The results reveal widespread farm-level resource misallocation in the surveyed region. Aggregate productivity (output) gains can be increased by 12%–52% if factors were reallocated from less productive to more productive farms. Our results further show that the use of AMS alleviates resource misallocation among farmers.

Originality/value – This study extends the existing quantitative framework and its applications by considering the distortions of capital and labor. Additionally, it estimates the role of AMS in agricultural misallocation and finds that AMS significantly alleviates resource misallocation.

Keywords Farm productivity, Resource misallocation, Allocative efficiency,

## 1. Introduction

The success of China's agricultural development since the 1980s has been attributed to rapid growth in aggregate agricultural productivity (Chen et al., 2022a, b; Ligon and Sadoulet, 2018), in which technological changes and improvements in technical and allocative efficiencies played irreplaceable roles. Recent studies have shown limited scope for boosting productivity through technological efficiency improvements under the prevailing technology (Liu et al., 2023a; Sun et al., 2022). In contrast, recent studies have found that severe factor misallocation has led to substantial losses in agricultural total factor productivity (TFP), although various adjustment costs may have been incurred (Restuccia, 2016; Sheng et al. 2017; Zhang et al., 2023). Therefore, reallocating productive factors to more productive units is a feasible approach for improving agricultural TFP (Hu et al., 2021; Restuccia and Rogerson, 2017).

Current literature has examined misallocation from the perspective of specific crops (Chen et al., 2022a), the entire agricultural sector (Zhou et al., 2019) and the agricultural and nonagricultural sectors (Liu et al., 2023b). They confirm that reallocation has resulted in significant aggregate output (productivity) gains in China (Restuccia and Rogerson, 2017). However, these studies have paid less attention to misallocations across individual farming households. Such distortions can also have a significant impact on the aggregate output (productivity). Therefore, this study uses farm-level data to analyze resource misallocation and address this gap.

Resource misallocation is prevalent in the agriculture of developing countries, including China (Adamopoulos et al., 2022; Hsieh and Klenow, 2009). Recent studies have shown that resource misallocation results from market friction, which disproportionately affects productive farmers and leads to substantial losses in agricultural productivity (Adamopoulos et al., 2022; Chari et al., 2020; Han et al., 2018; Restuccia, 2016). Most studies on input misallocation have focused primarily on institutional barriers to the land market.

In China, land-use rights have historically been administratively allocated by village collectives on an egalitarian basis according to household size. Although equitable, this system restricts more productive farmers from expanding their farming operations. Adamopoulos et al. (2022) and Zhang et al. (2023) find that such weak land institutions, characterized by egalitarian administrative allocation and limited land rental markets, create transaction frictions that contribute to land misallocation and losses in TFP.

However, according to Fuglie et al. (2019), land reform and optimized land allocation can only partially alleviate farm misallocation, and substantial productivity gains require reforms in other complementary factors, particularly in capital markets.

Intuitively, increasing allocative efficiency through capital markets such as agricultural machinery outsourcing services (AMS) could be a suitable alternative. As a form of labor division in agriculture, AMS leads to the adjustment in input structures (since factor markets are often interlinked), which effectively reduces labor drudgery and facilitates land transfer (Liu et al., 2019; Ma et al., 2018). To the best of our knowledge, there is no rigorous evidence regarding the effects of AMS on input misallocation.

This study aims to estimate the extent to which productive factors are misallocated at the farm level in China and explore the role of AMS in correcting misallocation. Therefore, we develop a theoretical framework to identify farm-level misallocations and seek improvements in TFP by calculating regional resource distortion. We then estimate the effect of AMS on resource (mis)allocation using farm-level data collected in 2020 from three major riceplanting provinces in China.

Our contributions to the literature are as follows: First, we measure the extent of the distortions in agricultural production and illustrate how capital and labor are misallocated. Specifically, we extend the quantitative frameworks of Hsieh and Klenow (2009) and Zhu et al. (2011) based on farm-level data to calculate allocative distortions in capital and labor, as well as a factor-distortion index at the farm level. Second, we examine the potential gains in aggregate output and productivity that result from efficient reallocation by calculating regional-level resource distortion. Third, we estimate the role of AMS in agricultural misallocation.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the theoretical framework of the study. Section 3 presents the data sources. Section 4 describes the measurement of resource misallocation. Section 5 presents empirical analyses of AMS and factor misallocation. Finally, Section 6 concludes the study and discusses the policy implications.

2.4 The influence of agricultural machinery services on resource misallocation

Over the past 3 decades, AMS have become increasingly prevalent and developed in China (Qiu and Luo, 2021; Yang et al., 2013). Studies have shown that these services have helped farmers improve their productivity (Qing et al., 2019).

Specifically, as shown in Figure 1, the use of AMS in agriculture enables farmers to alleviate labor constraints by replacing high-intensity manual labor with machinery. It also saves them capital by reducing the need to purchase their own machinery, thereby reducing farm misallocation.

Moreover, it affects resource allocation by alleviating technological constraints and improving input efficiency (Zou and Mishra, 2024). For example, small-scale farmers often face challenges in effectively controlling pesticide applications (in terms of frequency and quantity) because of their high technical requirements. However, AMS can help overcome these technical barriers (Cao et al., 2024), thereby reducing pesticide inputs and improving overall input efficiency (Zou and Mishra, 2024).

## 3. Data sources

From November to December 2020, a research team from the Beijing Institute of Technology surveyed rice farms in three Chinese provinces: Heilongjiang in the northeast, Hunan in the south and Jiangsu in the east. The three surveyed provinces ranked first, second and fourth, respectively, in rice output among all provinces, producing more than 35% of China's total rice by 2020 (National Bureau of Statistics of China, 2021).

Four counties were randomly selected from each province. From each county, three townships and one administrative village were randomly selected. In each village, 20 riceproducing farm households were randomly selected for face-to-face interviews from a list of households provided by the village committee, and 36 townships, covering 708 rice-farming households, were surveyed.

The survey provided complete information on crop inputs and outputs, enabling the construction of accurate outputs and productivity measures.

## 4. Measurement of resource misallocation

## 4.1 Measuring distortions

We use the dataset described in Section 3 to construct the farm-level output y, capital input K and labor input L in Equation (1). Following Chen et al. (2022b), Han et al. (2018) and Zhu et al. (2011), rice output denotes the rice output of the i th farmer. Capital inputs include all agricultural means of production in direct agricultural activities, including seeds, fertilizers and pesticides, as well as the leasing and fuel costs of agricultural machinery. The number of working days for planting, including days from family labor and AMS, represents labor input.

Furthermore, we use microdata to measure household distortions at the farm level. It should be noted that the production functions specified in Equation (1) implicitly assume homogeneous land input across farms, whereas, in practice, the dataset reveals substantial differences in land input across farms. Following conventions in the literature, we remove the variation in land input by normalizing output, labor and capital by land size.

Such a construction implies that we ignore the potential misallocation of land across farms, and therefore, the estimated misallocation may be conservative if land misallocation is substantial. Fortunately, this limitation is less concerning because land in rural China is allocated relatively equally across households under the land contract system; therefore, land cannot be effectively reallocated across farms in practice. Table 1 presents the descriptive statistics for these factors.

Measuring household distortions requires information on the parameters α and γ (input elasticities). The values we use are γ = 0.60, reflecting an income share of land of 0.40, and α = 0.43, implying a capital share of 0.43 and hence a labor income share of 0.17. These values, based on estimations for China's agriculture, fall within the range of elasticities of capital, labor and land reported by Zhu et al. (2011) for China (0.39, 0.18 and 0.43, respectively).

Measuring household distortions also requires information on factor prices. Considering the large differences in factor prices among the three provinces, we collect the prices of capital and labor for each province. The price of capital is determined as the interest rate of a one-year loan in 2020 plus one (i.e. 104.35%). The price of labor is represented by the average daily wage for hired agricultural labor, as reported by farmers, which ranges from 115 to 165 yuan.

Based on the parameters α and γ (input elasticities) and input prices, we separately derive capital (1 + τk) and labor (1 + τL) distortions by substituting the elasticity parameters into Equations (6)–(7) and combining the distortions for the factor inputs with Equations (10) and (12) to calculate household (D) and region (Dř) distortions.

We calculate the distortion levels (indicated by means) and standard deviations (indicated by standard deviations) separately across the three provinces.

## 4.2 Efficiency gains

Misallocation across households can be addressed by conducting counterfactual exercises to reallocate resources across farming households with different TFPs, thereby increasing agricultural output under constant total resources. To measure the efficiency gains from resource reallocation, we first obtain input elasticities from the estimation of production functions and calculate the parameters (τκ, τL, Di, Dī) using Equations (6), (7), (10) and (12). Second, using Equations (14) and (16) and the computed values of τK, τL, D, Dī, we can calculate the actual aggregate TFP (A) and efficient aggregate TFP (Ā). Finally, efficiency gains can be measured by the percentage change between efficient and actual TFP. Its respective value is equivalent to the change between the efficient output and the actual total output. The output gains achieved by eliminating distortions in each province vary across Heilongjiang, Jiangsu and Hunan (Table 3).

The estimated gain in aggregate output (productivity) from the efficient reallocation of land, capital and labor in Hunan Province is 51.5%, which is the highest among the three provinces. Heilongjiang Province ranks second with a potential increase of 19% in agricultural output. Reallocating factors increases the efficiency of Jiangsu Province by only 12.1%.

These values are smaller than those estimated in other studies conducted in China. For example, Zhu et al. (2011) focus on the short term and find that if all misallocations of labor and capital are eliminated, the aggregate output in China will increase by 11%–33%. Han et al. (2018) perform a similar exercise and find that the estimated efficiency gains from reallocating labor and capital are between 26% and 151%. According to Adamopoulos et al. (2022), the reallocation of land and capital can result in a 53.2% increase in aggregate productivity.

These results imply that, given limited resource endowments, reallocation can increase agricultural output. However, based on the literature, the estimated gains in aggregate output and productivity are not as high as expected. In this study, the efficiency gains did not exceed 60%.

## 5. Empirical application

## 5.1 Variables and descriptive statistics

Based on previous studies (Han et al., 2018; Zhu et al., 2011), the household distortion index D is set as the dependent variable in this statistical analysis and is calculated as an absolute value. Larger values of these variables indicate more severe misallocation of the corresponding factor. We included three groups of variables for resource distortion: AMS application, factor market characteristics and household and farmland characteristics.

The AMS is the main independent variable in this study. By applying third-party outsourcing services, the relative prices of machinery and labor are altered, which, in turn, affects the combination of the rural labor force and other factors (Huan et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2016). However, these effects have not yet received sufficient attention.

The ratio of the non-agricultural employment labor force to the total labor force in a village represents labor market distortion, indicating the availability of non-agricultural employment opportunities. Village income is used as a proxy for capital markets, reflecting capital flowability. Cultivated land size, land terrain, subsidy, distance from homestead to road, proportion of the labor force, value of household assets per capita, householders' educational attainment, whether a family member was the village leader and a dummy variable for provinces were employed as control variables in the regression analysis. previous studies, the Pearson correlation coefficient between the instrument and resource misallocation is insignificant. This indicates that the instrument is exogenous (Leng et al., 2020).

The significantly negative coefficient of AMS application in the outcome equation suggests that AMS participation improves resource allocation. This indicates that the adoption of AMS mitigates labor and capital constraints and, therefore, reduces resource misallocation. Specifically, AMS usage in agriculture enables farmers to alleviate labor constraints by replacing high-intensity manual labor with machinery while also helping them save on the capital required to purchase their own machinery.

Regarding the other control variables, the village non-farm proportion is positively related to distortion, which is consistent with the findings of Zhu et al. (2011). This may be because greater non-farm employment opportunities alter the household labor structure, increasing the share of elderly laborers in farming and thereby leading to higher levels of resource misallocation.

The village's total income significantly reduces factor distortions, suggesting that a better financial environment improves farmers' resource allocation. This aligns with Banerjee and Munshi (2004), who find that capital distortion is mainly caused by financial constraints

To further study the impact of the AMS, the ATE is estimated using a counterfactual framework, as shown in Table 6. The ATE of AMS on household resource allocation and the ATE on the treated (ATT) are –0.027 and –0.029, respectively, and both are statistically significant at the 10% level.

## 5.4 Robustness check

To check the robustness of our main findings, we use the share of service fees in the total cost as a proxy for AMS. Because the share of service fees is a continuous variable, a TEM is not suitable. Therefore, we estimate the regression equations using two-stage least squares (2SLS). Table 7 presents the results [4]. Our findings reveal that the application of an AMS reduces resource misallocation. These results are consistent with our main findings.

## 6. Conclusion and policy implications

The efficient allocation of resources in agriculture is widely regarded to be an effective approach for helping farmers achieve high productivity. Previous studies have examined the effects of resource misallocation but have paid less attention to the role of machinery outsourcing services in the context of a decreasing rural labor era. This study introduces a theoretical framework to estimate the resource (mis)allocation index (household distortion index) and aggregate TFP gains using rice farmers' outsourced services as examples to analyze the effects of outsourced machinery services on resource (mis)allocation in China.

Several findings emerge from the analysis. First, resources are misallocated across the surveyed rice-producing households. Second, the application of AMS improves the allocation efficiency of household farms through mechanized labor substitution and the introduction of advanced technologies. Third, AMS significantly alleviates resource misallocation.

Based on these findings, several policy directions have emerged that strengthen the role of AMS in improving resource allocation and productivity in China's rice sector. As rural labor continues to decline, AMS can act as an institutional substitute by reducing misallocation and diffusing advanced technologies, thereby enhancing household efficiency. Policies should, therefore, focus on supporting the development of specialized service providers through fiscal and financial incentives, as well as by promoting service diversification and extending coverage to smallholders facing greater constraints. Concurrently, upgrading to more advanced machinery and integrating technical guidance into service provision can accelerate the diffusion of modern farming practices, ensuring that the benefits of mechanization extend beyond mere labor substitution. Institutional reforms in land and service markets are equally crucial, as they can reduce transaction frictions and allow AMS to operate effectively in reallocating resources across households. Moreover, tailoring AMS development to regional conditions, particularly in areas with fragmented landholdings or labor shortages, while building cross-regional service networks, will help balance seasonal and spatial disparities in demand. Finally, embedding AMS into a broader food security strategy, combined with continuous monitoring of efficiency gains and inclusivity, can ensure that these services evolve into a sustainable mechanism for raising rice yields and safeguarding national grain security.
