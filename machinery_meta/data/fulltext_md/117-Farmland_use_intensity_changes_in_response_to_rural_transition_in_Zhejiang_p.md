## ABSTRACT

Dynamic changes of farmland use intensity (FUI) have an important impact on the sustainable agricultural intensification in developing countries. This study characterizes the temporal changes of FUI in response to rural transition during 1990–2015 in Zhejiang province, China. In particular, the FUI is measured by using indicators that are integrated by three weight determination methods, including analytic hierarchy process, Delphi method and entropy method. The rural transition is described from three aspects, namely regional urbanization, agricultural industrialization and agricultural commercialization. The impacts of rural transition on the farmland use intensity are quantified by multivariate linear regression. Results show that urban population proportion (UPP) and supporting capability of agricultural production (SCAP) have the positive effect on input intensity (IPI). UPP, development of secondary and tertiary industries (DSTI) and urban-rural income gap (URIG) present positive relationships with output intensity (OPI), while grain-sown area proportion (GSAP) and proportion of cash crop yield (PCCY) present negative relationships with OPI. Agricultural science and technology service (ASTS) and GSAP are positive contributors to utilization degree (ULD), while Agricultural mechanization (AGM) is a negative contributor to ULD. Redundancy analysis shows that the individual influence of regional urbanization is higher than that of agricultural industrialization and agricultural commercialization. Meanwhile the joint influences of regional urbanization, agricultural industrialization and agricultural commercialization are the strongest. Based on the results, the policies should give specific guidance to the rural transition for achieving sustainable agricultural intensification.

## 1. Introduction

Agricultural intensification has been widely spreading in developing countries during the past several decades (Bonny, 2011; Xie et al., 2016; Siebert et al., 2010). It aims to maximize the yields from limited farmland resources using modest input to feed the growing population. Agricultural inputs cannot be significantly reduced in China, given the continuous farmland abandonment in urbanization process (You, 2017; Zhong et al., 2016). China has become the world's largest user of agricultural chemicals (Huang et al., 2002). It consumes over 30% of global fertilizers and pesticides on only 9% of global farmland (Wu et al., 2018). Although agricultural intensification helps to guarantee the national food security (Zhong et al., 2017), it introduces various effects and threatens on biodiversity, ecosystem services, and public health (You and Zhang, 2016; Tsiafouli et al., 2015). For example, agricultural pollution worsens in accordance with the increasing pollution-related agricultural input intensity in Hubei province, China (You, 2016b). Meanwhile the excessive use of chemical fertilizer has become a major cause of agricultural non-point source pollution in China (Lu and Xie, 2018). Besides, agricultural intensification has a negative impact on health risks in Southeast Asia since occupational exposure to pesticides and consumption of pesticide-contaminated food (Lam et al., 2018). Therefore Chinese government tries to control the pollution-related agricultural input intensity associated with agricultural intensification. In particular, sustainable intensification is particularly advocated that maximizes the yields while minimizes the environmental pressure (Struik and Kuyper, 2017; Xie et al., 2018a). Besides, the farmers' enthusiasm for farming engagement in developed areas reduces since off-farm employment would bring higher income (Li and Wang, 2003). It is one of the potential threats to national food security because the farmers tend to reduce the agricultural inputs which may decrease the agricultural production yields. During a long period, Chinese government forbids farmers to leave land uncultivated (Xie et al., 2014). Article 37 of Land administration law of the People's

Republic of China stipulates that all units and individuals are forbidden to leave farmland uncultivated. From the above, with the development of social economy in China, farmland use intensity which reflects the status of agricultural intensification has constantly changed.

Given the complexity in nature, there is no consistent definition on farmland use intensity (Erb et al., 2013). Traditionally, the definition of farmland use intensity are based on the relationships between inputs and outputs in the process of farmland use. Brookfield (1972) described farming intensification as the substitution of agricultural inputs in terms of labor, capital or technology for farmland. Thus, farmers have to find a pathway for obtaining higher long-term production using limited farmland resources. Shriar (2000) defined the agricultural intensification as a process of raising farmland productivity by increasing the inputs on a per unit area basis. Meanwhile, some definition highlight that any kind of human interaction with agriculture in farming. Dietrich et al. (2012) defines farmland use intensity as the degree of yield amplification caused by human activities.

According to the definitions of farmland use intensity, a multidimensional framework is a proposed method to evaluate the farmland use intensity. In general, input intensity (IPI) and output intensity (OPI) were considered as two key dimensions for assessing farmland use intensity (Kühling et al., 2016; Zhang and Li, 2016) since the farmland use intensity reflects the relationships between inputs and outputs in the process of farmland use. Meanwhile, the definition of farmland use intensity implies the human interaction with farming. In the previous researches, irrigation index, cropping frequency and reclaimed farmland which affect agricultural productivity are also considered as the typical indicators for revealing the features of other farming activities (Xie and Liu, 2015; Chadha and Sharma, 1982; Li et al., 2013; Boserup, 1965). These indicators can be classified as the dimension that describes the utilization degree (ULD) for analyzing farmland use intensity. In the empirical studies, independent indicators that are composed of dimensions of farmland use intensity can describe farmland use intensity respectively (Li and Wang, 2003). Consequently, the indicators are classified into three dimensions that includes IPI, OPI and ULD in this study.

Previous studies have quantified the farmland use intensity. Comprehensive assessment is usually applied in assessing farmland use intensity in previous studies (Shriar, 2000; Zhang et al., 2012). It emphasizes the synthesis in one dimension of farmland use intensity since interactions exist among the independent indicators. One representative interaction is the replacement between agricultural inputs, such as the replacement between capital input and labor input. Traditionally, two basic forms including physical value form and economic value form of inputs and outputs are adopted in assessing farmland use intensity (Chen et al., 2009; Xie et al., 2016). The physical value form is selected in this study since the prices of inputs and outputs in agricultural production that should be determined in the economic value form have remarkable disparities in a longer time span. The weights of indicators play a key role in comprehensive assessment. The weight determination methods include subjective methods and objective methods (Weng et al., 2017). The subjective methods include analytic hierarchy process (AHP) (Park and Han, 2002), Delphi method (Dalkey and Helmer, 1963), etc. Objective methods include entropy method (Pan et al., 2015), principal component analysis (Maggino and Ruviglioni, 2009), Ma's objective weights (Ma et al., 1999), etc. Subjective methods of weight determination are based on expert evaluation, but sometimes incorporate subjectivity and uncertainty. Objective methods of weight determination are based on mathematical models, but the objective weights omit the different importance among indicators that cannot provide by mathematical model. In order to improve the reliability in the process of weight determination, subjective methods and objective methods are simultaneously employed in this study. And the consistency among these methods are tested. Above all, the comprehensive assessment is selected to assess farmland use intensity from three dimensions that includes IPI, OPI and ULD by using multiple methods for weight determination.

Many studies have used numerical models to examine the influencing factors of the dynamic farmland use intensity. The population pressure and land scarcity stimulates the long-term process of land use intensification (Ewert et al., 2006). Meanwhile, the land property right also has potential impact on farmland use intensity. Quisumbing et al. (2001) found that enhanced tenure security could increase long-term investments, and land use intensification took place simultaneously. Besides, household characteristics, such as family size, family labor, family income, education and age, influence the farming behavior, and thus relates with farmland use intensity (Hao et al., 2015). Previous studies have investigated the effects of economic transition on farmland use intensity. Urbanization has resulted in significant changes in demographic structure, which has a complex impact on farmland use intensity. You (2016b) found that pesticide use intensity continued to rise following shocks from the urban population proportion in urbanization, and responses of chemical fertilizer intensity first decrease and then increase subjected to the shocks from the urban population proportion. Li et al. (2013) revealed that urban expansion driven by population urbanization decreased the agricultural land use intensity. Socioeconomic transition in rural areas is often referred to as rural transition (Long et al., 2009). Long et al. (2011) described rural transition as changes in economic and social conditions resulting from industrialization and urbanization. The regional disparity caused by rural transition has close relations with the changes of farmland use intensity and agricultural productivity. (Chen et al., 2009; Ali, 2007; Long et al., 2009). However. the effects of multidimensional variables that exhibits the features of urbanization, industrialization and other transformation of agricultural production on the farmland use intensity haven't caused extensive concern in previous studies. Meanwhile, few researches have studied the joint influences of urbanization, industrialization and other transformation of agricultural production on the farmland use intensity. Therefore urbanization, industrialization and other transformation of agricultural production which are happening at once in rural transition should be taken as a whole in the analysis of the effects on the farmland use intensity. This study attempts to select multidimensional indicators to describe rural transition, and analyze the effects of rural transition on the farmland use intensity.

Farmland use intensity changes in response to rural transition can reveal the effects of socioeconomic factors on the agricultural intensification. It shows that not only individual peasant household characteristics but also socioeconomic factors have impacts on the farmers' farming behavior. Using a case of Zhejiang province in China, our specific objectives are to (1) quantify the farmland use intensity from three dimensions (i.e., IPI, OPI and ULD); (2) assess the relationships between rural transition indicators and metrics of farmland use intensity; (3) analyze the individual influences and joint influences of the components of rural transition on the farmland use intensity; and (4) discuss the policy implications.

## 2.1. Study area

Zhejiang province, bordered with Shanghai, Jiangsu, Anhui, Jiangxi and Fujian, is located in eastern coastal China (Fig. 1). It is one of the most developed regions in China since reform and opening-up. Zhejiang province has a humid subtropical climate with cool winters and long, hot summers. Benefited from the humid subtropical climate, growing season for crops is long in Zhejiang province. Plains and basins comprise about roughly one quarter of the total area, with fertile alluvial soil. The intensive farmland use is widely promoted to yield more agricultural productions given the high population density in Zhejiang province. Meanwhile, the rapid economic growth accelerates rural transition in Zhejiang province. ## 2.2. Developing indicators of rural transition

Regional urbanization, agricultural industrialization and agricultural commercialization are considered as three key dimensions to describe the rural transition in previous studies (Long et al., 2011; Chen et al., 2009; Ali, 2007) In reference of this framework, 15 indicators are selected to characterize the rural transition in Zhejiang province based on literature review. Then expert panel evaluation is applied to estimate the fitness of these indicators (Maes et al., 2011). Five researchers who are expert in these areas of land resources management or rural development positively responded to the invitation and constitute the expert panel for this research. The 5 experts judge these indicators from four aspects: scale appropriateness, scientific validation, suitability, and discriminating (Qiu et al., 2015; Weng et al., 2017). The fitness of indicators are classified into an ordinal scale from one (very unfit) to four (very fit): one (very unfit), two (unfit), three (fit), and four (very fit). Besides, the experts can add comments on each indicator. The judging process and judging standards of expert panel evaluation are expressed as follows: (1) the poorly performing indicators that are assigned a very low evaluation (< 2 for all judging criteria) from at least one expert are discarded. (2) The indicators that receives severe comments about their shortcomings are filtered out. (3) Four aspects of judging criteria are assigned same weights, and the average fitness scores of these indicators are calculated. (4) Three indicators per dimension of rural transition that receive highest scores are kept, unless these indicators are very similar. Based on the experts' judgment, the final 9 indicators for rural transition are finally kept (Table 1).

The main reasons for selecting these indicators are briefly described as follows. First, the regional urbanization reflects a process population migration from rural areas to urban areas (Su et al.;, 2014; You, 2016a). As urban populations grow, it has an important impact on secondary and tertiary industries (You, 2016b). Meanwhile, urbanization creates enormous impacts on the urban-rural imbalance of economy since the allocation of resources between rural areas to urban areas has been changed. Therefore three variables are selected to describe regional urbanization, including urban population proportion (UPP), development of secondary and tertiary industries (DSTI) and urban-rural income gap (URIG). Agricultural industrialization refers to a continuing process of mechanization (Kishida, 2017). In additional, one important feature of agricultural industrialization is that socialized services and cooperation, such as agricultural science and technology service, are provided in the different stages of the agricultural production. The agricultural science and technology service plays a key role in traditional agriculture transformation. Agricultural mechanization (AGM) and agricultural science and technology service (ASTS) are therefore chosen. Agricultural industrialization can increase the responsiveness of agricultural production to consumer demand. Thus, supporting capability of agricultural production (SCAP) is selected to describe agricultural industrialization. Third, the agricultural commercialization stimulates a market orientation of agricultural production. Zhejiang province is not major grain producing area in China. Meanwhile, the one important purpose of farmers' grain production is to supply farmers' grain ration by themselves in Zhejiang province. So the grainsown area proportion (GSAP) has a negative relationship with agricultural commercialization. Besides, the cash crop production and grain commercialization imply the farmers cultivate commercial crops to sell their agricultural outputs in the markets (Su et al., 2017). Therefore three variables are selected to describe agricultural commercialization, including grain-sown area proportion (GSAP), proportion of cash crop yield (PCCY) and grain commercialization rate (GCR).

## 2.3. Assessing farmland use intensity

The farmland use intensity is described from three dimensions including input intensity (IPI), output intensity (OPI) and utilization degree (ULD) (Erb et al., 2013; Li et al., 2013; Zhang and Li, 2016) (Table 2). Farmland use involves various types of agricultural input (Zhong et al., 2018). The intensive farmland use is a process of increasing agricultural inputs per unit of farmland including labor, agricultural machinery, chemical fertilizer and pesticide (Chen et al., 2009). Therefore IPI is classified into four representative parts: agricultural machinery use intensity, labor intensity, chemical fertilizer use intensity and pesticide use intensity. The agricultural productivity has tight relationship with input intensity. The farmland use intensity also can be described as the degree of yield (Dietrich et al., 2012). When the focus of farmland use intensity is on the output intensity, the output intensity can be measured in per unit of output. Per area, per capita and time unit are considered as representative per unit for analyzing output intensity (Turner and Doolittle, 1978). Output intensity implies the output capacity of one unit of input, such as farmland and persons employed in agricultural production (Liu et al., 2016). Meanwhile, the outputs can be classified into grain yield and crop yield (Chen et al., 2009; Levers et al., 2016). So per capita output of crop production that reflects the output capacity of one person employed in agricultural production in the process of farmland use is selected in this study. And OPI that covers per hectare yield of grain, per capita output of crop production and per hectare output of crop production is constructed. Another dimension of farmland use intensity is cropping frequency for a constant unit of land and time period and reclaimed farmland in one study area (Chadha and Sharma, 1982; Boserup, 1965; Xie et al., 2018b). The ULD is selected to reveal the features of farmland use conditions, cropping frequency and reclaimed farmland area for farmland use intensity. Irrigation helps to grow agricultural crops. Farmland which is rain-fed leads to low agricultural production performance. This includes irregular production and low intensification. Consequently, irrigation index is selected to describe ULD. And the variables for describing ULD is composed of the irrigation index, multiple cropping index and land reclamation rate.

The weights of indicators play a key role in assessing values of farmland intensity. Although there are many methods can be applied to determine weight, the generally accepted method for one specific study is hardly selected. In order to improve the weight determination process, we employed two subjective methods and one objective method to test their consistency. The subjective methods include AHP (Park and

Han, 2002) and Delphi method (Dalkey and Helmer, 1963). The principle steps to define weights by AHP can be expressed as follows: (1) The pairwise comparison matrix is completed by evaluating the relative importance between two indicators. (2) A normalized comparison matrix is created, and each value in the normalized matrix is divided by the sum of its column. (3) The weights that are average values of each row of this new matrix is computed. (4) The reliable estimate is did by assessing consistency index. In this study, five researchers who are expert in these areas of farmland use and agricultural production are selected to create three pairwise comparison matrices for IPI, OPI and ULD. The 1-9 linear scale are selected to assess the values of the relative importance between indicators. The values of the relative importance in the matrices for evaluating the weights are the average values of the original values that are assigned by five researchers. Delphi method is based on the agreement in the judgment from the independent analysis of experts. The principle steps to define weights by Delphi method can be expressed as follows: (1) A group of experts based on the topic are selected. (2)The initial weights are assessed based on their personal opinion. (3) The initial weights are returned to each experts, and the experts revised the weights. (4) At the end of each feedback, all weights are returned to the person who decides if another round is necessary or if the results can be accepted. In this study, the weight is the average values of the consistent weights that five experts assign to each indicator.

## 2.4. Statistical analysis

Multivariate linear regression is conducted to analyze the relationship between intensive farmland use changes and rural transition. In particular, the variables for assessing farmland use intensity are dependent variables, and rural transition indicators are independent variables. All the variables are entered and the stepwise regression procedure is selected to solve the multicollinearity problem. The mathematical formula for multivariate linear regression is expressed as

Where Yt is the t-th variable of farmland use intensity, Xr is the r-th variable of rural transition,βr is the parameter of r-th variable of rural transition in assessing the t-th variable of farmland use intensity, ε is a random error. Stepwise regression procedure that is selected in multivariate linear regression includes forward selection, backward elimination and bidirectional elimination.

In order to evaluate the relative influence of the three components of rural transition (regional urbanization, agricultural industrialization and agricultural commercialization), the redundancy analysis (RDA) is adopted to decompose the explained variances into several fractions (Anderson and Cribble, 2010; You, 2016c; Heikkinen et al., 2005; Wan and Su, 2016). The RAD is performed by using the program CANOCO (version 4.5) (ter Braak and Smilauer, 2002). The values of sum of all canonical eigenvalues in the each step for analyzing the partial regression between three components of rural transition and farmland use intensity in RAD are assessed. The proportion between values of all canonical eigenvalues and the total variation represents the relative influence of the three components of rural transition on farmland use intensity. Specifically, the variation which is indicated by total variation in RAD is partitioned into different fractions: (1) individual influence is determined by the one component of rural transition, (2) joint influences are determined by the two components of rural transition, (3) joint influences are determined by the three components of rural transition, and (4) unexplained total variations.

## 3. Results

## 3.1. Farmland use intensity changes in Zhejiang province

AHP, Delphi method and entropy method are adopted to assess weights that applied in assessing farmland use intensity. The values of consistency index pairwise comparison matrices are lower than 0.1 in AHP method, therefore these matrices are accepted for assessing weights due to their consistency. Meanwhile, the critical rule that decides the results can be accepted in Delphi method is the absolute values of differences among the weight values that five experts assign to one indicator are lower than 0.05. Results reveal that the weights assigned to the variables are varying with the three weight determination methods (Table 4). In order to test the rationality of weight calculation among three methods, the consistency test is did. The consistency test (Fig. 2) shows that all the coefficient of determination (R²) exceeds 0.700. Meanwhile the R² between AHP and Delphi method in assessing IPI, OPI and ULD exceeds 0.990. Besides the R² of three methods in assessing OPI are also greater than 0.990. Although the consistency test reflects the differences among three methods, the high consistency denotes that AHP, Delphi method and entropy method would not result in significantly different results of assessing farmland use intensity. In order to integrate the results of three weight determination methods, the arithmetic means of weights that are calculated by three weight determination methods are selected to assess farmland use intensity in this study. ## 3.2. Rural transition process of Zhejiang province

Temporal changes of rural transition indicators in Zhejiang province from 1990 to 2015 are shown in Fig. 4. For indicators of regional urbanization, UPP presents an increasing linear trend, and DSTI demonstrates an increasing cubic trend between 1990 and 2015 in Zhejiang province. Meanwhile URIG decreases firstly and then increases during 1990 and 2015. It suggests that the regional urbanization that is described from urban population and gross product of secondary and tertiary industries has accelerated since 1990. But the rapid urbanization has enlarged the income gap between the residents in rural areas and residents in urban areas for a long time. The increasing trend of the income gap has been transformed since the beginning of 21st century. For indicators of agricultural industrialization, AGM, ASTS and SCAP have various characteristics of temporal changes in Zhejiang province. AGM increases first, decreases in between and then increases again. ASTS presents a decreasing trend, and it implies that the agricultural industrialization is improved since ASTS is a negative index. And SCAP has an increasing trend until it bended downward. It signifies that reducing labor input by advancing agricultural mechanization plays the key role in the agricultural industrialization in Zhejiang province. Agricultural technology extension has been enhanced dramatically since the beginning of the 21st century. But it cannot significantly improve the agricultural production efficiency in the agricultural industrialization. The food production cannot support more residents in urban areas in the early 2010s. For indicators of agricultural commercialization, GSAP first has a downward trend and then increases after the bend point. PCCY and GCR have a negative trend. They first have an upward trend and then decreases after the bend point. It implies that temporal changes of GSAP, PCCY and GCR exhibit similar temporal trend of agricultural commercialization. More agricultural crops have been for sale in the market, rather than for family consumption in the agricultural commercialization since 1990. And the ratio of grain-sown area to crop-sown area has decreased. But the opposite trend which denotes decreasing agricultural commercialization appeared in the early 2010s.

## 3.3. Temporal changes of farmland use intensity in association with rural transition

Table 5 presents the relationships between rural transition and farmland use intensity. The regression models are suitable for predicting the response of farmland use intensity changes in response to rural transition, since over 95% of adjusted R² has been explained. Between 1990 and 2015, UPP and SCAP have the positive effect on IPI. The results denote that decreasing rural population and improving supporting capability of agricultural production can increase the agricultural inputs in Zhejiang province. From 1990 to 2015, UPP, DSTI and URIG present positive relationships with OPI, while GSAP and PCCY present negative relationships with OPI. It suggests that regional urbanization can increase the agricultural outputs that include per hectare yield of grain, per capita output of crop production and per hectare output of crop production. Meanwhile GSAP is a negative index, therefore the variables that describe agricultural commercialization have multiple effects on the OPI. The increasing coefficient for GSAP indicates that the decreasing agricultural commercialization can reduce OPI, while the increasing coefficient for PCCY denotes that the increasing agricultural commercialization can raise OPI. ASTS and GSAP are positive contributors to ULD, while AGM is a negative contributor to ULD. It implies that agricultural industrialization has negative influences on ULD. And the agricultural commercialization also has a negative influence on ULD. Besides, the influencing factors of rural transition on the IPI are entirely different from the influencing factors on the ULD. But the influencing factors of rural transition on the OPI are identical to the influencing factors on the IPI and ULD in some respects.

The redundancy analysis (RDA) is adopted to decompose the relative influences of different components of rural transition on farmland use intensity (Fig. 5). The results reveal that the individual influence of regional urbanization is similar to that of agricultural commercialization. And these influences are higher than that of agricultural industrialization. Meanwhile, the joint influences of regional urbanization and agricultural commercialization which explain the 17.6% total variation play the most important role in the joint influences of two components on the farmland use intensity changes. Besides, the joint influences of regional urbanization, agricultural industrialization and agricultural commercialization are the strongest. It explains the 56.9% total variation. These results imply that the evolution of farmland use intensity should be more subjected to regional urbanization and agricultural commercialization.

## 4. Discussion and policy implications

## 4.1. Characteristics of farmland use intensity changes

The farmland use intensity changes have various characteristics in Zhejiang province. The agricultural inputs, chemical fertilizer and pesticide in particular, have increased rapidly since 1980s in China (Wu, 2011; Su et al., 2011). It quickly raises the input intensity in farmland use (Chen et al., 2009). But the increasing trend of IPI is changed in early 2010s. Although farmers in Zhejiang province which is a developed province in China are able to bear the higher cost of agricultural inputs, IPI do not keep the increasing trend in the early 2010s. The decreasing trend of IPI in Zhejiang province has emerged in the past more than ten years. One important reason for this phenomenon is that the rural transition in Zhejiang province is recognized as one of the most drastic rural transition in the whole China. In contrast to IPI, OPI has kept a steady increase in Zhejiang province since 1990. The increasing output intensity denotes that economic benefits are the main objective in agricultural production in Zhejiang province. Zhejiang province is a traditional agriculture region with high-intensive farmland use. But the ULD has a decreasing trend between 1990 and 2000. It reveals that the intensive farmland use in Zhejiang province do not pay more attention to increasing cropping frequency and reclaimed farmland area in the process of rapid economic growth in rural areas. In generally, the change trends have an inverse relationship between IPI and ULD. These characteristics of farmland use intensity changes are corresponding to the entirely different influencing factors of rural transition among IPI, ULD. But the OPI has a steady change trend. It implies that increasing OPI is not achieved by simultaneously increasing the IPI and ULD in Zhejiang province. More varied influencing factors of rural transition affect these change trends of OPI.

## 4.2. Hidden causes of rural transition on farmland use intensity

The rural transition in Zhejiang province plays a key role in the temporal trend of farmland use intensity. The multivariable linear regressions could provide some important quantitative insights, regarding farmland use intensity changes in response to rural transition in Zhejiang province. There are hidden causes of rural transition on farmland use intensity. Regional urbanization is typically featured by decreasing rural population. Replacement of labor input with capital input is an inevitable process in the urbanization since the labor transfer from agriculture industry to secondary and tertiary industries to gain higher incomes (Swinnen et al., 2015; Christiansen, 2009). Therefore the inputs in farmland use, such as agricultural machinery, chemical fertilizer and pesticide, increase dramatically during regional urbanization. The impact of regional urbanization on the farmland use intensity in Zhejiang province is in line with a conventional wisdom in the study of Shi et al. (2011), which describes this conventional wisdom that off-farm employment contributes to environmental pollution by increasing the use of agro-chemicals. Such a finding is also consistent with the findings of Wu and Meng (1997), which the relocation of farm household labour from on-farm raise capital investment in grain production as a substitute for labour based on a survey in five China's provinces. The agricultural industrialization also raises the IPI by improving supporting capability of agricultural production in Zhejiang province. Increasing supporting capability of agricultural production aims to increase agricultural production to meet national food security needs (Babu et al., 2015). But increasing IPI to achieve national food security needs cannot be transformed completely in the process of improving supporting capability of agricultural production. Increasing agricultural productivity cannot completely realized through high-yield crop management and raising nutrient-use efficiency in Zhejiang.

The regional urbanization has a positive effect on the OPI. The positive relationship between UPP and OPI is consistent with the study of Huy and Nonneman (2016), which finds that rural to urban migration enhances household agricultural output in the Mekong River Delta Region of Vietnam. Meanwhile the OPI can also obtain benefits from rapid development of secondary and tertiary industries in urbanization since it can provide high-yield input and promotes a brisk market. Terrain has an important effect on the agricultural mechanization which is an important transition of agricultural production in the agricultural industrialization. It can explain the negative relationship between AGM and ULD in Zhejiang province. Hills areas are adverse to promoting agricultural mechanization (Vatsa and Saraswat, 2003). Consequently, IPI and OPI are tend to raise to increase agricultural output. It implies an important rule that the interregional population migration and interregional agricultural production trade have impact on the relationship between rural transition and farmland use intensity.

## 4.3. Policy implications

The growth of pollution-related agricultural input intensity has contributed to the increasingly serious non-point pollution in China (You, 2016b). The Chinese government tries to control increasing pollution-related agricultural inputs by adopting an action of zero growth in the use of chemical fertilizers and pesticides. The results reveal that the increasing process of agricultural input intensity stopped in the early 2010s in Zhejiang province. Therefore the farmland use cannot choose increasing chemical fertilizer use intensity and pesticide use intensity to achieve high yield. One ideal path for improving agricultural input intensity is to raise the agricultural machinery use intensity. Apparently, decreasing agricultural output intensity worsens the farmers' livelihoods, and the agricultural production cannot keep sustainable development. So the farmland use should focus on raising the use efficiency of agricultural input to increase output intensity. The arrangement of utilization degree should pay more attention to ecological protection for the farmland. Consequently, the agricultural infrastructure that can benefits the farmland use should keep a moderate increase in investment, and land reclamation cannot occupy the ecological land. Meanwhile, the farmland should be laid fallow for some time (Xie et al., 2018c).

The three dimensions of rural transition have various effects on the farmland use intensity. Obviously, the process of rural transition will continue. In order to achieve suitable farmland use intensity for sustainable intensive farmland use, we should give suitable guidance to the rural transition (Struik and Kuyper, 2017; Xie and Lu, 2017). The regional urbanization has positive impacts on the IPI and OPI. Consequently, we should optimize the urbanization forms to balance the agricultural input intensity and agricultural output intensity. The key point is to cultivate skilled farmers in rural areas when the labor transfer from agriculture industry to secondary and tertiary industries during the process of the population shifting from rural to urban areas. The skilled farmers are willing to increase the investment on the agricultural infrastructures. And they can adopt new agricultural technologies that decrease use of fertilizer and pesticides. Besides, the urbanrural income gap should be narrowed. One important measure is to raise the price of agricultural products, and control the growth of agricultural input prices. The agricultural industrialization should be accelerated. The agricultural technology extension in agricultural industrialization should pay more attention to improving the nutrient-use efficiency and minimizing the negative ecological impact in the process of raising the agricultural outputs (Lu and Xie, 2018). Besides, in order to promote the agricultural mechanization in agricultural industrialization, the fragmentation of the farmland should be reduced. The ULD cannot be raised by transferring the ecological land, such as forest, grass and wetland, to farmland. The mix of decreasing grainsown area proportion and growing proportion of cash crop yield can accelerate agricultural commercialization. But it has a conflict in increasing OPI. Considering national food security in China, the grainsown area proportion should be kept stable.

## 5. Conclusion

This study characterizes the relationships between farmland use intensity and rural transition in Zhejiang province, China. In particular, an integrated index is developed to assess farmland use intensity from three dimensions that includes IPI, OPI and ULD. The weights for indicators are the arithmetic means of weights that are calculated by AHP, Delphi method and entropy method. Regional urbanization, agricultural industrialization and agricultural commercialization are considered as the representative features of rural transition. The impacts of rural transition on the farmland use intensity are quantified by multivariate linear regression and RDA.

The findings show that UPP and SCAP are positive contributors to IPI. UPP, DSTI and URIG present positive relationships with OPI, while GSAP and PCCY present negative relationships with OPI. ASTS and GSAP have positive effects on ULD, while AGM has negative effects on ULD. Meanwhile the joint influences of regional urbanization, agricultural industrialization and agricultural commercialization are the strongest. This study suggests that rural transition has a tight link with farmland use intensity changes.
