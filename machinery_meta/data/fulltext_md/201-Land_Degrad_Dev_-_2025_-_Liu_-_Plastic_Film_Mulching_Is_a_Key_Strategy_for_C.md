## ABSTRACT

The connections between agricultural tech, policies, land use change, and development in the Loess Plateau remain unclear. Understanding these is vital for formulating sustainable strategies. This study attempts to quantify the contribution of plastic film mulching to yield increase from the perspective of land-use change, and to quantify the positive impacts of plastic film mulching from the perspective of agricultural green development. The Random Forest algorithm was used to analyze the correlations between crop yields and relevant indicators, based on data from 1980 to 2020 in the Loess Plateau. From 1980 to 2020, cultivated land steadily declined at an annual rate of 2.5%. Meanwhile, the grain self-sufficiency rate rose from 62% to 110%. Maize per-unit yield climbed from 2.55–3.54tha−¹ in 1980 to 5.19–7.56tha−¹ in 2020, and wheat from 1.19–1.73 to 3.35–4.58tha−¹. The total power of agricultural machinery showed a substantial increase of 54.63 × 10⁶ kW from 1980 to 2020, while the consumption of agricultural plastic film rose by 21.89 × 104 tons during the period from 1991 to 2020. Plastic film mulching (17.3%), mechanization (14.7%), and irrigation (16.5%) were key yield boosters. Ecological factors were dominant in green development (3+ positive indicators/10 variables). This study reveals the complex relationships among ecological, social, economic, and technological factors in agriculture. It provides valuable guidance for formulating sustainable agri-policies in the Loess Plateau and similar regions.

## 1 | Introduction

On a global scale, with the increasing severity of population growth, resource scarcity, and environmental issues, the transformation toward green agricultural development has emerged as a crucial pathway for ensuring food security and achieving sustainable development (Zhang, Feng, et al. 2024; Zhang, Yong, et al. 2024; Zhang, Zhou, et al. 2024; Lee, Zeng, et al. 2024). Agricultural green development (AGD) is a key pathway to harmonize ecological conservation with socioeconomic development, especially in the context of the United Nations Sustainable

Development Goals (SDGs) and China's green development strategy. While traditional agriculture faces the challenges of high resource consumption and environmental degradation AGD aims to transform this paradigm into a system characterized by high productivity, efficient use of resources, and low environmental impacts, while enhancing food quality and human well-being (Shen et al. 2020, 2024; Zhang, Feng, et al. 2024; Zhang, Yong, et al. 2024; Zhang, Zhou, et al. 2024). In the realm of agricultural production, land stands as an essential resource. It is a pivotal element for safeguarding ecological and food security across nations and serves as the material foundation upon which human existence rests (Grafton et al. 2015; Akana et al. 2023; Lee, He, et al. 2024). Land-use change, representing the most direct and prominent manifestation of the influence exerted by human activities on the Earth's surface system, assumes a critically important role in the context of global environmental change (Liu et al. 2018, 2014). It not only mirrors the intensity and modalities of human socioeconomic activities but also has far-reaching implications for the earth's natural ecosystems, climate regime, and biological diversity.

Dryland regions are generally plagued by issues such as low crop productivity, meager farmers' incomes, and severe environmental degradation. Moreover, they cover 47% of the global land area (Li et al. 2021). Their significance and the predicaments they face are self-vident (Lal 2009; Reynolds et al. 2007). As a region of significant ecological vulnerability in China, the Loess Plateau occupies a geographically distinctive position (Shen et al. 2023). Its ecological environment is characterized by extreme fragility, manifested in severe soil erosion, and a concomitant decline in soil fertility (Zhao et al. 2019; Cheng et al. 2023; Zhang et al. 2022, 2025). In recent years, the Loess Plateau region has achieved certain accomplishments in agricultural production (Li, Chen, et al. 2024; Li, Kou, et al. 2024). However, it simultaneously confronts numerous challenges. Issues such as resource waste and environmental pollution, which are caused by traditional agricultural production methods, severely impede the sustainable development of agriculture. Consequently, the transformation toward green agricultural development in the Loess Plateau region is of utmost urgency (Qu et al. 2022, 2024). This not only contributes to the improvement of the local ecological environment but also facilitates the enhancement of agricultural efficiency and an increase in farmers' incomes.

The shift in land use, known as land-use transformation, occurs when regional land use changes its form. This is driven by economic and social transformations and novel ideas, as indicated by Grainger (1995) and Long et al. (2010). It represents a transition in the utilization of land areas, influenced by evolving economic and social conditions and new concepts. Amidst this series of complex and subtle changes, land-use change, as one of the crucial factors characterizing the response of human activities to global change, stands out prominently (Feng et al. 2024). It not only reflects the dynamic changes in human socioeconomic activities but also serves as an important indicator for us to understand and assess global climate change (Howells et al. 2013; Yang et al. 2024). Since 2005, the Global Land Project (GLP) has officially established land-use change as one of the core topics in global change research (GLP 2005). This initiative has highlighted the pivotal role of land-use change in global environmental dynamics, compelling both the academic community and policymakers to direct more attention toward the monitoring and research in this field.

The application of plastic film mulching in dryland crop production has attracted increasing attention in recent years. Numerous studies have demonstrated that this practice can improve soil moisture, enhance crop yields, and mitigate the negative effects of drought and land degradation, especially in arid and semiarid regions (Xiao et al. 2023; Gao et al. 2019). However, the role of plastic film mulching in shaping land use change and promoting green agricultural development—especially under various driving factors—remains unclear in dryland regions in the Loess Plateau (Wang et al. 2023). In response to the current research's lack of knowledge about the mechanisms of land-use evolution and constraints to green agricultural development, this study focuses on the Loess Plateau. First, it examines the central role of land-use change in driving changes in the agrifood system to understand the spatial and temporal evolution of different land-use types and their driving mechanisms. Then, it determines how such changes can affect the agri-food system further. Second, the study thoroughly explores the agricultural, economic, and environmental challenges facing the Loess Plateau. Through a review of the agricultural development history of the Loess Plateau, the study systematically catalogs the contribution of farmland management practices to increased food production, with a focus on the key role played by mulching practices. This study provides a unique perspective on the interactions between technology, policy, and agricultural development. Finally, the study constructs an evaluation system for green agricultural development that includes socioeconomic, crop production, and ecological environment aspects. The relative importance of each covariate in explaining green agricultural development is analyzed and assessed to reveal constraints to green agricultural development in the region.

## 2 | Materials and Methods

## 2.1 | Study Area

In this study, the four core provinces in the Chinese Loess Plateau, namely Shaanxi Province, Shanxi Province, Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region, and Gansu Province, are selected as the main research areas. The specific geographical locations are shown in Figure 1. This region has a typical temperate continental climate, characterized by arid conditions. The annual precipitation is significantly lower than the national average, and the distribution of precipitation is extremely uneven, with obvious seasonal and spatial differences. In terms of soil conditions, the area is mainly covered by sandy soil and saline-alkali soil. Due to their unique physical and chemical properties, these two soil types pose special requirements for crop growth conditions. Sandy soil has poor water- and -fertilizer-etaining capacity, while saline-alkali soil has the problem of excessive salt content, which affects the absorption of water and nutrients by crop roots. Therefore, targeted soil improvement measures are needed to enhance agricultural production efficiency.

## 2.2 | Data Source

This dataset comprehensively covers the core indicators of agricultural production in four key provinces in the Loess Plateau region—Shaanxi, Shanxi, Ningxia, and Gansu—from 1980 to 2020. The construction of this dataset mainly relies on the publicly-released statistical materials by the National Bureau of Statistics of China and the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs, ensuring the authority and reliability of the data (Table 1).

The data were obtained by accessing the official website of the National Bureau of Statistics (https://data.stats.gov.cn/) and the data platforms of relevant agricultural and rural departments. It encompasses the annual sown area of grain and the total output in the above-mentioned four provinces. The collection of this time-series data not only reflects the changing trends in the scale of agricultural production in the Loess Plateau region but also provides a solid data foundation for in-depth analysis of the relationship between grain yield and sown area, assessment of agricultural production efficiency, and exploration of influencing factors. It is worth noting that the unit of measurement for grain yield in this dataset is uniformly ton per hectare (t ha-¹).

## 2.2.1  Research Methodology

The Random Forest algorithm was employed to evaluate the correlations and importance between the yield and 30 agricultural green-development indicators. Continuous variables and categorical variables were examined using Pearson correlation analysis and linear regression analysis, respectively. If p < 0.01, the correlation was considered significant.

The "glmm.hp" package was used for hierarchical partitioning analysis to identify key farm management practice indicators affecting crop yields and to assess the relative contribution of each farm management practice factor through hierarchical partitioning analysis (Lai et al. 2023, 2022).

## 3 | Results

## 3.1 | Land-Use Changes in Loess Plateau (1980- 2020)

In this study, through analyzing the characteristics of landuse changes in the Loess Plateau over nearly 40years (from 1980 to 2020), the significant trends of transformation in landuse types in this region are revealed. The land-use types are subdivided into six categories: cultivated land, forest land, grassland, water area, construction land, and unused land. The total area of cultivated land in the region has shown a continuous decreasing trend over the past 40 years. Specifically, from 1980 to 2000, the cultivated land area decreased by 1720.4ha (Figure 2a). In the subsequent 20-year period (2000-2020), it further declined by 1511.54ha, with an average annual reduction rate of 2.5% (Figure 2b). These results reflect the pressure exerted on cultivated land by the urbanization process and the adjustment of the agricultural structure. The reduction of cultivated land is not an isolated phenomenon but is accompanied by a transformation into other land-use types. Between 1980 and 2000, 353.03ha of cultivated land were converted into other types, with 86.17% of it being transformed into grassland. From 2000 to 2020, this conversion amount increased to 474.54ha. Although grassland remained the primary recipient (75.28%), there was also an increasing trend of conversion into other types such as forest land and construction land. Notably, despite the overall decline in the total area of cultivated land, there was still a conversion of some other land-use types into cultivated land. From 1980 to 2000, 265.69 ha of land were converted into cultivated land from forest land, grassland, water areas, construction land, and unused land. By the period from 2000 to 2020, this figure had increased to 319.59 ha (Figure 2c). During these two time periods, over 90% of the conversions originated from grassland, highlighting the significance of grassland as a potential cultivated land resource. Overall, the land-use changes in the Loess Plateau exhibit a trend of shifting from a single-type pattern to a diversified one, and from agricultural land to nonagricultural land.

During the historical period from 1980 to 2000, the population increased by 31%, indicating a substantial expansion of the population size. Although the per-capita GDP increased by 3.5 times during this period, this economic achievement was unable to fully offset the pressure brought about by the rapid population growth. As vividly shown in Figure 3a, the growth trajectories of the population and GDP gradually diverged, revealing an escalating imbalance between the population surge and the allocation of economic resources. Upon further analysis, Figure 3b reveals that the cultivated land area remained relatively stable during the study period, without significant increases or decreases. This indicates that despite a 31% population increase, the improvement in grain productivity was not reliant on largescale expansion of cultivated land but rather on technological advancements and enhanced production efficiency. Meanwhile, the grain self-sufficiency rate remained stable within the range of 80%-90%. This statistic not only reflects the robust state of national food security but also implies the crucial role that agricultural production plays in meeting the growing demands of the population. In summary, between 1980 and 2000, the improvement in grain productivity went hand in hand with economic development. However, the rapid population growth highlighted the complex relationship and potential contradictions between the two. Enhancing grain productivity emerged as the core objective of agriculture during this period. During the period from 2000 to 2020, the economy witnessed remarkable and sustained development. As shown in Fig (a), the per-capita GDP experienced exponential and rapid growth. Meanwhile, the population growth rate slowed down compared to the previous period. This economic achievement, coupled with the changes in population dynamics, together constitutes a significant characteristic of the socioeconomic development during this period.

## 3.2 | Analysis of Population-Yield Change Characteristics

In the agricultural sector, although Figure 3b indicates that the cultivated land area declined to some extent during this period, the grain self-sufficiency rate shifted from a state of insufficiency to self-sufficiency and even exceeded that level. Notably, in the Loess Plateau region, the grain self-sufficiency rate increased significantly from 62% in 1980 to 110% in 2020. Since 2020, the cultivation of cash crops has witnessed a remarkable upsurge, with the planting area expanding fivefold to sixfold compared to the past. This trend is directly reflected in the substantial increase in the proportion of cash crops, which soared from 2%-4% in 1980 to 10%-20% in 2020. As depicted in Figure 4, the per-unit yields of major grain crops such as maize and wheat have all witnessed substantial increases. Taking maize as an example, its per-unit yield rose from 2.55-3.54tha−1 in 1980 to 5.19-7.56tha−1 in 2020. The per-unit yield of wheat also increased from 1.19-1.73 to 3.35-4.58 t ha−¹. Moreover, the overall per-unit yield of grain climbed from 1.61-1.95 to 4.30-5.43t ha−¹. Against the backdrop of limited and decreasing cultivated land area, the guarantee of food security is primarily attributed to the remarkable increase in the per-unit yield of grain.

The remarkable enhancement of agricultural mechanization stands out as a key highlight in this transformation. Between 1980 and 2020, the total power of China's agricultural machinery jumped from 14.54× 104 to 69.17×104kW, a significant increase of 375.7%. Notably, during the period from 2010 to 2015, the level of mechanization ascended at an unprecedented pace, reaching a high of 95.35× 106kW in 2015 (Figure 5). This shift has not only significantly boosted agricultural production efficiency but also facilitated the large-scale and standardized development of agricultural production, laying a solid foundation for grain yield increase. The trend of mechanization provides the technological basis for largescale mulching. The extensive use of agricultural plastic films has also significantly increased crop yields. Starting from 43,700 tons in 1991, the amount had grown to 262,600 tons by 2020 (Figure 5). Especially since 2010, the usage of agricultural plastic films has shown a rapid growth trend, reaching a peak of 302,800 tons in 2016. The popularization of agricultural plastic films has effectively improved the farmland environment, increased soil temperature and humidity, promoted crop growth, and thus contributed to the steady increase in crop yields.

## 3.3 | Assessment of AGD

Taking 1980, 2000, and 2020 (the benchmark years of AGD— AGD policies) as examples, this study classified 30 selected relevant AGD indicators into four levels (IV, III, II, and I) to deduce the current situation and characteristics of the agricultural structure's AGD under land-use changes (Figure 6). Overall, the number of social-economic indicators at Level I decreased from 10 in 1980. By 2020, all these indicators had been improved. This paper analyzes the influencing factors of the spatial differentiation of grain production. The average explanatory power of social-economic factors on the spatial differentiation of grain yield is at a medium level.

Covariates were employed to explore the primary driving factors of land-use change on agriculture. These covariates were sourced from the IPCC and relevant literature and could be categorized into three types: socioeconomic factors (10 variables), crop production factors (10 variables), and ecological and environmental factors (10 variables). For detailed information on the 30 covariates, please refer to Table S1. Based on the dataset, the relative importance of each covariate in explaining AGD was evaluated through Random Forest analysis (Breiman 2001). The importance value was calculated as the mean increase in the mean squared error (%IncMSE). We utilized regression analysis to assess the differences among the relative importance of the three categories of covariates. A difference was considered significant if p < 0.05. We also examined the correlations between yields and the 30 covariates. Continuous variables and categorical variables were tested using Pearson correlation analysis and linear regression analysis, respectively. A correlation was considered significant if p < 0.05. We observed that socioeconomic aspects, food production, and ecological and environmental aspects had similar importance in explaining AGD (p = 0.05; Figure 7a; Table S1). The variance fractions uniquely explained by each category of these variables were substantial (5%–15%). Most of the 30 covariates were significantly positively correlated with AGD (Figure 7c).

## 4 | Discussion

## 4.1 | Analysis of the Driving Forces Behind Land-Use Changes

From 1980 to 2020, the total area of cultivated land in the Loess Plateau continuously decreased. Specifically, it decreased by 3231.94 ha over this period, with an average annual reduction rate of 2.5%. Concurrently, the decrease in cultivated land was accompanied by its conversion into other land-use types, with grassland being the primary destination. Overall, the land-use change in this region demonstrated a trend of transformation from a single-type pattern to a diversified one, and from agricultural land to nonagricultural land (Ning et al. 2018). In terms of population and the economy, during the period from 1980 to 2000, the population increased by 31% and the per capita GDP grew by 3.5 times. The dual drive of population growth and socioeconomic development has increased the demand for crops for food, fodder, and fuel, leading to intensive use of resources for means of agricultural production, jeopardizing system stability and environmental integrity (Jwaideh and Dalin 2025). However, the pressure brought about by population growth was not fully offset by the economic achievements. The growth trajectories of the population and GDP deviated from each other, reflecting an imbalance in the distribution of population and economic resources. During this period, the cultivated land area remained relatively stable. The increase in grain productivity was attributed to technological advancements and improvements in production efficiency. One of the most effective strategies for increasing crop yields in drylands is plastic film mulching (PM) (Liu et al. 2025). The grain self-sufficiency rate remained stable within the range of 80%-90%, indicating the crucial role of agricultural production in ensuring food security.

Since 1999, ecological restoration projects such as the Grainfor-Green Program and the conversion of cropland to forest have been rigorously and extensively implemented in China, including the Loess Plateau region (Zhou et al. 2020). The spatial distribution of land-use changes in the Loess Plateau exhibits a remarkable trend, with a reduction in cultivated land and an expansion of grassland occurring in numerous areas. The implementation of these policies is the primary driving force behind these land-use changes (Huang et al. 2021). In areas of the Loess Plateau with more robust economic growth, the demands for urban expansion and industrial development often prompt the conversion of cultivated land into construction land, thus accelerating the decline in cultivated land area (Miao et al. 2021). The main causes of the reduction in cultivated land are likely the acceleration of the urbanization process and agricultural structural adjustments (Liu et al. 2018). Driven by population growth and economic development, the rapid expansion of urban areas and infrastructure construction has led to a substantial encroachment on cultivated land (Wang et al. 2016; Bai et al. 2014). Simultaneously, the shift toward more profitable agricultural activities, such as the cultivation of high-value crops or the development of animal husbandry, has also contributed to the conversion of cultivated land (Liu et al. 2024; Zhu et al. 2021). The increase in forest land area not only reflects the region's emphasis on ecological environmental protection but also represents the successful implementation of policies like the conversion of cropland to forest and the Grain-for-Green Program.

During the period from 1980 to 2000, rapid population growth coexisted with economic development. Gradually, an imbalance emerged between the pressure on resource demand brought about by population growth and the achievements of economic growth. Despite the increase in population, the cultivated land area did not expand significantly (Ma et al. 2018). However, the improvement in grain productivity was achievable, which was attributed to advancements in agricultural technology and enhancements in production efficiency (Jiao et al. 2016). This reflected a transformation in agricultural production methods. The stable grain self-sufficiency rate indicated that agricultural production played a crucial role in meeting the demands of population growth. It also implied that agricultural development at that time focused on tapping the potential of existing cultivated land to cope with the pressure of food demand resulting from population growth. From 2000 to 2020, the remarkable economic development and the slowdown in population growth rate jointly shaped the socioeconomic characteristics of this period. Economic growth provided the economic foundation for land-use changes, influencing the allocation of land for different purposes. Moreover, the change in the population growth rate indirectly affected the demand for cultivated land and other land-use types.

## 4.2 | Socio-Managerial Factors Affecting Yield Changes

In the agricultural sector, although Figure 3b indicates a decline in the cultivated land area during this period, the grain self-sufficiency rate has shifted from a state of insufficiency to self-sufficiency. Notably, in the Loess Plateau region, the grain self-sufficiency rate has significantly increased from 62% in 1980 to 110% in 2020. Since 2020, the cultivation of cash crops has witnessed a remarkable growth, with the planting area expanding five to six times compared to the past. This trend is directly reflected in the substantial increase in the proportion of cash crops, which has soared from 2%-4% in 1980 to 10%-20% in 2020. As shown in Figure 4, the per-unit yields of major grain crops such as maize and wheat have increased substantially. Taking maize as an example, its per-unit yield has risen from 2.55-3.54tha−1 in 1980 to 5.19–7.56tha−¹ in 2020. Additionally, the overall perunit yield of grain has climbed from 1.61–1.95 tons per hectare to 4.30-5.43tha−¹. Against the backdrop of limited and decreasing cultivated land area, the guarantee of food security is primarily attributed to the remarkable increase in the per-unit yield of grain.

The remarkable enhancement of agricultural mechanization stands out as a key highlight in this transformative process (Lu et al. 2024; Wang et al. 2025). Since 1980, the total power of agricultural machinery in China has experienced a leap-forward growth, surging from 14.54× 106 to 69.17 × 106kW in 2020. This shift has not only significantly boosted agricultural production efficiency but also propelled the large-scale and standardized development of agricultural production, laying a solid foundation for grain yield increase. The widespread use of agricultural plastic films has significantly increased crop yields. Studies in agricultural fields have shown that using plastic film for mulching can increase crop yields by over 30%. This practice is crucial for ensuring the security of agricultural products (Fan et al. 2017). The film's ability to retain soil moisture, regulate ground temperature, and suppress weed growth creates optimal conditions for crop growth and development in various agricultural environments, thereby improving yields. The consumption of agricultural plastic films grew from 43,700t in 1991 to 262,600t in 2020. The widespread use of agricultural plastic films has effectively improved the farmland environment by raising soil temperature and humidity, which in turn has promoted crop growth and thus driven the steady increase in crop yields.

Despite the reduction in cultivated land area, the increase in the grain self-sufficiency rate is primarily attributed to the remarkable rise in the per-unit grain yield. This phenomenon reflects a shift in the agricultural production model from relying on the expansion of land area to depending on the enhancement of per-unit-area output. The increase in the planting area and proportion of cash crops indicates an adjustment in the agricultural industrial structure (Zhang, Feng, et al. 2024; Zhang, Yong, et al. 2024; Zhang, Zhou, et al. 2024; Nouri et al. 2020). Driven by economic interests, farmers, while ensuring food security, may allocate some of their cultivated land to grow cash crops with higher returns. The substantial growth in the perunit yield of major grain crops not only makes this structural adjustment feasible but also guarantees the stability of the grain supply (Hawkesford et al. 2013).

The substantial increase in the level of agricultural mechanization is a crucial factor driving the growth of grain production (Ma et al. 2023). With the rapid growth of the total power of agricultural machinery, especially during the highspeed development period from 2010 to 2015, mechanized operations replaced a large amount of human and animal labor, significantly enhancing production efficiency. The realization of large-scale and standardized agricultural production has made various agricultural processes, such as planting, irrigation, fertilization, and harvesting, more precise and efficient, thereby contributing to the increase in grain yields. This also reflects the powerful impetus of technological progress on agricultural development, providing strong support for the transformation of agriculture toward modernization. The continuous increase in the usage of agricultural plastic films has made a remarkable contribution to the growth of crop yields (Zhang et al. 2018). The extensive application of these films has improved the microenvironment of farmland. By increasing soil temperature and humidity, it has created more favorable conditions for crop growth. This environmental optimization has promoted the growth and development of crops, thus increasing crop yields. It is worth noting that, while increasing crop yields, mulching has also brought about environmental problems that cannot be ignored. Currently plastic pollution has become an important factor driving environmental change, with soil quality degradation triggered by agricultural film residues being a major bottleneck constraining sustainable agricultural development (Qi et al. 2020). The gradual fragmentation of mulch during long-term use generates microplastic pollution, and the accumulation of these plastic fragments in the environment has been shown to negatively affect soil functioning, thereby posing a major challenge to environmental sustainability (Wang et al. 2024; Zhao et al. 2024). In the future, sustainable agricultural production methods should be explored to balance the relationship between yield improvement and environmental protection Overall, the application of agricultural mechanization and agricultural plastic films represents important means of ensuring food security and promoting agricultural development under the condition of limited cultivated land resources.

## 4.3 | Observation of the Combined Influence of Multiple Driving Factors

The per capita GDP exhibits a stronger explanatory power for grain output, ranking among the top three factors. The per capita GDP reflects the comprehensive strength of farmers' income and industrial collaboration. More importantly, regional economic growth indicates an increase in farmers' income. While grain output rises, nonagricultural income also becomes substantial. The urbanization rate mainly affects the spatial differentiation of grain consumption, yet its impact is not significant. The influence of income level on grain consumption shows an upward trend. Income level serves as the foundation for consumption. The explanation of disposable income of residents for grain consumption is on the rise. As living standards rise, consumption patterns become more varied, and grain consumption is less directly linked to income. From 1980 to 2000, as people's living standards were relatively low, there was a close relationship between grain production and per capita GDP. With the improvement of residents' living conditions and material standards, the proportion of food consumption expenditure has declined, and the influence of farmers' income on grain consumption has weakened (Li, Chen, et al. 2024; Li, Kou, et al. 2024). However, residents' demand for vegetables and fruits has increased, reducing the differences in dietary structure. From 1980 to 2000, as people's living standards were relatively low, there was a close relationship between grain production and per capita GDP. With the improvement of residents' living conditions and material standards, the proportion of food consumption expenditure has declined, and the influence of farmers' income on grain consumption has weakened (Wang et al. 2018). However, residents' demand for vegetables and fruits has increased, reducing the differences in dietary structure.

Overall, the indicators of grain production demonstrate an up ward trend. Nutrient utilization efficiency is the natural factor with the most potent explanatory power for the spatial divergence of grain yields (Rosolem and Husted 2024), followed by plastic film mulching and straw returning to the field. As the area of cultivated land decreases in the context of land use, the modification of arable land indicates a decline in the restrictive impact of land type on grain production (Zhang and Tian 2024; Zhang, Yong, et al. 2024; Zhang, Zhou, et al. 2024) Farmland management measures serve as the fundamental conditions for grain production. The ecological environment indicators rank higher than those of the socioeconomic aspects and grain production. Plastic film mulching and pesticides are the two most crucial production factors influencing grain production (Serrano-Ruiz et al. 2021). During the conversion of the majority of cultivated land, the accumulation of soil organic matter has decreased. In 2000, it played a pivotal role in the steady increase of crop yields. However, from the perspective of agricultural resource allocation theory, the intensity of fertilizer use has a weak explanatory power on crop yield enhancement. The amount of fertilizer becomes a key factor affecting high and low yield. Only reducing the intensity of chemical fertilizer and land use can enhance the yield per unit of fertilizer (Mózner et al. 2012). In summary, the results of the single-factor detection of AGD factors indicate that ecological environment elements are of crucial importance to grain production, followed by changes in socioeconomic elements. The influence of factor inputs on grain production has decreased. There is a synergistic effect among various factors. Specifically, the interaction between the intensity of chemical fertilizer application and labor force is significant. In 2020, regional production and cultivation will have the largest increase in the explanatory power of total power of agricultural machinery, effective irrigation rate, and fertilizer application intensity. In particular, diesel consumption by agricultural machinery accounts for about one-third of the country's total diesel consumption. The combustion of this fossil fuel releases many air pollutants that seriously threaten China's carbon neutrality targets and plans to improve air quality (Zhuang et al. 2025). Furthermore, multiple driving factors jointly govern AGD. First, in terms of the socioeconomy, under favorable social policies, farmers' incomes increase more rapidly. This enables fewer cultivated lands to meet the needs of a larger population (e.g., an increase in cash crops), thus leading to an increase in the input of soil nutrient utilization, which is consistent with the findings of our research.

## 5 | Conclusion

Land-use in the Loess Plateau underwent significant transformation. The cultivated land area decreased at an average annual rate of 2.5%, with a shift toward more diversified land-use types, especially conversion to grassland. Policy-driven ecological restoration projects, along with urbanization and agricultural structural adjustments, were the main drivers of these changes. These factors also interacted with population and economic growth, highlighting an imbalance in resource distribution between 1980 and 2000, and a more complex relationship between economic development, population growth rate, and land-use from 2000 to 2020. In terms of agricultural yields, despite the reduction in cultivated land, the grain self-sufficiency rate improved significantly, mainly due to a remarkable increase in per-unit grain yield. Agricultural mechanization and the use of agricultural plastic films were identified as crucial factors contributing to yield growth. Multiple driving factors were found to jointly influence AGD. Among them, ecological environment elements were of primary importance for grain production, followed by socioeconomic changes, while the influence of factor inputs declined. The per capita GDP had a strong explanatory power for grain output, and various factors, such as urbanization rate, income level, and dietary changes, also played roles in different aspects of agricultural production and consumption. In conclusion, the study emphasizes the complex interplay of ecological, social, economic, and technological factors in shaping agricultural development in the Loess Plateau.
