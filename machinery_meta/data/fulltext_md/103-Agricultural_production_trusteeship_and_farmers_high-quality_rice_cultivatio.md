## ABSTRACT

Against the backdrop of accelerated global agricultural transformation and continuous growth in market demand for high-quality grain products, agricultural production trusteeship, as an innovative model within the modern agricultural service system, plays a pivotal role in promoting the enhancement of both quality and efficiency in grain production. Grounded in the transaction cost theory and the division of labor theory and incorporating a farmer production model, this paper constructs a theoretical analytical framework termed "Agricultural Production Trusteeship (APT) - Factor Constraints Alleviation (FCA) - Farmland Scale Management (FSM) - High-Quality Rice Cultivation (HRC)" to examine the impact of APT on farmers' HRC and its underlying mechanisms, with empirical validation conducted using micro-survey data gathered from rice growers in Jiangxi Province, a major rice-producing region in China.The research findings demonstrate that APT substantially enhances farmers' HRC, a conclusion that holds firm even after addressing endogeneity concerns and undergoing a battery of robustness tests. Mechanism analysis reveals that APT generates a substitution effect by FCA, thereby offsetting the shortcomings of small farmers in terms of labor, machinery, information, and technology. Consequently, this reduces transaction costs and facilitates HRC. Simultaneously, APT yields a division-of-labor effect through FSM, thereby integrating small farmers into the division-based economy and encouraging contiguous specialization, which promotes HRC. Notably, APT can through FCA, moreover expedite FSM, thus promoting farmers' HRC. Further analysis finds that trusteeship in pre-production stage and semi-trusteeship model of APT are more efficacious in promoting farmers' HRC. However, the promotional effect of APT on HRC is relatively weaker for risk-averse farmers and those who have not participated in variety training programs.This study offers policy insights for numerous developing countries dominated by small farmers, aiming to accelerate the establishment of ATP systems and steer farmers toward actively participating in the high-quality crop varieties cultivation.

## 1. Introduction

At present, the global agricultural sector is undergoing an accelerated transformation, with the transition from traditional extensive production models to efficient, green, and high-quality production emerging as the mainstream trend (Arouna et al., 2021). As residents' living standards rise and consumption structures upgrade, consumers' demand for grain products is gradually shifting from "having sufficient food" to "enjoying high-quality meals" and "consuming healthy food." The market demand for high-quality grain products exhibits a sustained growth trajectory (Herforth et al., 2025). Agriculture, as a cornerstone industry of the national economy, has ushered new opportunities while confronting more stringent requirements (Bustos et al., 2016).Owing to the problems of tightening resource constraints and low production efficiency in traditional agricultural production models, it has become increasingly challenging for them to keep pace with the market environment characterized by a growing demand for high-quality grain (Tilman et al., 2011). Moreover, the continuous expansion of the global population and the frequent occurrence of extreme weather events triggered by climate change have further exacerbated the pressure on grain supply. How to enhance agricultural productivity and ensure a stable supply of high-quality grain has thus become an urgent task for governments around the world (Mao et al., 2025). Particularly in developing countries, promoting agricultural technologies and improving productivity are of paramount importance for fostering economic growth, alleviating poverty, and achieving sustainable development goals (Islam, 2024).Against this backdrop, high-quality varieties, serving as the core technological carriers for enhancing agricultural productivity, are increasingly underscoring their significance. These varieties contribute as much as 50 %-90 % to global yield growth (Rozelle et al., 2003; Bruins, 2009). High-quality varieties can not only drive the transformation of traditional subsistence agriculture into efficient agricultural systems but also, while boosting yields, take into account biodiversity conservation, thereby achieving the coordinated development of ecological and economic benefits (Anik et al., 2023). Furthermore, high-quality varieties possess stronger environmental adaptability, enabling them to effectively cope with the risks posed by climate change, ensure the stability of grain production, and provide sustained supply support for the escalating demand for high-quality grain (Hultgren et al., 2025; Chemura et al., 2025).As a major agricultural nation, China places great emphasis on the development of the seed industry and the promotion of high-quality varieties. From 2020 to 2023, the "No. 1 Central Document" issued by the central government each year has designated the research and development of key seed technologies and the promotion of high-quality varieties as key tasks. Through policy support and capital investment, it has injected vitality into seed industry innovation and the popularization of high-quality varieties. However, despite the significant advantages of high-quality varieties and the increasing policy support, obstacles still persist in their actual promotion process. For instance, the long seed production cycle, the high difficulty for farmers in selecting appropriate seeds, and the increased planting risks have led many farmers to continue using traditional low-yielding varieties (Chemura et al., 2025). The aforementioned phenomena reflect a structural imbalance between technology supply and adoption behavior (Mao et al., 2025). Therefore, understanding farmers' planting behaviors regarding high-quality varieties is of crucial importance for breaking through the dilemma of variety promotion, meeting the market demand for high-quality grain, and aligning with the trend of agricultural transformation.

Planting high-quality varieties essentially constitutes farmers' adoption behavior with respect to new technologies, and current research predominantly centers on the factors influencing farmers' adoption decisions.First, personal characteristics, encompassing age, education level, non-agricultural employment, farming experience, living environment, and risk preference, significantly influence adoption decisions (Chandio and Yuansheng, 2018; Bannor et al., 2020). The latest research by Mao et al. (2025) indicates that the aging of the agricultural labor force further impedes farmers' adoption of improved varieties by heightening risk aversion tendencies and reducing farm size. Second, management and scale factors, such as cultivated land area, joint decision-making, storage technology, bargaining power, market demand, and selling price, affect whether farmers adopt high-quality varieties (Wossen et al., 2024; Euler et al., 2024).Third, farmers' social networks and information acquisition capabilities also have an impact on the adoption of high-quality varieties (Varshney et al., 2022).Fourth, variety characteristics, including yield stability and environmental adaptability, are key determinants of farmers' adoption behavior (Asrat et al., 2010).Lastly, and most crucially, different promotion models significantly affect the adoption effectiveness of high-quality varieties among farmers. The promotion of high-quality varieties mainly relies on three models: the government-led model, the market-oriented model, and the service organization promotion model. Under the government-led model, the government formulates supportive policies, organizes agricultural technology promotion teams, and regularly conducts technical training and promotion activities to encourage farmers to adopt high-quality varieties (Nakano et al., 2018). However, this model heavily depends on administrative mechanisms, making it challenging to establish a sustainable system that incentivizes farmers' active participation. Moreover, its effectiveness is influenced by variations in local execution capabilities and financial investment.In contrast, the market-oriented model rapidly disseminates high-quality varieties through channels such as agricultural e-commerce platforms and social media. Although this model has a wide reach, its effectiveness is constrained by differences in farmers' technological capabilities and insufficient information acquisition, and it has not yet fully realized its potential (Shiferaw et al., 2011).On the other hand, the service organization promotion model, through its localized service network rooted in rural areas, places greater emphasis on meeting farmers' practical needs and providing technical support, thereby facilitating information exchange. When promoting high-quality varieties, this model is more practically feasible at the operational level and yields better actual promotion results (Su et al., 2025; Tran et al., 2025). Unfortunately, the impact of this model on farmers' adoption of high-quality varieties has not received sufficient attention in existing research.

Agricultural production trusteeship represents one of the key forms of promotion carried out by service organizations. As an innovative model within the modern agricultural service system, it denotes a cooperative arrangement in which farmers, while retaining their land management rights, entrust some or all facets of agricultural production to third-party trusteeship management organizations (Zhao et al., 2023). Compared to the general outsourcing of production processes, agricultural production trusteeship demonstrates a higher level of standardization and organization, with more stable supply-demand relationships and stronger interest linkages between the involved parties (Lu and Yuan, 2021).Agricultural production trusteeship management breaks free from the operational predicament of traditional agriculture, which is characterized by "small-scale and fragmented" production. It adheres to the principle of "farmer-led, service-assisted" (Zhong et al., 2025). Specifically, farmers (the principals) retain decision-making power over crop variety selection and cultivation practices, while service organizations (the agents) are required to provide customized professional services based on the farmers' determined needs for planting high-quality varieties. Therefore, agricultural production trusteeship may play a pivotal role in facilitating farmers' decisions to plant high-quality varieties: On the one hand, by leveraging their professional expertise and market information, trusteeship organizations can guide farmers in selecting high-quality varieties that are well-suited to local conditions and have promising market potential (Hendrikse and Veerman, 2001). Moreover, stable trusteeship relationships and interest linkage mechanisms can enhance farmers' anticipated returns from planting high-quality rice, thereby encouraging them to actively adopt such varieties (Arouna et al., 2021). On the other hand, agricultural production trusteeship constitutes an important form of specialized division of labor in agriculture, enabling the reduction of risks and costs associated with planting high-quality varieties through indirect investment approaches, thus bolstering farmers' willingness and motivation to cultivate high-quality varieties (Fu et al., 2025).By the end of 2022, the annual area under agricultural production trusteeship management in China had reached 1.67 billion mu-times, involving over 78 million small farmers (Sun et al., 2024). It has gradually emerged as a significant driving force in promoting the organic integration of small farmers and modern agricultural development in China.

Existing literatures have conducted numerous valuable explorations regarding the effects of agricultural production trusteeship:First, it focuses on the impact of agricultural production trusteeship on agricultural input-output. Using survey data from 1565 farming households in the major rice-producing regions of Hubei Province, Zhang et al., 2023a, b found that agricultural production trusteeship significantly promotes rice yield per unit area. Moreover, it can effectively enhance farmers total factor productivity (Zhao et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2025). Verkaart et al. (2017) discovered that agricultural outsourcing services can reduce planting costs through the factor substitution effect, exhibiting a pronounced factor allocation effect (Geng and Pan, 2024), thereby facilitating increases in crop yield and production efficiency. Additionally, while helping farmers overcome constraints in labor, capital, and technological endowments and reducing factor inputs, agricultural production trusteeship may also encourage farmers to transfer in land for large-scale land management, thereby improving economies of scale (Jiang and Song, 2025).Second, attention is directed towards the influence of agricultural production trusteeship on farmers' income. By enhancing crop yield, expanding cultivated land area, and reducing production costs, agricultural production trusteeship increases farming income (Baiyegunhi et al., 2019). The shift to non-agricultural employment also leads to an increase in wage income (Zhang et al., 2024a,b). Furthermore, through technological progress and efficiency improvements, agricultural production trusteeship can elevate agricultural total factor productivity, ultimately narrowing the urban-rural income gap (Ren et al., 2025). Third, consideration is given to the effects of agricultural production trusteeship on the rural environment. Utilizing provincial panel data from China spanning 2012 to 2021, Zhao et al. (2023) explored the carbon emission performance of agricultural production trusteeship and found that it significantly suppresses carbon emissions in the crop farming sector. In contrast, Bambio and Agha (2018) argue that the emergence of agricultural production services, driven by commercial profit motives, has exacerbated the irrational use of chemical fertilizers. Additionally, some studies suggest that participation in agricultural production trusteeship will enhance farmers' willingness to transition to green production, increase the probability of adopting green production technologies, improve green production efficiency, and ultimately enhance the overall rural environment (Geng and Pan, 2024; Zhang et al., 2023a,b).

Overall, the existing literatures present the following limitations: First, prior studies have thoroughly investigated how diverse socioeconomic factors and government policies affect farmers' adoption of high - quality crop varieties. However, there is a notably scarce amount of research on the role of agricultural production trusteeship, a service organization promotion model, in this regard, and this paper aims to fill this research gap. Second, research on the impact effects of agricultural production trusteeship has mainly concentrated on agricultural input - output, farmers' income, and the rural environment, while neglecting its influence on a crucial farmer production behavior: high - quality crop varieties cultivation. Furthermore, the impact mechanisms also have not been subjected to a systematic analysis.Third, only a limited number of studies have differentiated the disparities stemming from trusteeship services that encompass various stages (pre - production, in - production, and post - production) and different models. Although a small proportion of studies might have explored the differences between labor - intensive and technology - intensive trusteeship types during the in - production stage (Geng and Pan, 2024; Zhang et al., 2023a,b), the factors affecting farmers' decisions to cultivate high - quality varieties may not be confined solely to trusteeship services during the in - production stage. Additionally, few studies have considered that variations in farmers' risk preferences or external support conditions can result in differences in their decisions to cultivate high - quality varieties through agricultural production trusteeship, there may be an inadequate understanding of the practical issues, potential opportunities, and challenges encountered in the development of agricultural production trusteeship and high - quality crop varieties cultivation by farmers.

In view of these, this study employs survey data collected from rice - farming households in Jiangxi Province, China, to investigate how agricultural production trusteeship influences farmers' high - quality rice cultivation. When compared with existing research, this paper makes the following contributions: First, from research perspective, this paper is the pioneer in confirming that the service promotion model embodied by agricultural production trusteeship can significantly drive farmers to cultivate high - quality rice varieties. It enriches the existing body of evidence on the factors affecting farmers' adoption of high - quality varieties, presenting a novel perspective for promoting high - quality varieties and enhancing the quality and efficiency of grain production in developing countries dominated by small farmers.Second, at the theoretical level, grounded in transaction cost theory and division of labor theory, this paper constructs a theoretical framework: "agricultural production trusteeship - factor constraints alleviation - farmland scale management - high-quality rice cultivation". It elucidates the mechanism by which agricultural production trusteeship impacts farmers' high - quality rice cultivation, thereby further refining and expanding the assessment of the development effects of agricultural production trusteeship and offering a new breakthrough point for relevant theoretical research.Third, at the policy level, this research explores and identifies the heterogeneous impacts of agricultural production trusteeship at different stages and with different models on high - quality rice cultivation by farmers with diverse endowment conditions. It provides practical policy references for countries to further scientifically optimize the precise provision of agricultural production trusteeship and encourage farmers to cultivate high - quality varieties.

## 2. Theoretical framework and research hypothesis

## 2.1. The direct impact of agricultural production trusteeship on farmers' high - quality rice cultivation

Epitomized by Schultz (1964), the rational small - farmer school contends that farmers are rational actors who strive to maximize their benefits, making production decisions through a cost - benefit analysis. When acting as rational economic agents in the decision - making process regarding the cultivation of high - quality rice, farmers' primary concern is to maximize returns while operating under cost constraints. The disparities in risk, cost, and profit between high - quality rice and ordinary rice exert a direct influence on this decision - making process (Mao et al., 2025). For an extended period, China has been implementing a two - tier management system that takes household contract management as the foundation and integrates unified management with separate management. As the fundamental units in agricultural production and operation, farmers typically operate within specific production and business contexts as well as socio - cultural milieus, which can result in a "path dependence" on traditional production methods. This "path dependence" leads farmers to be swayed by existing production models and experiences when confronted with new production options.The study by Atanu and Schwart (1994) reveals that the intensity of a producer's adoption of new technology hinges on their awareness of the technology and their decision to adopt it. Essentially, the cultivation of high - quality rice represents a process of farmers adopting new varieties, which is also characterized by high risks and slow returns. For agricultural producers and operators, particularly small farmers, due to their limited resource endowments and weak risk - bearing capacities, their willingness and level of new high - quality rice varieties adoption are far from satisfactory.It is noteworthy that agricultural production trusteeship can pool resources and offer professional services, thereby reducing the costs and risks associated with small farmers cultivating high - quality rice and enhancing their farming returns. It is anticipated to emerge as a crucial approach to resolving the predicament faced by small farmers in cultivating high - quality rice. 2.2. The influence mechanism of agricultural production trusteeship on farmers' high-quality rice cultivation

## 2.2.1. Factor constraints alleviation

Coase (1960) was the first to proposethe real existence of transaction costs and introduce them into the economic analysis of firms, pointing out that a firm's choice of production method depends on the relative magnitudes of management costs and transaction costs. Building on this, Williamson (1975) argued that the cause of transaction costs lies in market failures resulting from the combined influence of individuals and the trading environment. Dahlman (1979) categorized the contents of transaction activities into search costs, negotiation and decision - making costs, contract costs, monitoring costs, and switching costs.In summary, transaction costs are an umbrella term encompassing various costs incurred during market transactions, including those related to information search, coordination, negotiation of terms, and the smooth execution of transactions. Transaction costs originate from the division of labor and, in a reciprocal manner, define the scope of the market and the extent of division - of - labor deepening. Their magnitude is contingent upon transaction efficiency.Agricultural production trusteeship falls within the domain of transactions and, as such, naturally gives rise to negotiation and monitoring costs. When acquiring agricultural production trusteeship services, farmers will inevitably take into account factors such as the asset - specificity requirements associated with trusteeship different production links, scale constraints, and the controllability of risks. Based on these considerations, they will ultimately determine whether to trusteeship and which specific links to trusteeship(Verkaart et al., 2017). Therefore, it can be reasonably argued that agricultural production trusteeship is the outcome of farmers' rational evaluation of their household economic benefits(Zhong et al., 2025). Assuming that the production efficiency of farmers' self - production is equivalent to that of trusteeship, farmers will make trusteeship decisions after conducting a rational analysis of the relative magnitudes of their management costs and transaction costs. When management costs surpass transaction costs, farmers will opt for trusteeship; conversely, they will choose self - production.

The emergence of agricultural production trusteeship has brought about significant changes in farmers' factor inputs and agricultural management approaches. It has relaxed the constraints imposed by farmers' household resource endowments, forming a "substitution relationship" with them. This has led to a reduction in transaction costs during the production process, weakened the limitations imposed by objective production conditions, and effectively promoted farmers' high - quality rice cultivation. Specifically, these effects are manifested in the following four aspects.

2.2.1.1. Alleviating Labor Constraints. The comparative advantage inherent in non - agricultural employment has drawn a substantial number of rural young and middle - aged laborers to seek employment opportunities outside their hometowns, leaving behind elderly and physically vulnerable individuals to undertake farming activities. This phenomenon has led to an unstable supply of household agricultural labor, characterized by both an inadequate quantity and sub - par quality (Mao et al., 2025).When farmers opt to handle all production processes independently, they may find it necessary to hire temporary labor. This hiring process entails search costs associated with identifying suitable workers. Concurrently, engaging in negotiations with the hired labor regarding working hours, wage levels, and other contractual terms incurs negotiation and decision - making costs. Moreover, overseeing the work process of the hired labor to ensure that they fulfill production tasks in accordance with requirements generates supervision costs (Zhang et al., 2024a,b).In stark contrast, providers of agricultural production trusteeship services generally possess a relatively stable and

well - trained labor force. By purchasing trusteeship services, farmers can eliminate the need to expend significant amounts of effort and bear costs related to searching for, negotiating with, and supervising labor. Leveraging their professional management and operational models, trusteeship service providers are capable of efficiently organizing labor for production, thereby reducing the transaction costs stemming from labor - related issues. This, in turn, allows farmers to redirect more effort towards other economic activities, ultimately enhancing their household economic welfare (Li and Ito, 2021).

2.2.1.2. Alleviating Machinery Constraints. Agricultural production cannot be achieved exclusively through human labor; it requires the input of agricultural machinery. The agricultural machinery utilized in distinct stages of agricultural production exhibits significant differences, showcasing a high level of asset specificity. Were farmers to independently procure all the requisite agricultural machinery for each production stage, they would face the issue of inadequate funds, and the substantial capital outlay would further increase their financial strain

(Zhou et al., 2025).Due to the limited planting scale, the frequency of machinery utilization is relatively low, leading to excessively high purchase and operational costs for machinery. Under such circumstances, if farmers intend to employ machinery for production, they may have to resort to renting it (Zheng et al., 2025). This incurs search costs related to locating machinery rental information, as well as negotiation and decision - making costs when bargaining over rental prices and durations with machinery owners. Simultaneously, during the machinery utilization process, it is crucial to monitor the operation and quality of the machinery to ensure the uninterrupted progress of production, which, in turn, generates supervision costs.Providers of agricultural production trusteeship services are typically outfitted with a complete and advanced range of agricultural machinery, accompanied by professional operators. After farmers opt to purchase trusteeship services, they no longer need to purchase or rent machinery on their own, thus avoiding various transaction costs associated with machinery procurement and rental. The trusteeship service providers can also organize the use of machinery in a rational and efficient manner in accordance with production demands, enhancing the utilization rate of machinery. This enables farmers to reap the benefits of mechanized production at a reduced cost, ultimately elevating production efficiency (Belton et al. 2021).

2.2.1.3. Alleviating Information Constraints. During agricultural production, farmers are required to access a substantial volume of information, including market trends, planting techniques, and meteorological data, to make well - informed and rational production decisions. Nevertheless, farmers face limitations in terms of the channels through which they can obtain such information, and ensuring the authenticity and accuracy of the information poses a significant challenge (Abdul, 2023). To secure reliable information, farmers may have to devote a considerable amount of time and effort to searching, which gives rise to information - search costs.Furthermore, due to information asymmetry, farmers frequently find themselves in a disadvantaged position when conducting transactions with agricultural input suppliers and agricultural product purchasers. They are susceptible to unfair treatment, such as price fraud. This not only heightens the uncertainty associated with transactions but may also result in a loss of farmers' interests, thereby indirectly increasing transaction costs (Shen et al., 2022).Providers of agricultural production trusteeship services generally boast more extensive information channels and more professional information - analysis capabilities (Holloway et al., 2000). They are capable of promptly capturing market dynamics, updates in planting techniques, and other pertinent information, and then conveying it to farmers. By purchasing trusteeship services, farmers can take advantage of the information superiority of the trusteeship service providers, thus reducing information - search costs.At the same time, when trusteeship service providers engage in transactions with agricultural input suppliers and agricultural product purchasers, they possess certain bargaining power. This empowers them to negotiate more favorable prices and more advantageous transaction terms for farmers, thereby mitigating the transaction costs caused by information asymmetry and enhancing farmers' market competitiveness (Hendrikse and Veerman, 2001).

2.2.1.4. Alleviating Technology Constraints. Public agricultural technology extension resources are constrained, which poses significant challenges for most farmers in directly accessing technical support. Concurrently, with the ongoing advancement of agricultural science and technology, a continuous stream of new planting techniques is being introduced. Nevertheless, owing to their limited knowledge and skills, farmers encounter difficulties in mastering and applying these novel technologies (Caffaro et al., 2020).To acquire new technologies, farmers often have to participate in training courses, purchase technical materials, and undertake other related activities, all of which entail learning costs. Furthermore, even when farmers have successfully mastered new technologies, a variety of issues may emerge during their practical application. This is primarily due to a lack of experience and technical guidance, leading to suboptimal production efficiency and an increase in the costs associated with technology application (Peng et al., 2025). Agricultural production trusteeship can function as a channel for the transfer of human capital and knowledge capital, facilitating the integration of high-value-added technologies into the agricultural production process (Zhong et al., 2025). trusteeship service providers generally possess a professional technical team with in-depth knowledge and extensive practical experience in agricultural production technologies. These teams are capable of offering comprehensive technical support to farmers, encompassing the promotion and application of new technologies as well as providing technical guidance throughout the production process.By purchasing trusteeship services, farmers are relieved of the burden of bearing the costs of learning new technologies on their own. Moreover, they no longer need to be concerned about unresolved issues during technology application. This streamlined approach enhances the convenience of technology acquisition and utilization, ultimately contributing to improved agricultural business returns and profitability (Baiyegunhi et al., 2019).

## 2.2.2. Farmland scale management

Smith (2002) put forward the established "Smith's Theorem"as early as 1776. The central proposition of this theorem is that the division of labor constitutes the wellspring of economic growth, yet the degree of division of labor is restricted by the market's scope. Youno (1928) built upon "Smith's Theorem," contending that the level of division of labor is contingent upon the market scope, and that these two factors interact with and are mutually reliant on each other. Consequently, "Young's Theorem" was formulated, which unveils the self - fulfilling mechanism of increasing returns through the concept that "the division of labor generally hinges on the division of labor." In contemporary society, knowledge capital is widely dispersed, rendering it challenging to concentrate resources and technologies within a single individual. The diverse preferences and ability differentials among behavioral agents in resource utilization further underscore individual heterogeneity, thereby giving rise to individuals with unique comparative advantages (Han et al., 2024). It is evident that the division of labor lays the groundwork for specialization, and specialization is the inevitable consequence of the division of labor. The division of labor can be classified into vertical division of labor and horizontal division of labor. Correspondingly, specialization can also be divided into vertical - division - of - labor - based specialization and horizontal - division - of - labor - based specialization.When applied to the agricultural sector, farmers production and management operations also manifest two forms of specialization: vertical - division - of - labor - based specialization and horizontal - division - of - labor - based specialization. On the one hand, the entire agricultural production process is segmented into several distinct stages. Given their limited resources, individual farmers will, based on their comparative advantages and the objective of profit maximization, retain the stages at which they are most proficient. For certain production stages, the cost of farmers producing them independently may exceed that of directly trusteeship these stages to service providers. In such scenarios, farmers are compelled to adopt an indirect production approach by outsourcing these stages and retaining only a few production stages, a practice known as vertical specialization in production (Deng et al., 2020).On the other hand, when a farmer's operational scale reaches a certain threshold, economies of scale make it more lucrative for the farmer to focus on agricultural activities or the production of specific crops, rather than engaging in a wide range of agricultural products or activities. At this juncture, farmers tend to concentrate exclusively on agricultural activities or the production of specific crops, a phenomenon referred to as horizontal specialization (Zhang et al., 2025).

Drawing inspiration from the separable farmer production model proposed by Bardhan and Udry (1999) and Deininger and Jin (2008), this study examines how agricultural production trusteeship can induce division - of - labor effects through large - scale farmland management, resulting in contiguous specialization and facilitating farmers' high - quality rice cultivation.The availability of agricultural production trusteeship services does not necessarily translate into farmers' actual input. Only when farmers procure agricultural production trusteeship services do these services truly exert an influence on their production and management activities. We can conceptualize agricultural production trusteeship and farmland as two distinct types of inputs that farmers utilize in agricultural production. The impact of agricultural production trusteeship on large - scale farmland management can be reframed as farmers' decision - making regarding the allocation of these two factors. ## 2.2.3. Factor constraints alleviation and farmland scale management

Division of labor theory has not given adequate attention to the transaction costs embedded in the division of labor itself. Coase (1960) noted that the reason firms can supplant market price mechanisms in coordinating the division of labor is that a firm's internal management costs may be lower than market transaction costs. Consequently, a firm's economies of scale are jointly determined by its external (market) transaction costs and internal management costs. The division of labor and its extent can be elucidated by transaction costs.In scenarios characterized by a low degree of specialization, producing a product inevitably involves numerous components or process stages that have not attained their optimal production scales. Hence, individuals endeavor to promote specialized production to reap the economies of scale derived from the optimal scales of these components or process stages. When agricultural technological advancements facilitate the separation of certain production links, and the optimal scales of these links exceed the scales determined by the overall farm size, individuals will persistently pursue the separation of these links. However, production technology factors can only account for people's motivation to promote specialization. The true determinant of whether specialization can be realized lies in the capacity to reduce transaction costs. In fact, it is transaction costs that delineate the boundaries of the division of labor and also those of agricultural family operations.Horizontal and vertical divisions of labor in the agricultural sector are not mutually independent but interrelated, exhibiting a division-of-labor integration effect. This is because the formation of the division of labor is not unconditional, it is constrained by market capacity (Smith, 2002). Market capacity is manifested through the transaction frequency in vertical division of labor and the transaction density in horizontal division of labor (Luo, 2017). Different production stages of the same crop require varying frequencies of labor within a production cycle. A high labor frequency implies a high transaction frequency, which in turn indicates a larger market capacity, thereby alleviating the constraints imposed by market capacity on the division of labor (Luo, 2017). When farmers achieve horizontal division-of-labor specialized production through farmland scale management, and the planting area of a single crop by an individual farmer reaches the maximum of their managed area, the emergence of service demands for a particular production link can increase the transaction density of services in that link and expand the market capacity for services in that link (Foster and Rosenzweig, 2022).

## 3. Data source, variables and model selection

## 3.1. Data source

The research data presented in this paper are derived from the "Double Hundred and Double Thousand" survey project, which was carried out from June to July 2023 across 216 villages in 72 townships within 24 counties (cities) of Jiangxi Province. The selection of Jiangxi Province as the research site is primarily grounded in the following three considerations: First, Jiangxi Province is endowed with superior natural conditions and serves as a significant rice-producing region in southeastern China. It features a warm and humid climate, abundant precipitation, and fertile soil. As a vital commodity grain base in China, it has long been one of the two provinces supplying commodity grain to the entire nation, playing a pivotal role in safeguarding China's national food security.Second, Jiangxi Province demonstrates both typicality and uniqueness in the cultivation of high-quality rice. On the one hand, similar to most other rice-growing regions, Jiangxi Province confronts common challenges in transitioning to high-quality production, such as the lag in developing high-quality rice varieties and the poor integration of the industrial chain. On the other hand, it also distinguishes itself from other regions. For example, the climatic conditions in some ricegrowing areas in northern China differ markedly from those in Jiangxi Province, which provides a unique climate highly favorable for the growth of a diverse array of high-quality rice varieties. Furthermore, compared to some economically developed regions in southern China where agricultural modernization is more advanced, there are significant gaps in terms of capital investment and technological application in high-quality rice cultivation between these regions and Jiangxi Province, making it difficult to directly replicate and promote their development paths. Therefore, studying high-quality rice cultivation in Jiangxi Province enables the summarization of strategies with broad applicability. This not only offers practical experiences that can be directly referenced for the upgrading of the high-quality rice industry in other similar ecological zones in China and even globally but also provides region-specific solutions to address common challenges faced by different rice-growing regions.Third, with the substantial out-migration of rural labor in China, farmers' rice cultivation is significantly influenced by trusteeship services, with agricultural production trusteeship being a representative example (Zhang et al., 2025). Jiangxi Province has exhibited a certain degree of continuity and targetedness in its agricultural support policies, including those related to agricultural production trusteeship, which have positive implications for promoting the high-quality transformation of the rice industry. Taking Jiangxi Province as the research object allows for a more precise identification of the current development status and existing problems of agricultural production trusteeship.

## 3.2.1. Explained variable

Drawing on the research by Mao et al. If they had adopted such varieties, further inquiries were made about their cultivation scale.To tackle the issue where a small number of farmers had difficulty accurately determining whether the rice varieties they planted were of high quality, the research team first provided training for the surveyors on rice variety identification during the preliminary stage. During the actual survey, the surveyors made comprehensive judgments based on practical factors, such as the labels on farmers' rice varieties, variety prices, and seed usage amounts.

## 3.2.2. Explanatory variables

Drawing on the research conducted by Zhong et al. (2025) and Zhang et al. (2025), this study employs whether farmers participate in "agricultural production trusteeship(APT)" as the explanatory variable. If farmers are involved in any one or more of the diverse stages of agricultural production trusteeship, encompassing pre-production unified seedling cultivation and supply trusteeship(PUST), mid-production plowing and harvesting trusteeship(MPHT), mid-production joint planting and prevention trusteeship(MPPT), and post-production production-marketing linkage trusteeship(PPLT), they are deemed to be participating in agricultural production trusteeship and are assigned a value of 1; otherwise, a value of 0 is assigned.The "number of agricultural production trusteeship(NATP)" in which farmers participate is adopted as an alternative variable for "agricultural production trusteeship" to carry out robustness tests. A greater number of agricultural production trusteeship services that farmers engage in indicates a higher degree of agricultural production trusteeship.This paper further delves into an analysis of agricultural production trusteeship across different stages and models. Specifically, if farmers participate in agricultural production trusteeship at any given stage, that particular stage is assigned a value of 1; otherwise, it is assigned a value of 0. If farmers are involved in agricultural production trusteeship at two or three stages, it is classified as a semi-trusteeship(ST) model and is assigned a value of 1; otherwise, a value of 0 is given. If farmers participate in agricultural production trusteeship at all stages, it is considered a full-trusteeship (FT) model and is assigned a value of 1; otherwise, a value of 0 is assigned.As illustrated in Fig. 5 below, a preliminary observation can be drawn: participation in agricultural production trusteeship can substantially enhance the scale of farmers' high-quality rice cultivation. Regarding different trusteeship models, the ST model results in a significantly larger increase compared to the FT model.

## 3.2.5. Instrumental variables

There may exist an endogeneity issue of reciprocal causation between agricultural production trusteeship and farmers' high - quality rice cultivation. That is, farmers who cultivate high - quality rice are more likely to have their families participate in agricultural production trusteeship services. In light of this, drawing on the research of Zhong et al. (2025), this paper selects three instrumental variables: "village - level trusteeship service price(VSP)", "township - level number of trusteeship organizations(TNO)", "city-level trusteeship task target (CTT)". Theoretically, in terms of relevance, the trusteeship service price directly affects the cost for farmers to participate in trusteeship. The lower the price, the stronger the demand and willingness of farmers to engage in trusteeship. The number of trusteeship organizations reflects the service supply capacity. The more organizations there are, the higher the service accessibility, which promotes farmers' participation in trusteeship. The trusteeship task target is a core indicator at the policy level to drive trusteeship services. When the target is higher, local governments usually provide more subsidies and strengthen organizational cultivation, thereby encouraging farmers to participate in trusteeship. Thus, the above - mentioned instrumental variables meet the requirement of relevance. From the perspective of exogeneity, the trusteeship service price is the result of the combined influence of market factors. The development of the number of trusteeship organizations is closely related to external factors such as policy support, resource endowments, and market demand. The trusteeship task target is formulated by municipal governments based on regional agricultural development plans, fiscal budgets, and higher - level policy requirements, which is a top - down exogenous policy. Therefore, all three are unrelated to the planting decisions of individual farmers, satisfying the exogeneity requirement of instrumental variables. The paper will further conduct empirical tests on the rationality of these instrumental variables later. ## 4. Empirical results and analysis

## 4.1. Benchmark regression result

We utilized Equation (15) to investigate the impact of agricultural production trusteeship(APT) on farmers' high-quality rice cultivation (HRC). Columns (1) to (4) in Table 2 display the estimation results derived from the Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) model, incorporating sequentially household head individual characteristics (IHC), family and operational characteristics(FOC), market and village characteristics (MVC). Thus, Hypothesis H1 is validated.

## 4.2.3. Robustness test based on endogenous switching regression model

The Endogenous Switching Model is well - suited for examining the impacts of choice decisions that account for sample selection, self - selection, and the interactions between the adoption decision and other covariates that influence the outcome equation (Shi et al., 2025). Consequently, this study further utilizes the Endogenous Switching Model to concurrently reduce the biases caused by sample selection and self - selection in the benchmark regression estimates.

(1) We construct an estimation equation for the decision to opt for agricultural production trusteeship(APT) and a separate one for the outcome related to farmers' high - quality rice cultivation (HRC). As presented in Table 7, the Wald test for the model's goodness - of - fit is statistically significant at the 1 % level, which validates the suitability of employing the Endogenous Switching Model. Both lnSp and LnSnp are statistically significant at the 1 % level, indicating the existence of selection bias. The positive error correlation coefficient rp suggests that farmers who participate in agricultural production trusteeship cultivate high - quality rice on a larger scale compared to the average farmer. In contrast, the negative error correlation coefficient rnp implies that farmers who do not participate in agricultural production trusteeship cultivate high - quality rice on a smaller scale relative to the average farmer.

(2) We have constructed a counterfactual framework to analyze farmers' high - quality rice cultivation(HRC), taking into account whether they participate in agricultural production trusteeship (APT). As shown in Table 8, the average treatment effect of farmers' participation in agricultural production trusteeship on their high - quality rice cultivation exhibits a significantly positive impact at the 1 % statistical significance level.Under the counterfactual assumptions, had farmers who actually engage in APT refrained from doing so, their high - quality rice cultivation would have dropped by 1.130, equivalent to a 77.39 % decline. On the contrary, if farmers who currently do not participate in APT were to do so, their high - quality rice cultivation would have risen by 0.133, marking a 34.19 % increase. This clearly illustrates that farmers' participation in APT can substantially boost their high - quality rice cultivation, thus confirming the robustness of the research findings presented in this paper.

## 4.2.4. Robustness test based on the change of measurement mode of explanatory variables

Drawing on the prior approach of using "whether to participate in agricultural production trusteeship" as a proxy variable for agricultural production trusteeship, this study adopts "the number of agricultural production trusteeship(NAPT)" as an alternative variable. It investigates whether, as farmers' participation intensity in agricultural production trusteeship rises, the scale of their high - quality rice cultivation(HRC) also grows.As illustrated in Table 9, all instrumental variable tests have successfully met the required criteria. No matter which method is employed - the OLS regression, the Heckman two - stage model, or the 2SLS model - the extent of agricultural production trusteeship participation demonstrates a significantly positive effect on farmers' high - quality rice cultivation at the 1 % significance level. This suggests that as farmers become more deeply involved in agricultural production trusteeship, their high - quality rice cultivation will correspondingly increase, thereby further reinforcing the robustness of the conclusions drawn in this paper.

## 6. Further analysis

## 6.1. Heterogeneity of different trusteeship stages

The preceding analysis has illustrated the positive impact of agricultural production trusteeship(APT) on farmers' high-quality rice cultivation(HRC), demonstrating that farmers' participation and deeper engagement in such trusteeship services can substantially motivate them to cultivate high-quality rice. However, trusteeship services at different stages of rice production exhibit significant disparities in terms of functional attributes, implementation methods, and impacts: preproduction unified seedling cultivation and supply trusteeship(PUST) involves the screening, breeding, and unified provision of high-quality rice seeds, directly influencing the quality foundation at the initial stage of cultivation; mid-production plowing and harvesting trusteeship (MPHT), mid-production joint planting and prevention trusteeship (MPPT) encompasse efficient tillage and harvesting, standardized production, and field management during the cultivation process, determining the output efficiency and growth quality of high-quality rice; post-production production-marketing linkage trusteeship(PPLT) for production-marketing linkage focuses on market connection, brand building, and value realization of high-quality rice, affecting farmers' final returns and sustained motivation for cultivating high-quality rice. Therefore, conducting analyses solely from the perspective of participation or the degree of participation makes it challenging to accurately identify the differences in the impact of each stage on farmers' highquality rice cultivation (Mi et al., 2020). Further research on a stage-by-stage basis holds significant practical necessity.

## 6.2. Heterogeneity of different trusteeship models

Agricultural production trusteeship(APT) can be categorized into two distinct models: the semi-trusteeship(ST) model and the fulltrusteeship(FT) model. The semi-trusteeship model grants farmers a certain level of autonomy, empowering them to flexibly choose the agricultural production links they intend to outsource based on their actual situations. In contrast, the full-trusteeship model places greater emphasis on the comprehensive orchestration by professional service organizations, providing farmers with a "one-stop" solution for highquality rice cultivation.Different models of agricultural production trusteeship play unique roles in integrating agricultural resources and boosting production efficiency. A comprehensive investigation into these two trusteeship models, coupled with a clear understanding of their respective advantages and disadvantages in high-quality rice cultivation, facilitates the development of more precise and effective supportive policies. It also guides agricultural service organizations in optimizing their service portfolios and enhancing service quality.

This study has revealed the structural differences in the impact of agricultural production trusteeship(APT) on farmers' high-quality rice cultivation(HRC) from the perspectives of trusteeship stages and trusteeship models. However, as the primary decision-makers in high-quality rice cultivation, farmers' individual characteristics and external support conditions also significantly influence their planting behaviors. Specifically, farmers' risk attitude and their participation in variety training programs can lead to varying degrees of responsiveness to agricultural production trusteeship due to group differences, thereby affecting the outcomes of high-quality rice cultivation.

This can be attributed to the following reasons: risk-averse farmers tend to make conservative decisions. Even when agricultural production trusteeship services are available, they are reluctant to substantially increase their high-quality rice cultivation due to concerns about the risks associated with new varieties and technologies. In contrast, risk-neutral and risk-preference farmers are more appreciative of the advantages brought by trusteeship services, such as technical guidance and market linkage. Moreover, risk-preference farmers are relatively more inclined to try new things and make full use of trusteeship services to reduce production uncertainties, thus being more willing to cultivate high-quality rice (Fu et al., 2025).

## 7. Conclusion

## 7.1. Conclusion and discussion

Firstly, this study investigates the impact of agricultural production trusteeship on farmers' high-quality rice cultivation, thereby enriching the existing body of evidence concerning the factors influencing farmers' adoption of high-quality varieties. In developing countries across Central Europe, Asia, and Africa, including Ethiopia, Malawi, and the Congo, smallholder farmers have long served as the backbone of agricultural production (Adamopoulos and Restuccia, 2014; Rada and Fuglie, 2019). This demographic generally confronts challenges such as insufficient agricultural resources and constrained production scales (Picazo-Tadeo and Reig-Martinez, 2006). To maintain their livelihoods, they are frequently compelled to adopt an extensive production mode marked by high input and low output, which renders it arduous for them to fulfill the sustainability and high-quality requirements of modern agriculture. Smallholder farmers are devoid of both the technical support necessary for introducing high-quality varieties and the capacity to shoulder the risks associated with trial and error, resulting in their agricultural production being ensnared in a cycle of "low quality and low efficiency".As a novel form of agricultural division of labor, agricultural production trusteeship can effectively introduce high-quality rice varieties. Simultaneously, by integrating dispersed production factors through specialized services, it aids smallholder farmers in surmounting resource and scale limitations, enabling them to transition from "extensive subsistence" to "high-quality development" (Picazo-Tadeo and Reig-Martinez, 2006). This study reveals that farmers' engagement in agricultural production trusteeship significantly boosts their high-quality rice cultivation, with those participating in such trusteeship being more inclined to grow high-quality rice. This finding addresses a gap in existing research regarding the influence of service promotion models, typified by agricultural production trusteeship, on farmers adoption of high-quality varieties. Additionally, it offers an optional and efficacious pathway for promoting high-quality varieties and facilitating the high-quality transformation of agriculture in developing countries.

Secondly, this paper delves into the mechanisms by which agricultural production trusteeship influences farmers' high-quality rice cultivation. On the one hand, agricultural production trusteeship positively impacts farmers' high-quality rice cultivation by utilizing substitution effects to alleviate factor constraints and reduce transaction costs. Under the traditional agricultural production model, farmers frequently face constraints stemming from multiple factors, including labor, machinery, information, and technology. The scarcity or inefficient allocation of these factors severely impedes the high-quality rice cultivation. As an innovative form of agricultural management, agricultural production trusteeship effectively alleviates farmers' factor constraints during the production process through various approaches, thereby stimulating their enthusiasm and motivation for cultivating high-quality rice.On the other hand, agricultural production trusteeship also fosters farmland scale management and facilitates contiguous and specialized farming by harnessing the effects of division of labor, further propelling farmers' high-quality rice cultivation. By integrating fragmented farmland resources, agricultural production trusteeship enables centralized and contiguous land management, promoting the division and specialization of agricultural production. This allows farmers to concentrate more on the core aspects of rice cultivation, enhancing the degree of specialization in farming.Crucially, agricultural production trusteeship establishes a virtuous circle and synergistic effect between factor constraints alleviation and farmland scale management. By easing factor constraints, agricultural production trusteeship creates the necessary conditions and guarantees for large-scale farmland management. In turn, the realization of large-scale farmland management expands market capacity, further facilitating the development of service-scale management and accelerating the concentration and optimal allocation of production factors. This bidirectional, mutually reinforcing relationship enables agricultural production trusteeship to play a more prominent role in promoting farmers' high-quality rice cultivation. This significant finding not only reveals the unique advantages and potential of agricultural production trusteeship in promoting high-quality rice cultivation among farmers but also convincingly demonstrates that farmland scale management and service scale management are not mutually exclusive or isolated; instead, they can be integrated and mutually reinforcing. This finding is consistent with the research conclusions of Yin et al. (2024). The theoretical significance of this paper lies in its integration of the "Smith Theorem," the "Young Theorem," and transaction cost theory, as well as its combination of vertical and horizontal division-of-labor theories. This approach enhances the understanding of the deepening of division of labor, transaction costs, and their interrelationships.

Finally, this study reveals that agricultural production trusteeship services at different stages and under different models have varying impacts on farmers' high-quality rice cultivation. Moreover, the influence of agricultural production trusteeship on farmers' high-quality rice cultivation differs based on farmers' individual risk attitude characteristics and whether they have participated in variety training as part of external support conditions.Specifically, first, although all stages of agricultural production trusteeship significantly affect farmers' highquality rice cultivation, there are notable differences in the extent of their impacts, with the following order of effectiveness:pre-production unified seedling cultivation and supply trusteeship > mid-production joint planting and prevention trusteeship > post-production productionmarketing linkage trusteeship > mid-production plowing and harvesting trusteeships. Second, the semi-trusteeship model of agricultural production trusteeship has a more pronounced impact on farmers' highquality rice cultivation compared to the full-trusteeship model. Third, compared to risk-averse farmers, agricultural production trusteeship has a more significant impact on risk-neutral farmers' high-quality rice cultivation. Fourth, in terms of variety training participation, agricultural production trusteeship has a more significant impact on farmers who have participated in variety training compared to those who have not.Therefore, this paper argues that it is essential not only to clarify the differences in the impact effects of agricultural production trusteeship at different stages and under different models but also to tailor the promotion of agricultural production trusteeship to promote high-quality rice cultivation based on actual needs, with emphasis on specific areas as appropriate. Additionally, it is necessary to consider the differences in impact effects based on farmers' risk preferences and variety training participation. In advancing agricultural production trusteeship, it is crucial to take into account farmers' individual characteristics and external support conditions. From a practical application perspective, this study provides a solid policy basis for countries to develop agricultural production trusteeship in a localized manner to promote farmers' high-quality rice cultivation.

## 7.2. Policy recommendation

Based on the aforementioned research findings, this paper draws the following implications:

Firstly, it is of paramount importance to intensify support for the development of agricultural production trusteeship. On the one hand, efforts should be made to vigorously foster a diverse array of agricultural production trusteeship organizations. This entails implementing preferential policies related to agricultural production trusteeship and adopting measures such as government procurement, leading by exemplary cases, project promotion, and resource integration. These measures aim to enhance the endogenous development momentum and service supply capacity of these organizations, ensuring they can provide farmers with comprehensive, high-quality, and specialized trusteeship services. On the other hand, it is essential to improve farmers' access to agricultural production trusteeship services. This can be achieved by establishing convenient information service platforms and conducting promotional activities to alleviate farmers' concerns about trusteeship services and guide them to actively adopt such services, thereby creating favorable conditions for the full realization of the positive effects of agricultural production trusteeship services.

Secondly, there is a need to coordinate and advance the simultaneous development of service scale management and farmland scale management. It is imperative to elevate the organizational level of small-scale farmers by establishing farmer professional cooperatives, agricultural industrialization consortia, and other organizational forms to unite scattered small farmers into a community of shared interests. This will alleviate the factor constraints faced by small farmers in terms of labor, machinery, information, and technology, and promote the development of service-scale management. Simultaneously, it is crucial to further refine land transfer policies, establish and improve land transfer markets, standardize land transfer procedures, reduce transfer costs and risks, facilitate the concentration and contiguousness of farmland, and encourage small farmers with the intention of expanding their operational scale to acquire land, thereby creating conditions for scale management. Moreover, continuous innovation in the service models of trusteeship organizations should be encouraged to promote specialization in division of labor, foster a virtuous cycle between alleviating factor constraints and promoting farmland-scale management, and achieve deep integration of farmland and service-scale management, providing more robust guarantees for farmers' high-quality rice cultivation.

Thirdly, it is vital to scientifically optimize and precisely promote the distinctive development of agricultural production trusteeship. In the pre-production stage, emphasis should be placed on increasing support for unified seedling raising and supply trusteeship. This involves establishing high-quality seed source breeding bases and standardized seedling raising centers to ensure the supply of high-quality seed sources and the quality of standardized seedling raising. Meanwhile, based on local agricultural production characteristics and farmers' needs, diverse service forms such as "menu-style", "order-based", and full-process trusteeship should be selectively offered to meet the individualized needs of different farmers. For risk-averse farmers, guidance should be provided to help them reduce trial-and-error costs and minimize losses resulting from cultivation decision-making errors through trusteeship services. The coverage of variety training should be expanded, and farmers should be taught high-quality rice cultivation techniques and management experience through training sessions, on-site demonstrations, and technical guidance, enabling more farmers to master scientific cultivation methods and achieve more substantial benefits from highquality rice cultivation through trusteeship. Furthermore, it is essential to continuously improve the supervision mechanism for trusteeship services, establish a sound service quality evaluation system, and strengthen daily supervision and performance evaluation of trusteeship organizations to ensure service quality and safeguard farmers' legitimate rights and interests, enabling all farmers to leverage trusteeship to achieve efficient high-quality rice cultivation and promote agricultural efficiency and farmers' income growth.

## 7.3. Limitation

This study presents certain limitations that warrant attention and improvement in subsequent research endeavors.First, this study centers on conducting surveys and analyses within Jiangxi Province. Although Jiangxi Province holds a distinctive and representative position in highquality rice cultivation, notable disparities exist among different provinces and countries in terms of economic development levels, the promotion of high-quality rice varieties, climatic conditions, and agricultural policies. These discrepancies can significantly influence farmers' decisions regarding participation in agricultural production trusteeship, as well as their choices and enthusiasm for cultivating highquality rice varieties. Consequently, such variations may lead to divergent research findings across different regions. To address this issue, future research should broaden its geographical scope to encompass multiple rice-growing areas characterized by diverse climatic zones and economic development levels. Furthermore, incorporating crossnational comparative case studies would be beneficial to enhance the universality and broad applicability of the research conclusions.Second, this study relies solely on cross-sectional data collected from farmers at a single time point. While this approach can, to a certain extent, reflect the contemporary situation of farmers' participation in agricultural production trusteeship and high-quality rice cultivation in Jiangxi Province at that specific moment, it is crucial to recognize that agricultural production trusteeship is a dynamic and evolving process. As time progresses, farmers' awareness and acceptance of trusteeship services may undergo changes, and the content, quality, and market prices of these services will also experience adjustments. Additionally, the effects and mechanisms of various complex factors influencing farmers' cultivation of high-quality rice vary across different time periods, potentially resulting in an inadequate and superficial understanding of certain phenomena and issues. To gain a more in-depth and comprehensive understanding, future research should utilize longitudinal data spanning multiple years to analyze the dynamic nature of farmers' participation in agricultural production trusteeship and its long-term impacts on highquality rice cultivation.
