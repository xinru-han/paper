# The impact of mechanization on crop production in China

Fangbin Qiao

China Economics and Management Academy, Central University of Finance and Economics, Beijing, China

## ABSTRACT

The rapid development of mechanization has been well reported, but its impact on China's agricultural production remains unclear. This study first uses a theoretical model to analyse the impact of mechanization on sown areas of different crops. The results show that mechanization has a positive impact on grain crops and a negative impact on non-grain crops. Then using nationally representative provincial-level panel data, the empirical analyses confirm these results. Thus, the sown areas of cotton, oil and sugar crops will be reduced by more than 20% within five years. Consequently, China is expected to depend on international markets to meet the domestic demand for cotton, oil and sugar in the future. Even though mechanization has a positive impact on the sown areas of grain crops, a reduction in the diversity of grain production poses a new threat to the stability of China's grain production and food security.

## I. Introduction

Mechanization plays a key role in a country's transition from traditional labour-intensive production to a modern high-efficiency, capital-intensive production mode (Ruttan 2001; Pingali 2007; Otsuka 2013; Qiu and Luo 2021; Ma, Zhu, and Zhou 2022). As the world's largest developing country, China is experiencing this transition. Due to land fragmentation and smaller farm sizes caused by the implementation of the household responsibility system at the end of 1970s, agricultural mechanization was practically stagnant before the early 2000s (Qiao and Yao 2015). However, rapidly rising wage rates and sizable subsidies from the government have accelerated the development of mechanization since then (Yang et al. 2013; Qiao 2017). As a result, the comprehensive mechanization rate of crop cultivating, sowing and harvesting in China increased from less than one-third in 2003 to more than two-thirds in 2018 (MOA 2015, 2019). According to the Ministry of Agriculture (2021), the comprehensive mechanization rate will increase to 71% for all crops and to 84% for major grain crops in 2020.¹

Mechanization has had important implications for China's agricultural production. As overall wages increased, more farmers left their villages and migrated to cities. The rural-urban migration of 2017 was 286.5 million, which was about half the rural population (National Bureau of Statistics ofChina - hereafter NBSC 2018a, 2018b). However, due to the wide use of machines, the total sown area of all crops increased from 156.32 million hectare (ha) in 2000 to 166.33 million ha in 2017 (NBSC 2018b). More importantly, the grain production continually increased from 462.18 million tons in 2000 to 661.61 million tons in 2017 - an increase of 43.15% (NBSC 2018b). Thus the increase of grain production is companioned by the development of mechanization. The positive impact of mechanization on grain production has been well documented in China (Qing et al. 2019; Zheng, Ma, and Zhou 2021) and other developing countries, such as India and Bangladesh (Begum et al. 2018; Kandel et al. 2021).

However, previous studies showed that mechanization has had a negative impact on some nongrain crops. For example, using panel data collected in China's major cotton production provinces, Qiao (2017) showed that mechanization has led to a reduction in the sown area of cotton.

However, since Qiao (2017) focused on a cotton producing region, it is unclear whether the negative impact of mechanization on other non-grain crops would hold in non-cotton producing regions. So the impact of mechanization on other non-grain crops – such as oil and sugar crops – is unclear.

This study seeks to answer these questions. Specifically, I seek to quantify the impact of mechanization on the production of grain crops and non-grain crops. Based on theory, this study will first identify how mechanization has affected crop structure in China. Then, using nationally representative panel data and empirical models, I will quantitatively measure the contribution of mechanization on the sown areas of grain crop and the sown areas of non-grain crops in China, respectively. The empirical results suggest that due to the development of mechanization, the sown area of grain will increase by 17.74% within five years, while the annual reduction of the sown areas of non-grain crops will be approximately 3.53%.

China's grain production and its structural changes are not only important domestic issues, but also world issues (Brown 1995). With more than 1.4 billion people, China is the world's most populous country. As a result, China's shifts in agriculture have had a major impact on global imports and exports. For example, it seems that soybean has become China's important weapon during the trade war between China and the United States, the largest exporter. On the other hand, the reduction of non-grain crops (i.e. cotton, sugar crops and oil crops) might provide more opportunity for major exporters in the world market.

The key idea of this paper is related to an important line of thought in the literature. In recent years, rising wages are challenging many Asian countries in which most farmers are smallholders (Otsuka 2013). Mechanization – a labouring-saving production method – developed rapidly (Foster and Rosenzweig 2010, 2011; Diao et al. 2014; Wang, Yamauchi, and Huang 2015). At the same time, institutional change (such as land and machine rental markets) which was first introduced by Hicks (1932) and Hayami and Ruttan (1985) occurred (Yamauchi 2016). In this context, I examine the impact on different crops. As rising wages and mechanization caused the change of relative advantages of different crops, the production structure – especially of the grain crop – may have changed.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows: In the next section, I develop a theoretical model and analytically discuss the impact of rising wages on mechanization and the impact of mechanization on grain crops and non-grain crops. In the third section, I apply econometric models to nationally representative panel data to test whether the analytical results conform to the theoretical model; further, I quantitatively measure the magnitude of the impact of a rising wage rate and the impact of mechanization. The final section concludes.

## II. Theoretical model

I classified all the crops into grain crops (e.g. rice, wheat and corn) and non-grain crops (e.g. cotton, oil and sugar crops) for two reasons. First, grain crops have the largest sown area in each province in mainland China. Second, grain crops are major competitors of non-grain crops, in terms of land.

To establish the theoretical model, I assumed that a farmer maximized total family income by choosing up to three activities: farming the grain crops, farming the non-grain crops and/or engaging in off-farm work. I assumed that a farmer's labour inputs for the non-grain crops, the grain crops and the off-farm work were L1, L2 and L3, respectively. Assuming total labour input was L, I had L3 = L − L1 − L2. Assuming that the sown area of the non-grain crop was A1 and the total farm size was A, the sown area of the grain crops was A2 =A – A1. As discussed above, machines are widely used in all the stages of grain production, but not for non-grain crops. For simplicity, I assumed that a machine was used in the production of the grain crops but not in the production of the non-grain crops. Finally, I used a Cobb-Douglas production function for the non-grain crops and the grain crops, so I had ynon\_grain = Lq1 A1 and ygrain = Lα2(A − A1)δ2Mβ2. Here, α1 and δ1 represented the elasticities of labour and land in the production of non-grain crops, respectively. As is standard, I assumed that α1 >0, δ1 >0 and α1 + δ1 ≤1. Similarly, α2, β2 and δ2 represented the elasticities of labour, agricultural machinery and land used in grain production, respectively. I then assumed α2 >0, β2 >0, δ2 >0 and α2 + β2 + δ2 ≤1. Finally, since the non-grain crops were the labourintensive crops while the grain crops were the labour-extensive crops, the labour elasticity of the non-grain crops was greater than that of the grain crops; in other words, α1 > α2. Finally, M represented the machinery input.

I assumed p₁ and p2 were the output prices of the non-grain products and the grain products, respectively. The variable p₃ was the wage rate of the offfarm work, while P4 was the price of mechanical power. Thus, the farmer's total profit, π, can be written as:

## III. Empirical models and estimates

I empirically tested whether the two hypotheses from the theoretical analyses were correct. Then I quantitatively estimated the impact of rising wage rates on the development of mechanization and the impact of mechanization on the grain crops and non-grain crops. I will discuss my data sources as well as how I established the empirical models and estimates.

## Data, wage increase, mechanization and cotton-sown area

The data used in this study has three main sources: the China Statistical Yearbook (Zhongguo Tongji Nianjian), the All China Data Compilation of the Costs and Returns of Main Agricultural Products (Quanguo Nongchanpin Chengben Shouyi Ziliao Huibian) and the China Agricultural Machinery Industry Yearbook (Zhongguo Nongye Jixie Gongye Nianjian). Specifically, the data on national and provincial sown areas, the sown areas of grain crops, non-grain crops (cotton, oil and sugar crops), consumer price index (CPI) and the total power of agricultural machinery were from the China Statistical Yearbook (various years). The data on output prices, labour quantity and costs, machine costs, total production costs of grain crops and all non-grain crops were from the All China Data Compilation of the Costs and Returns of Main Agricultural Products. Finally, the provincial-level data on agricultural machinery purchase subsidies from the central government, the total membership of the agricultural machinery service organizations and the comprehensive mechanization rate of cultivating, sowing and harvesting are from the China Agricultural Machinery Industry Yearbook (various years). The basic characteristics of variables used in this study are summarized in Table 1. Figure 1 explicitly shows the crosssectional variability of the major variables used in this study (i.e. wage rate, the total power of agricultural machinery, the sown areas of grain crops and the sown areas of non-grain crops).

The estimated coefficient for P3 represented the impact of the wage rate on farmers' land allocation decisions.³ Note that rural-urban migrants may have had many off-farm work opportunities. However, provincial-level wage rates for these off-farm work opportunities were not available. In this study, the average wage of employees engaged in a construction unit was used for a couple of reasons. First, a job in a construction unit was one the most common for the migrant workers. The variable P was a vector of variables. The estimated coefficient of P measured the impact of output prices on sown areas. To avoid the impact of other factors (such as CPI) on output prices, I constructed a price ratio using the non-grain price divided by the grain price. Finally, because farmers adjusted their production based on the prior year's prices, the lagged value of the nongrain/grain price ratio was used in the estimation.

The variable Technology was used to capture the impact of technological progress. To promote agricultural production, the Ministry of Agriculture (MOA) introduced new crop varieties, and the varieties that have shown especially good performance in one special region. The numbers and the total areas of varieties promoted by MOA were obtained from the China Seed Industry Big Data Platform (Zhongguo Zhongye Dashuju Pingtai), which was published by the Bureau of Seed Management within the MOA (MOA 2017). This study used the number of varieties promoted by the MOA as the proxy variable for technological change. Like the P variable, a variety ratio using the number of non-grain crop varieties divided by the number of grain crop varieties was devised.

The variable Province was a vector of provincial dummies, while Year was a vector of year dummies. These two variables were used to study the impact of heterogeneity in provinces and across time. Adding these two variables makes the model a fixed-effect model. The purpose of this study was to measure the impact of mechanization of agricultural production. However, the comprehensive mechanization rate did not change significantly until the early 2000s. For this reason, the main models in this study used data from 2000 to 2016.

The instrumental variable was Subsidy. Subsidy measured the impact of the government subsidy on the agricultural machinery purchase. ## Empirical tests of hypotheses 1 and 2

The estimates of Equation (11) are shown in Table 2. The first and the fourth columns show the estimates for the total power of agricultural machinery. The second and third columns show the estimates for the sown areas of the grain crops and the non-grain crops. The last column shows the estimates of the share of the grain crops. I also tried to estimate Equation (11) in different scenarios, such as using a different time period or a different estimation strategy (i.e. using a long version of the data and adding an interaction term between power and the non-grain crops dummy in the estimation function). All these attempts yielded similar results which imply that the estimates were robust. I will discuss the estimates of the total power of agricultural machinery. Then I will discuss the estimates of the impact of mechanization on the grain and non-grain crops. In doing so, the two hypotheses will be tested.

As expected, the estimates of the mechanization function showed that both an increasing wage rate and government subsidies had positive impacts on the development of mechanization. As shown in the second row, the estimated coefficient of the wage rate variable was positive and statistically significant. In other words, the estimates confirmed the first hypothesis. That is, mechanization accelerated as the wage rate increased. Similarly, the positive estimated coefficient of the agricultural machinery purchase subsidy from the government was positive and statistically significant, indicating that the financial subsidy also contributed to the rapid adoption of machines in agricultural production. The impacts of subsidies and rising wage rates were consistent with previous studies (such as Qiao 2017).

The estimates for sown areas of grain crops and non-grain crops are shown in the second and third columns of Table 2. As expected, the estimated coefficient of the power of agricultural machinery was positive in the grain function and negative in the non-grain function (first row). Replacing the sown area of grain crop by the share of the grain crops yielded similar results (last column). On the other hand, the estimates of the share of the nongrain crops should be the same as that of the grain crops – except that the signs of the estimated coefficients of all the variables were the opposite. ## Impact of mechanization in different regions

As discussed above, Table 2 shows that as mechanization increased, the sown areas (and the share) of the grain crops increased, while the sown areas (and the share) of the non-grain crops decreased. However, the estimates might be misleading as the non-grain crops were a combined group including many different crops. Hence, I re-estimated Equation (11) by subdividing the non-grain crops into three categories: cotton, oil and sugar. Traditionally, they are the major non-grain crops in China. By doing so, I quantitatively estimated the magnitude of mechanization on different nongrain crops. The estimates can help to determine whether any of these crops will follow soybean's footsteps and become an agricultural product that is heavily dependent on international markets.

Grain crops were the major crops in every province, while cotton, oil and sugar crops were not planted in every province. For example, cotton was planted at notable levels in about 14 provinces, with the rest of the provinces almost have no cotton production. For this reason, I first estimated Equation (11) in China's major cotton production region. Similarly, I then estimated the equation in a major oil crop production region and a major sugar crop region, respectively. Finally, I calculated the price and cost of oil crops using a weighted average using the sown areas of peanut and rapeseed crops, while those using a weighted average of sugarcane and beet for the sugar crops.

The fourth through sixth columns show the estimates for a major oil crops production region, while the last three columns show the estimates for the major sugar crops production region. The estimates shown in Table 3 are very similar to that of Table 2. For example, the estimates showed that mechanization had a positive impact on the sown area of the grain crops and a negative impact on the sown area of cotton, oil and sugar crops (row 1). Also, both the estimated coefficients of the subsidy and the wage rates were positive, indicating that they contributed to the development of mechanization (rows 2 and 3). Replacing the sown area by the shares of the grain crops and cotton (or oil or sugar crops) yielded very similar results. For simplicity, the estimates of shares are not shown.

As discussed, the estimates of the first three columns of Table 3 used data from 14 major cotton production provinces: six provinces in the Yellow River valley (Hebei, Shandong, Tianjin, Shanxi, Shaanxi and Henan), six provinces in the Yangtze River valley (Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Anhui, Jiangxi, Hunan and Hubei), and two provinces in Northwest China (×Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region and Gansu Province). ## IV. Results and discussions

Mechanization plays a very important role in agriculture as well as in the entire economy of a developing country. More importantly, mechanization – especially in its early stages – has a different impact on the various crops. The study, both analytically and empirically, has shown that mechanization has had a positive impact on grain crops and a negative impact on non-grain crops. According to the results, if the growth rate of mechanization holds, within the next five years, the sown area of grain crops will increase by 16.20%, while the nongrain crops will decrease by 20.33%.

These results first confirm that mechanization contributes to China's grain production and food security. On one hand, as shown in Table 4, in all three production regions, the average annual impact of mechanization on the sown area of grain crops was 3.24%. That is, the development of mechanization contributed to the continued growth of grain crops during the past few decades. Hence, it seems that mechanization had contributed to the growth of grain production and food security.

However, it is worth noting that even though the total sown area of grain crops has continued to increase over the last decade, the diversity of grain crops has been reduced. According to national data, the ratio of the sown areas of the three major grain crops (i.e. rice, wheat and corn) to that of the total grain crops increased from 69% in 1978 to 83% in 2016 (NBSC various years). Even though the change in the share seems small in terms of magnitude, it implies a significant change for other grain crops. For example, the sown area of millet decreased from 42.71 million ha to 8.57 million from 1978 to 2016; that is, the sown area of millet decreased by 80%. The sown area of sorghum experienced a similar decrease during the same time period. For barley, the sown area decreased from 9.14 million ha to 4.29 million ha from 2002 to 2016. Due to issues with data availability, I could not quantitatively estimate the impact of mechanization on these grain crops using econometric models. However, like non-grain crops, these grain crops are labour-intensive and/or machine-extensive crops. Hence, it is reasonable to believe that mechanization might be negatively associated with the sown area of these grain crops.

Thus, even though mechanization has had a positive impact on the total sown area of grains, it has had a negative impact on the diversity of the grains. It should be noted that the reduction of diversity in grain production might threaten the stability of grain production and food security. For example, a weather disaster – such as a flood, drought or pest infestation – might have a more severe impact on the three major grain crops than on other grain crops. Consequently, grain production might be seriously affected. In this sense, the impact of mechanization on the stability of grain production and food security also deserves attention.

Second, results of this study show that mechanization might threaten the production of non-grain crops. As shown in Table 4, in the regions which produced all three non-grain crops (cotton, oil and sugar crops), the average impact of mechanization on the sown area of non-grain crops is 4.07%. That is, the non-grain crops will decrease by more than one-third in 10 years. The predictions shown in Table 4 comports with the national data that shows that the reduction of bast fibre was even more severe. According to the NBSC (various years), the production of bast fibre was 262 tons in 2016, which was only 5.89% of the 4,448 tons produced in 1985. Hence, if the reduction of the non-grain crops continues, some crops might be severely affected. And if they follow in the footsteps of soybeans, these non-grain crops might become heavily dependent on international markets.

## V. Conclusions and policy implications

China is experiencing the structural transformation, the movement of workers from low-productivity to higher-productivity, through which agriculture contributes to economy wide productivity growth (Fan et al. 2003; Zhang and Diao 2020). During the transformation, millions of labours leave rural areas and find non-farm work in cities (Cai 2018). Rural-urban migration necessitates the widely use of agricultural machinery, which is an important substitute for labour input (Qiao 2017; Zheng et al. 2022). The impact of mechanization and its determining factors had been well documented (Feng et al. 2010; Li 2018; Shi, Paudel, and Chen 2021; Jetté-Nantel, Hu, and Liu 2020). This study shows the heterogeneous impact of mechanization on grain crops and non-grain crops.

To encourage the development of mechanization, further policies should address two issues. First, the heterogeneity of mechanization between grain crops and non-grain crops. This study shows that heterogeneity mechanization adoption rates led to an increase of sown area of grain crops and a reduction of the sown area of non-grain crops. It is expected this situation will continue as more farmers will migrate to cities. To reverse the situation, special policies are needed to accelerate the development of mechanization for non-grain crops. Due to the small sown area and land fragmentation, investing on agricultural machinery for non-grain crops might not be economical for both private firms and farmers. Hence, providing financial subsidies for purchasing agricultural machines, is a useful way to accelerate mechanization for non-grain crops.

Second, further policy should address the heterogeneity of mechanization among farmers and regions. Mechanization adoption rates of farmers with different farm size vary significantly, which leads to different impact on the productivity and farmers' activities (Qiu, Choy, and Luo 2022; Ma, Zhu, and Zhou 2022; Qiu and Luo 2021; Wang et al. 2020). Consequently, farmers might choose to abandon the land that is not appropriate for machines or less profitable (Qiu et al. 2021). Similarly, due to a great variety of geological structures, topography and other factors, the heterogeneity of the mechanization adoption rates exists among regions, which has important impact on agriculture and the regional economy (Zhang, Yang, and Thomas 2017). Hence, further policy should also enhance land consolidation via land rental market. In addition, especially in mountain areas, developing scale and topography appropriate farm machines can help promote agricultural mechanization and reduce cropland abandonment.
