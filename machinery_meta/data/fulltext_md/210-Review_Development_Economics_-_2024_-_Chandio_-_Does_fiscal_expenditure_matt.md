## Abstract

Rice is a staple food for vast population of China and it remarkably contributes toward China's food security. Several fiscal programs have been implemented by Chinese central government for sustainable agriculture production. In this study, we examine the long-run influence of agricultural public investment (API) and (agro)chemical inputs use (i.e., fertilizer and pesticide) on rice yield in main rice-producing provinces of China. Second-generation panel unit root tests, Westerlund error correction model cointegration test, Augmented-Mean Group (AMG) and Common Correlated Effect Mean Group (CCEMG) estimation techniques have been used in this article. The cointegration test results reveal strong evidence of the existence of the long-run co-integrating link between the considered series. The results of the AMG and CCEMG methods show that (i) API, (ii) fertilizer use and (iii) power consumption for agricultural machinery positively and significantly impact rice productivity,

whereas pesticide usage affects it negatively. Panel quantile regression showed that API, fertilizer use, power consumption, and sown area are significant and positive at 0.10–0.90 quantiles. The results also indicate that pesticide usage increases rice production at 0.10-0.30 quantiles but decreases rice production at 0.40-0.90 quantile levels. Based on these findings, we derive pertinent implications for China's food security.

## 1  INTRODUCTION

Sustainable Development Goal #2 (Zero hunger) aims to eradicate world hunger, establish food sanctuary and better nutrition, and endorse sustainable development in the agriculture sector (Mughal & Fontan Sers, 2020). This multidimensional objective consists of three interdependent sub-objectives: eradicating hunger and enhancing nutrition, attaining food security by boosting production and income, and promoting sustainable agriculture across countries (Galabada, 2022; Ma et al., 2023). According to estimates, over 870 million people worldwide suffer from chronic malnutrition, with the majority living in developing countries where rice is strongly linked to political and economic stability as well as food security (Muthayya et al., 2014).

Food security in China is vital for the nation's economic development and social stability, as well as for global food security (G. He et al., 2019; Huan et al., 2022; Li et al., 2014). In the last 30 years, China has remarkably guaranteed food security (Zhu, 2004); however, such fiat was achieved at the cost of high land, water, fertilizers, and pesticides utilization, resulting in carbon emissions and climatic changes. Equally, for a country with the size and population of China, it is also impossible to depend on food imports from the global market to ensure enough food for everyone (Q. He et al., 2024; Ma et al., 2023; Zhuo et al., 2023). These factors exacerbated the long-term difficulty of securing China's food security (Huan et al., 2022).

In China's agricultural sector, government expenditure is contingent on the materials necessary to farmers to maintain yearly growth, such as high-quality pesticides and an efficient water irrigation system to improve crop yields (Gao et al., 2022). In crop-specific cases, rice cultivation is an important part of China's agricultural industry because rice is the primary staple food for more than 65% of the Chinese. In 2018, China farmed 30.19 million hectares of rice and produced over 148.5 million metric tons, making it the world's leading rice producer (see Figure 1).

Moreover, the trend of rice production in major rice-producing provinces of China is depicted in Figure S1. Nonetheless, in recent years, concerns about environmental sustainability, food security, and socioeconomic well-being have led the Chinese government to adopt and implement various pro-agricultural policies and boost public green investment to raise farmers' income and enhance sustainable food production capacity (Xu et al., 2011). The present study emphases on numerous policies and plans the Chinese government introduced to overcome the problem of sustainable production and judicious resource use. For instance, in 2015, China introduced the National Sustainable Agriculture Development Plan (2015-2030) to promote resource conservation, environmental protection, and sustainable agricultural development (NBSC, 2015). It focuses on improving irrigation efficiency, promoting water-saving technologies, and enhancing the use of organic fertilizers and bio pesticides. Another plan, the Green Food Development strategy (2016-2020), was announced in 2016 with the goal of increasing the production of green food, including organic rice, while reducing the use of chemical fertilizers and pesticides (Xu et al., 2011). It promotes the use of environmentally friendly farming methods and the establishment of green food certification systems. Then, in 2016, China announced the Soil Pollution Prevention and Control Action Plan (2016-2020), which focuses on preventing and controlling soil pollution, enhancing soil quality, and maintaining food safety. It encourages the use of healthy agricultural practices such as crop rotation, integrated pest management, and organic fertilizers (Chandio et al., 2023).

Besides these plans, China also introduced a cluster of policies to deal with broader issues, such as water-saving agricultural policies (Yang et al., 2022). To encourage farmers to employ water-saving irrigation techniques, such as drip and sprinkler irrigation, the Chinese government has implemented a number of water-saving policies (Cao et al., 2020). These policies seek to increase water use efficiency and decrease water waste in rice production. Another policy is crop rotation and diversification, which encourages farmers to adopt crop rotation and diversification practices to improve soil health and lower pest and disease risk (Zhang et al., 2022). This policy can also help improve soil fertility and reduce the need for chemical inputs. In order to encourage farmers to embrace sustainable agricultural practices such organic farming, integrated pest control, and the use of environmentally friendly technologies, China also introduced subsidies and financial incentives during the same period (Liu et al., 2021).

Another policy step taken by the Chinese government in recent decades is research and development (R&D). It has made significant investments in R&D to boost sustainable rice cultivation. This step entails doing research on novel rice varieties that are resistant to pests and diseases, provide larger yields, and can endure the effects of climate change (Zhang et al., 2022). In addition to R&D, China implemented extension services and farmer training programs to assist farmers in adopting sustainable agricultural practices such as integrated pest control, organic farming, and water-saving irrigation techniques (Huang et al., 2020). Finally, China advocated agroecological zones as part of its strategic planning to encourage the protection and sustainable use of its agricultural resources. This strategy aimed to maintain ecological balance, protect biodiversity, and provide food security.

These policies and measures promote sustainable rice production in China while addressing the environmental, social, and economic challenges of China's rapid agricultural development. Given these policies, this study points out the key variables: consumption of fertilizers, agricultural machinery power, and pesticides use from 2000 to 2020, presented in Figures S3-S5, respectively. Nonetheless, rice yield and sustainability are challenged by the growing scarcity of resources (land, water, labor, and machinery), the ineffective usage of inputs (herbicides, fertilizers, insecticides, and water), and the increasing costs of growing crops (Chauhan et al., 2017).

This study follows the study of Fan et al. (2009), which suggests that green finance (via public agricultural investments) leads to increased agricultural productivity, and the study of (Zhai et al., 2017), which suggest that the agricultural technical advancements (such as new production processes, novel seeds, fertilizers, water management methods, insecticides, and advanced agricultural technology) and favorable production environments significantly influence food security and agricultural production. Theoretically, this study follows Keynes's exposition that public expenditures are used as an exogenous factor (instrument) to increase growth. In other words, the study adopts the Keynesian perspective that the government can intervene in the economy by spending money on public goods and services, stimulating aggregate demand, and creating more income and employment opportunities. Considering this perspective, the study assumes that rice grain production is a public good to ensure food security and justify the green investment to help the farmers overcome climate risks. It implies that rice grain production is a beneficial activity that is non-rivalrous and non-excludable, which can provide sufficient and nutritious food for the population and protect the environment from degradation and pollution. Therefore, the (Chinese) government may invest in green technologies and practices that can enhance the productivity and resilience of rice farmers in the face of climate change.

The present study, for the first time in existing literature, examines the long-run effect of green finance (via agricultural investment) and technical progress (via fertilizer use, pesticide use, and total power consumption to agricultural machinery) on rice productivity in the perspective of major rice-producing regions of China over the period from 2000 to 2020 by using novel panel data techniques. It assumes the Cobb Douglas production function to estimate the elasticities: agricultural public investment (API), fertilizers use, power consumption, and planting area variables may have a considerable impact on rice production. Besides, this study applies second-generation panel methods, including cross-sectionally augmented Im, Pesaran, and Shin (CIPS) and cross-sectional augmented Dickey-Fuller (CADF) stationary tests (Pesaran, 2007), second-generation panel cointegration (Westerlund, 2007, error correction model [ECM]), and second-generation panel estimators such as Augmented-Mean Group (AMG) and Common-Correlated Effect Mean Group (CCEMG). Using all second-generation panel techniques, the empirical outcomes of the present study are robust and reliable. Furthermore, the study aims to investigate the following research questions:

## 2  LITERATURE REVIEW AND HYPOTHESES FORMULATION

## 2.1  Nexus between investment and agricultural output

Enhancing economic growth, particularly agricultural development, is crucial for food security. Without efforts to mitigate food insecurity, the world will face a catastrophic food shortage (Chandio, Jiang, Akram, et al., 2022). Climate change is intensifying, harming economic development, causing ecological imbalances, and leading to unsustainable development. Therefore, all governments, organizations, and businesses are investing in finding climate change solutions that will assist them in achieving their development goals and minimizing their environmental impact. To this end, one of the many solutions is green growth economies, which necessitate selecting green investments (Tran et al., 2020). In developing countries, government spending is the major tool used by policymakers to boost economic growth, which is a critical component of supporting overall economic development, such as improving infrastructure, education, housing, and health, and increasing agricultural productivity. Several academics evaluated the impact of government investment on agricultural output in diverse worldwide regions using a combination of econometric methodologies.

In particular, Xu et al. (2011) investigated the influence of several types of government spending on China's food industry. They discovered that government spending had a considerable impact on food production, pricing, and trade. Furthermore, greater public spending on agricultural R&D, farm subsidies, and irrigation has a little impact on other economic sectors. Similarly, De (2018) observes that public spending has a positive and significant impact on India's agricultural output and economic growth. Matthew and Mordecai (2016) investigated the impact of government agricultural spending on agricultural productivity in Nigeria. They discovered a long-term link between agricultural production and government agriculture spending.

Similarly, Zhu (2004) analyzed the impact of public expenditure in agricultural research and irrigation on the growth of grain assembly output. The author asserted that increased public spending on agricultural research would increase grain production output. Mustapha and Enilolobo (2019) also examined how public agricultural investments affect agricultural output in sub-Saharan Africa (SSA). Results demonstrated that public funding is essential to the success of the agricultural sector in feeding SSA. Ahmed et al. (2019) discovered that public investment in infrastructure, education, and health favorably affects the value addition of agriculture in Pakistan.

Likewise, Ewubare and Eyitope (2015) scrutinized the effect of agricultural expenditures on agricultural production in the case of Nigeria. The findings demonstrated that agricultural expenditure positively and substantially affected agricultural production. Recently, Gao et al. (2022) applied autoregressive distributed lag (ARDL) to analyze the influence of public expenditure on agricultural R&D and irrigation on China's agricultural production. Their findings found that public expenditures in agricultural R&D, such as irrigation, considerably impacted crop production. Saengchai et al. (2019) scrutinized the influence of government agriculture expenses on real agricultural GDP. They demonstrated a favorable and substantial impact of government expenditure on the real farm GDP in the long run. Salim and Islam (2010) utilized time series econometrics to evaluate the long-term effect of R&D spending and climatic change on agricultural production and overall output growth. They found that investment in public R&D is crucial for agricultural production development over the long term. Recent research by Usman et al. (2021) examined the influence of R&D modernism implementation and atmospheric conditions on Pakistan's agriculture's total factor productivity (TFP) development. The results indicated that adopting agricultural R&D innovations is vital to increase agricultural output. Likewise, Hurley et al. (2014) concluded that investing in agricultural R&D has a high return and increases production by 2.5%-5% compared to non-R&D spenders. In addition, Table 1 shows the summary of preceding studies.

## 2.3 | Literature gap

Upon conducting a comprehensive analysis of the existing body of literature, a number of gaps emerge. In the first stages, a majority of published research has focused on assessing the effects of climate change on cereal crop output, specifically in the context of China. However, there has been a limited exploration of the interplay between API and grain production. There is a lack of research investigating the impact of API on rice output in China, specifically in major rice-producing regions using panel data. Furthermore, the majority of prior studies have employed the ARDL model, fully modified ordinary least squares (FMOLS), and dynamic ordinary least squares (DOLS) estimation methods. In contrast, the current study employs more advanced panel data estimation approaches, including the panel co-integration ECM, AMG

CCEMG, and panel quantile regression (PQR). The consideration of cross-sectional dependence (CSD) in panel data analysis is crucial for obtaining reliable and robust results.

## 3  THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK, DATA, MODEL CONSTRUCTION, AND METHODOLOGY

## 3.1  Theoretical outline

The following theoretical notions can be used to explain the impact of API and technological progress on rice productivity in China: initially, the resource-based perspective mechanism implies that organizations with distinct and valuable resources are more likely to attain longterm competitive advantage. In the context of rice productivity, API and technical progress can be seen as valuable resources that can enhance the productivity of rice farmers. Furthermore, in the context of sustainable development that emphasizes the need to balance economic, social, and environmental objectives (Hu et al., 2021). Agricultural financing and technological innovation can both help to sustainable development by encouraging resource efficiency and lowering the negative environmental impacts of rice growing. Furthermore, according to the diffusion of innovation theory, the acceptance of new technologies is influenced by a variety of characteristics such as relative advantage, compatibility, complexity, and observability. These factors can influence the adoption of agricultural financing and technological progress, which can have an impact on rice farmers' production (Farooq et al., 2023). The impact of API and technical progress on rice yield is also influenced by numerous elements, containing the availability of resources, the level of investment, and the adoption of new technologies (Usman et al., 2021). Agricultural finance, which includes financial instruments and services that promote sustainable development, can provide rice farmers with the resources needed to invest in new technologies and practices that can improve rice output. Technical progress, which includes the development and adoption of new technologies and practices, can enhance the efficiency and sustainability of rice farming (Zhai et al., 2017).

## 3.4.3 | Panel long-run cointegration test

In the same step, bearing in mind the CSD properties of the variables/dataset and having employed a stationarity test that provides the selected panel distinctive, the consequent longrun cointegrating approach to be utilized requires also taken into the cognizance of the problem of CSD in the selected sample inspection. ## 5 ↑ CONCLUSIONS, POLICY IMPLICATIONS, AND LIMITATIONS OF THE STUDY

## 5.1  Conclusions

The current study examined the long-term impact of governmental investment and technological progress on rice production. From 2000 to 2020, it used balanced panel data for selected main rice-producing provinces in China. The AMG and CCEMG estimating methods are used to examine the long-run influence of green finance and technological progress on rice output in China's major rice-producing provinces. The effect of exogenous variables on rice production is assessed for robustness using the quantile regression technique. In addition, causal relationship between the considered variables is explored by using JKS Granger causality test. The main findings from the present study reveal that public investment, fertilizer use, power consumption for agricultural machinery, and sown area positively influence rice productivity within China's major rice-producing provinces. In contrast, pesticide usage and labor adversely influence rice yield. The findings of the panel quantile regression show that across various quantile levels, public investment, fertilizer use, power consumption, and sown area significantly contributed towards rice production, while pesticide usage negatively affected rice production in higher quantiles.

## 5.2  Policy implications

Based on the study results, the following are the policy implications for policymakers. First, the previous studies empirically proved that for sustainable production in the agriculture sector, public investment plays a pivotal role in food security sustainability. Hence, this research recommends that the Chinese government may prioritize expanding investment in green agricultural production, R&D, better irrigation system, green energy, and rural economic development. Besides, the Chinese government may strengthen the local system for better governance of agricultural and public finances to power sustainable production. Second, agrochemical inputs (i.e., fertilizer and pesticide) are an effective component of agricultural production, significantly boosting rice yield. The results of this study proved that pesticide usage negatively affected rice production within major rice-producing provinces of China. It implies that Chinese farmers are overusing chemical pesticides, which harm soil, the environment, and human health. Therefore, policymakers may follow the adaptive policy for pesticide usage, the right quantity for the right crop. Fertilizers are also part of the agrochemical inputs, and policymakers may develop a policy to encourage integrated input management practices, such as best management practices (BMPs) for fertilizer application based on soil testing, precision fertilization, and integrated nutrient management. The subsidy program may follow the principle of“payment for environmental services."It implies that farmers receive financial incentives for providing ecological benefits, such as reducing nutrient runoff, improving soil quality, and enhancing crop yields. Third, considering the role of extension services, the local government may encourage extension officers to train rice farmers on chemical inputs properly. Specifically the Chinese government may develop a network of agricultural innovation platforms (AIPs) that may facilitate the exchange of knowledge, information, and technologies among different stakeholders in the agricultural sector, such as farmers, researchers, extension agents, and private companies. These AIPs may follow the principle of “co-innovation," meaning that stakeholders collaborate and co-create solutions to address common problems and opportunities in agriculture. Next, the role of digital and innovative technologies may not be ignored; therefore, the AIP may benefit from digital tools and platforms, such as mobile phones, social media, and e-learning, to enhance the accessibility and quality of extension services. Lastly, to meet the domestic food demand, the policymakers may develop a policy to increase the planting of rice areas, appropriate use of chemical input applications, and allocate agricultural funds that may significantly assist in increasing rice productivity and ensuring food security.

## 5.3  Limitations of the study

One significant constraint of this research pertains to its exclusive focus on the rice-producing provinces of China when examining API. Our findings indicate that in the key rice-producing regions of China, API, along with technology advancements such as the use of fertilizers, pesticides, and farm machinery, significantly influences rice yields. Nevertheless, the various variables contributing to climate change are often overlooked. In future study endeavors, it would be prudent for scholars to take into account the influence of climatic variables on cereal crops output throughout different regions of China. Moreover, the potential for replicating the findings of this study in the future extends to major grain-producing nations worldwide, providing an area of interest within the scope of forthcoming investigations.
