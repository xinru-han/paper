## ABSTRACT

Based on the theory of New Economic of Labor Migration (NELM) and Induced Technological Change, this study takes 935 part-time households in the Loess Plateau region as the research object. The study analyzes the current situation of non-agricultural labor transfer and agricultural production, and describes the intrinsic relationship and mechanism of the action between non-agricultural labor transfer, factor allocation and farmland output-rate. The direct and indirect effects of non-agricultural labor transfer on unit farmland output are tested empirically through the mediating effect model and the moderating effect model, and the effectiveness of the mechanism of action is further tested using the moderated mediation model (Bootstrap). The empirical results show that nonagricultural labor transfer has significant and negative impact on farmland output. Moreover, labor-substituting factor inputs play a partially mediating role between non-agricultural labor transfer and farmland output-rate, with a mediating effect size of – 1.552, accounting for 53.073 % of the total effect. The findings also demonstrate that higher the proportion of slope cropland, the smaller the positive effect of labor substitute factor input on farmland output. Similarly, non-agricultural labor transfer has a significant and negative effect on farmland output for low-part-time peasants and cash crops, the negative effect on peasants participating in farmland transfer is not significant, and a larger negative effect on distant working peasants. Based on the above findings, this paper puts forward targeted policy recommendations for optimizing the allocation of agricultural factors and improving agricultural production efficiency, aiming to provide a theoretical basis and decision-making reference for ensuring abundant grain production and agricultural harvest in the Loess Plateau region, lifting peasants out of poverty and increasing their income, and realizing the rural revitalization strategy.

## 1. Introduction

In China, how does the non-agricultural labor transfer affect the agricultural production process and its yield? Despite the severity of the COVID-19 outbreak, China has maintained social stability, which is largely due to the stable supply of food and important agricultural and sideline products. For a developing country like China with a population of 1.4 billion, food security has always been a top priority in the governance of the country. In the context of the continuous expansion of migrant workers in China, how to ensure that the level of food production only increases but not decreases has become one of the important issues of concern to the government and academia. The optimal allocation of rural labor force between agricultural and non-agricultural sectors and between urban and rural areas not only has a great impact on urban economy, but also has a profound impact on rural development and agricultural production (Guohua and Zheng, 2021). With the migration of a large number of high-quality labor force, left-behind, women and the elderly have become the main force of rural agricultural production. In this process, how to fill the labor vacancy caused by the transfer has become an important problem to be solved urgently.

As the scale of non-agricultural labor transfer increases, the existing rural labor force become weak and insufficient (Liao et al., 2019), resulting in the continuous decline of agricultural land yield rate. However, the National Bureau of Statistics data show (Fig. 1) from 2014 to 2019 every year in China: migrant workers, total agricultural output value, and grain yield per unit area, have rising trend. Which indicates that the increase of labor non-agricultural transfer did not cause the decline of farmland yield rate, but agricultural performance on the whole showed a trend of continuous rise. So, in the process of the continuous expansion of the scale of rural labor transfer, what causes the agricultural land yield rate to increase instead of decrease? According to the existing research results, the possible reasons mainly include the following aspects: first, the optimization of the structure of agricultural production factors, improving the labor production efficiency and agricultural production efficiency of farmers (Engerman and Sokolof, 2000). Secondly, agricultural family management is gradually moving towards the division of labor economy (Long, 2020), and prompting farmers to use services instead of labor. Thirdly, the increase of agricultural output caused by the progress of agricultural technology (Zhang et al., 2017) and finally, government subsidy policies, climate and other factors (Haque et al., 2020; Simola and Antti, 2018; Imran et al., 2019).

With the rapid development of urbanization and industrialization, among the three major factors of agricultural production activities, the scarcity degree and price of labor relative to land and capital are still rising. Fig. 2 shows the labor input, machinery input and fertilizer input per unit of rural land in China from 2013 to 2018. It shows that the machinery operation cost, fertilizer net use and employment cost in agricultural production are all on the rise, and the comparative advantage of labor factor against other agricultural production factors is on the rise. Peasants are more likely to invest labor factors with comparative advantages into industrial sectors with higher returns and maximize family income through factor substitution and factor redistribution (Baležentis et al., 2021). This indicates that when discussing the impact of labor off-farm transfer on farmland productivity, we should not only consider the change of labor input, but also discuss the substitution effect of agricultural mechanization services and other production factors such as chemical fertilizers and pesticides on labor factors (Li et al., 2020). In other words, the increase of off-farm income caused by off-farm labor transfer may compensate for the loss of labor factors by changing the input structure of agricultural factors, so as to maintain or increase the yield of agricultural land. Under the circumstance that labor force is no longer infinite supply and continuous transfer, improving the yield rate of agricultural land is the key to maintain China's agricultural competitiveness and stable supply of agricultural products. Loess Plateau is a traditional dry farming area in China, as well as a main grain producing area. For a long time, soil erosion is serious, natural disasters occur frequently, population outflow trend is obvious, land productivity is low, and agricultural output level is generally not high. Therefore, it is of great practical significance to carry out the research on non-agricultural labor transfer and agricultural production in the loess plateau region to alleviate the contradiction between regional population and resources to ensure regional food security. Therefore, in this study, the system research on the Loess Plateau region rural non-agricultural labor transfer influence on farmland productivity, and considering the factors of agricultural labor transfer caused changes in the structure and the agricultural configuration for specific rural non-agricultural labor force transfer, and the linkage mechanism between farmland productivity has important theoretical significance, also has the reality urgency.

Domestic and foreign scholars(Long, 2020; Haque et al., 2020) have done a lot of research on the off-farm labor transfer and agricultural production behavior of farmers, and the research field is constantly broadening and comprehensive, which has important reference significance for us to analyze the relationship between the off-farm labor transfer and agricultural production behavior. However, the following deficiencies need to be further supplemented: First of all, on the research object, although studies have involved the contact between the two (Li et al., 2020), they usually take the peasants without non-agricultural transfer and those with non-agricultural transfer into consideration in a unified analytical framework, the "pure peasants" and "part-time peasants" considered together, because of its motivation and decision factors of family structure adjustment strategy is different, can lead to inaccurate results. Secondly, in terms of research content, existing studies (Liao et al., 2019) have not reached a consensus on the relationship between off-farm labor transfer and agricultural land output, mainly ignoring not only the direct impact but also the indirect impact between the two, lacking systematic theoretical and mechanism analysis. Thirdly, in the research path, although existing studies (Wang et al., 2020) have considered the role of the substitution mechanism of factors of production, most of them are based on qualitative analysis and ignore the impact of terrain conditions on it. At the same time, although existing studies (Martínez-Casasnovas et al., 2010) have discussed the difficulty of substitution of factors of production, they only conduct qualitative analysis or simple empirical study based on subjective classification of mountains and plains, lacking accurate quantitative research on relevant issues. Fourthly, in terms of research methods, most of the existing studies (Li et al., 2020) only use the Causal Steps Approach to verify the mediating effect and the moderating effect, ignoring the unreasonable test and insufficient effectiveness of this method.

This paper attempts from the following three aspects to supplement to the existing research: First is to determine the research object, because diverse type peasants (i.e., pure peasants, part-time peasants, non-farming households) in factors of family configuration and the agricultural production decision, there is big difference, etc. This article focuses on the non-agricultural labor transfer degree influence on farmland productivity; thus, this article chooses "part-time peasants" as the research object; Secondly, the research content, this paper builds "non-agricultural labor transfer-factors allocation-farmland productivity" theoretical analysis framework, which reveals the non-agricultural labor transfer mechanism of the impact of farmland productivity. At the same time, the ratio of farmland area with slope range from 7° to 25° to the total farmland area at county level was used as factor substitution difficulty to quantify. Third, in terms of research methods, two test methods, Causal Steps Approach and Bootstrap, are used to test the mechanism in order to obtain more accurate test results.

## 2. The theoretical basis

From the perspective of household factor allocation, this paper takes part-time households as the research object to explore the impact of nonagricultural labor transfer on agricultural land output per unit area. The change of peasants' agricultural production situation is fundamentally caused by the change of peasants' behavior. In combination with Alexander V. Chayanov's The Theory of Peasant Economy and the New Economics of Labor Migration (NELM) (Stark, 1991), peasants tend to follow the principle of utility or income maximization and rationally allocate family labor, income and other factors on the premise of considering family resource endowment. ## 2.1. The direct impact of non-agricultural labor transfer on the farmland output rate

In the practice of non-agricultural transfer of rural labor force in China, the non-agricultural transfer of labor force directly affects the agricultural land yield from the following three aspects. First, the number of workers has decreased (Liao et al., 2019), the most direct manifestation of non-agricultural labor transfer is the loss of agricultural labor force, which reduces the agricultural labor force and labor time of peasants. Although the moderate loss of agricultural labor force is helpful to alleviate the phenomenon of over-density of agricultural labor force, when the excessive loss of labor force, the enthusiasm of peasants in agricultural production will be constantly reduced, and the land may be unable to get intensive cultivation because of the shortage of labor force, thus adversely affecting the yield rate of agricultural land. Second, the loss of labor force reduces the comprehensive quality of surplus agricultural labor force (Zhang et al., 2018). Non-agricultural labor transfer is not only a process of reducing the number of agricultural labor force, but also a process of losing young and high-quality labor force. The left-behind population structure in rural areas has changed, and women, elders and children have become the main residents in rural areas. Therefore, the reduction of the quantity and quality of rural surplus labor force is not conducive to the improvement of farmland productivity. Third, the higher the degree of concurrent employment of peasants, the importance of agricultural production in family management will be weakened (Min et al., 2018), mainly in the following two aspects: On the one hand, with the deepening of the degree of non-agricultural labor transfer, the shortage of labor supply increases the cost of agricultural production for peasants. When the marginal cost is higher than the marginal revenue, peasants will choose to give up agricultural production in order to maximize their interests. On the other hand, the increase in the proportion of off-farm household income makes peasants less dependent on agricultural production, and the degree of sideline agriculture will deepen (Hennessy and O'Brien, 2008). Based on the above analysis, proposing the Hypothesis 1:

Hypothesis 1. Non-agricultural labor transfer has a negative impact on farmland output rate.

2.2. Indirect impact of non-agricultural labor transfer on farmland output rate

## 2.2.1. Mediation effect of factor substitution

Non-agricultural labor transfer not only has a direct impact on farmland yield, but also indirectly affects peasants' farmland output by changing the input structure of agricultural factors. Factor substitution mainly refers to the substitution of labor force by capital (Alvarez-Cuadrado et al., 2017), which means that peasants increase the purchase of capital factors such as pesticides, fertilizers, agricultural machinery and agricultural socialized services in order to compensate for the reduction of labor force factors.

Combined with existing studies, the impact of labor off-farm transfer on agricultural factor input structure is mainly reflected in the following three aspects. Firstly, the increase in household income brought by offfarm labor transfer can alleviate the financial constraints faced by agricultural production. As peasants concurrently the degree of deepening, only on family surplus labor force for agricultural production is often difficult to maintain the original agricultural income, the investment of agricultural machinery is needed to save manpower and farming (Xin et al., 2021), but due to different link using agricultural machinery has the difference, namely asset specificity is stronger, peasants can't burden each link mechanization needs money. At the same time, if peasants want to make long-term investment in land, they often use capital intensive production factors such as organic fertilizer, whose high capital input will greatly increase the burden of peasants and form capital constraints. The increase of household income level brought by the non-agricultural labor transfer can alleviate part of the capital constraints faced by agricultural production and increase the factor purchasing ability of peasants. Secondly, non-agricultural labor transfer will reduce the agricultural labor supply of peasants, and objectively require peasants to increase capital input without changing the land management area and planting type. When the labor quality and intensity decline, peasants will make up for the loss caused by labor loss by purchasing machinery, chemical fertilizers, pesticides and improved seeds in a short period of time (Li et al., 2020), which shows the change in the input structure of agricultural factors, namely the capital-labor substitution relationship. Finally, the emergence of socialized agricultural services alleviates the constraints of insufficient agricultural labor force of peasant households (Tongwei et al., 2020). Under the background of the increase of opportunity cost of farming and the rapid rise of agricultural labor cost, it is an important choice for peasants to adopt alternative service elements (Haeussler and Sauermann, 2020). Peasants can choose to buy agricultural hire service or agricultural machinery leasing service, which saves a lot of funds and relatively scarce labor factors for peasants to buy agricultural machinery by themselves, breaks through the limitations of peasants' original resource endowment, and is beneficial to peasants' agricultural production.

The impact of adjustment of factor input structure on farmland yield is mainly reflected in the following two aspects. On the one hand, from the perspective of Neoclassical Economics, agricultural development can be regarded as the substitution process of modern agricultural factors for traditional agricultural factors (Schultz, 2013). Through the continuous optimization of factor input structure, it can promote the transformation of agriculture from labor intensive to capital intensive, and then promote the improvement of agricultural land yield rate. On the other hand, the decrease of labor factors and the increase of capital factors will often lead peasants to optimize the allocation of land factors (Alvarez-Cuadrado et al., 2017). The main form of capital replacing labor is to promote the improvement of farmland productivity through the scale effect caused by land concentration and the specialization effect caused by division of labor. Based on the above analysis, proposing the Hypothesis 2:

Hypothesis 2. Agricultural substitution factors input plays a mediating role in the effect of non-agricultural labor transfer on farmland output rate.

Hypothesis 2a. Non-agricultural labor transfer has a positive impact on the agricultural substitution factors input.

Hypothesis 2b. Agricultural substitution factors input has a positive impact on the farmland output rate.

## 2.2.2. Moderation effect of resource endowment constraints

With the continuous transfer of labor force, the substitution effect of material capital on labor force is more and more obvious. According to the Induced Technological Change (Hayami and Ruttan, 1985), the substitution of material capital for labor force needs to consider the possibility of its realization, which will be affected by topographic conditions, agronomy, technology supply and other factors, among which topographic conditions are the most important constraint conditions that cannot be easily changed (Martínez-Casasnovas et al., 2010). Compared with plain areas, hilly and mountainous areas have complex geographical conditions and serious land fragmentation, which have a significant barrier effect on labor substitute production factors (Latruffe and Piet, 2014), mainly in the following two aspects. First, reduce farm machinery field accessibility and operation convenience (Tarolli and Straffelini, 2020). When agricultural production is carried out under the terrain conditions of small plot area, irregular shape, large slope and scattered distribution, the low feasibility of agricultural machinery operation and the inefficiency of factor input will reduce the use value of peasants' capital factors (Wang et al., 2020). Second, there is no scale effect and constantly reduce the marginal effect of factor input. The farmland in hilly and mountainous areas is seriously fragmented, mostly scattered and uneven, with poor concentration and large height drop (Zhong et al., 2020), which makes it difficult to achieve large-scale land management and has no scale effect. Compared with plain areas, peasants in hilly and mountainous areas often need to invest more material capital to achieve the same land output rate. Based on the above analysis, proposing the Hypothesis 3:

Hypothesis 3. Topographic conditions negatively moderated the effect of the input of alternative production factors on farmland output rate.

## 3. Methodology

## 3.1. Study area and sampling description

The research data in this paper are derived from the mixed cross section data obtained from field investigation in the Loess Plateau region in China in 2018 and 2019. The research area includes Shaanxi, Gansu and Ningxia provinces. The terrain of this area is inclined from northwest to southeast. Except for some Rocky Mountains, most of this area is covered by thick loess, which is a typical region of loess Plateau in northwest China. It contains two landform types: plateau gully region and hilly gully region. The study considers different type areas according to erosion types, soil erosion condition, population density, etc, in the plateau gully region selected typical representative areas are; Qian County, Changwu County and Luochuan County, while in the plateau hilly-gully region selected typical representative areas are; Ansai County, Mizhi and Wuqi County. Similarly, in the loess hilly-gully region, Anding District, Weiyuan County, Pengyang County and Yuanzhou District are selected as sample areas. The corresponding images of the study area were drawn using ArcGIS10.3, as can be seen in Fig. 4, the further north the elevation difference is, the more obvious the landform difference. This survey adopts typical survey, stratified random sampling and simple random sampling. Firstly, considering the differences in terrain conditions, planting types, policy implementation and social economy, six counties (districts) in Xianyang City, Yan'an City and Yulin City in Shaanxi Province, two counties (districts) in Dingxi City of Gansu Province and two counties (districts) in Guyuan City of Ningxia Province were selected by typical survey method. Secondly, the method of stratified sampling and simple random sampling were used to randomly select 2–3 towns in the above 10 counties, 4–5 villages were randomly selected from each town, and 10–15 peasants with communication ability were randomly selected from each village for questionnaire survey, and a total of 1422 samples were obtained. The research object of this paper is peasants who both engage in agricultural production and have non-agricultural labor, that is, part-time peasants. Therefore, the paper excludes pure peasants, samples with zero actual cultivated land area, and samples with agricultural output value or major independent variables missing, and finally 935 effective samples are obtained. The sample distribution is shown in Table 1. The content of the questionnaire involves the basic information of the respondents and their families, the characteristics of cultivated land and the input-output status of farmland.

## 3.2. Research objectives

The essence of peasant household differentiation is that the proportion of agricultural income decreases in household income. With the rapid development of economy and society, the peasant household differentiation in China presents an evolutionary process of “pure peasants—part-time peasants—non-peasants" (Siyang et al., 2021). The higher the degree of concurrent occupation of peasant households, the smaller the number of rural labor force engaged in agricultural production, the lower the proportion of agricultural income in household income. As an economic analysis unit and the object of management analysis, the peasant household can realize the improvement of its own production level through rational allocation of various elements of the family. This study mainly discusses the impact of the degree of labor transfer on peasants' agricultural production behavior and output results. For pure peasants and non-farming, although it can be regarded as 0 and 1, its influence mechanism is quite different from that of part-time peasants. Therefore, this paper chooses part-time peasants as the research object.

## 3.3.3. Mediator

The mediator of this paper is expressed by the input degree of labor substitute production factors (Kim, 2016). Labor substitution production factors mainly include agricultural machinery, chemical fertilizer and pesticides, social production services, etc. Therefore, this paper adopts the sum of the per mu use of mechanical chemical fertilizer and pesticides and the cost of employment to represent the size of substitution effect. We measured the total amount of input of labor-substituting factors of production by asking about the purchase and input of each factor, and then calculated the degree of input per hectare of land by dividing the total amount of input of labor-substituting factors of production by the area of land operated by the farming household.

## 3.3.4. Moderator

In this paper, topographic conditions of the region where the cultivated land is located are taken as moderator to test whether the impact of structural adjustment of agricultural factors on agricultural land output is different due to different geographical conditions. According to the concept of slope cultivated land, considering that all slope cultivated land of 25° and above has been converted to forest in the Loess Plateau region, and referring to the research of Zheng Xuyuan et al. (2018), this paper adopts the proportion of cultivated land area with slope range from 7° to 25° in the total cultivated land area as a proxy variable of topographic conditions. The higher the proportion of sloping farmland is, the more serious the soil erosion is, the less conducive to mechanical operation, and the more difficult it will be to replace labor factors. Fig. 6 shows the proportion of sloping farmland area in each county.

## 3.3.5. Control variables

In order to avoid other factors that may affect the structural adjustment of agricultural factors and the output of farmland, this paper selects the following control variables: age, years of education, health level and risk preference variables reflect the individual characteristics of the households. The average education level of family members, the proportion of the elderly, the proportion of female, the cost of family life and credit variables reflect the family characteristics. The area of cultivated land, the quality of cultivated land and the degree of fragmentation and whether irrigation reflects the characteristics of farmland. The year 2018 was set as a time dummy variable, and whether Shaanxi and Gansu were used as regional dummy variables to control the influence of time and regional factors on the regression results. ## 4.1. Analysis of the direct effect (Hypothesis 1)

This paper first provides a preliminary description of the relationship between non-agricultural labor transfer, farmland output rate, and inputs of labor-substituting factors of production in the sample of the study area. As shown in the figure, as the proportion of household labor off-farm transfer increases, the overall trend of farmland output rate decreases, and the input of capital factors first increases and then decreases, which is consistent with the regression results in the later section (Fig. 7).

In the current study, Stata16.0 software is used to estimate the impact of off-farm labor transfer on agricultural land yield. Heteroscedasticity robust standard error is used for all regressions, and multicollinearity test is carried out for all regressions. The variance inflation factor (VIF) is all lower than 3.0, indicating that the degree of correlation between the variables is within a reasonable range, and there exists no serious collinearity problem. Hypothesis H1 is preliminarily verified.

The estimated results of control variables are basically consistent with the expectation. The results of regression (3) showed that the age of household head had a significant negative impact on farmland outputrate and their risk preference has a significant positive impact on farmland yield. Household living expenses have a significant positive impact on farmland yield. The higher the living expenses of peasant households are, the higher their living standards are. The improvement of the living standards of peasant households is conducive to the agricultural productive investment of peasant households, and thus to the improvement of the agricultural land yield rate. Land quality had a positive impact on land yield at the significance level of 1 %. The higher the level of output per unit of agricultural land for farm households involved in agricultural land transfer. The higher the level of output per unit of agricultural land for farmers who mainly grow cash crops. When peasants invest the same agricultural production factors, the better the land quality and the higher the agricultural production efficiency. The land area and the degree of fragmentation of agricultural land both negatively affected the agricultural land yield at the statistical significance level of 1 %. Due to the high degree of farmland fragmentation, the increase of cultivated land area does not bring scale effect, but will affect peasants' fine tillage of farmland, and then negatively affect their yield per unit area (Hou et al., 2021). Irrigation has a significant positive effect on the 1 % level, indicating that peasants with irrigation have a higher yield per unit area compared to peasants with totally dependent on the weather.

## 4.1.1. Endogeneity

There may be endogenous problems in non-agricultural labor transfer and land yield due to reverse causality and omitted variables. On the one hand, lower level of agricultural land output will encourage peasants to choose local non-agricultural employment or out-migrating for work, increasing the possibility of labor transfer. This reverse causality relationship will overestimate the impact of non-agricultural labor transfer. On the other hand, due to the limitations of the research design, there may be some variables that affect both the non-agricultural employment of peasants and the output of agricultural land, such as village traffic conditions and policy implementation, which may be omitted. Therefore, this paper intends to use instrumental variable method to solve potential endogeneity problems. Based on Yang's (2021) research, this paper selects the average value of non-agricultural labor transfer of other peasant households in the village except the respondents as the instrumental variable. It should be noted that whether peasants choose local non-agricultural employment or out-migrating for work is closely related to the overall non-agricultural transfer of labor force in this region, while does not directly affect the agricultural land output efficiency, that is, in theory, it conforms to the requirements of correlation and exogeneity of instrumental variables.

Regression (4)–(6) in Table 4 are a series of test results of 2SLS in which control variables and dummy variables are gradually added. Wald endogeneity results reject the hypothesis of no endogeneity at the 1 % level. Meanwhile, p value of DWH test is less than 0.05, which rejects the null hypothesis that the explanatory variable is exogenous. Therefore, non-agricultural labor transfer can be considered as an endogenous explanatory variable. In addition, the F statistic in the first stage of 2SLS is 37.80, greater than the empirical value of 16.38 (Wu et al., 2018; Stock and Yogo, 2005), thus rejecting the null hypothesis of "existence of weak instrumental variables". After introducing instrumental variables to address the potential endogeneity, the results of regression (6) show that the estimated coefficient of non-agricultural labor transfer on farmland yield is negative at the significance level of 1 %. This result indicates that the non-agricultural labor transfer of part-time peasants has a significant negative impact on farmland productivity, and Hypothesis 1 is supported.

## 4.2.2. Moderation effect

The test results of moderating effect of topographic conditions are shown in Table 5. ## 4.2.3. Moderated mediation effect

According to the above model setting, the relevant term of the moderator is a1b2、 a3b1、 a3b2, and the existence of the moderated mediating effect can be confirmed by the significance test of the coefficients in any of the three groups. As shown in Table 5 regression results, a1 = − 8.330 ; a3 = 0 ; b1 = 0.932 ; b2 = − 0.162. Therefore, it can be concluded that a1b2 ≠ 0, the moderated mediating effect hypothesis is established. Since the product standard error is not considered to judge the significance of a product only based on the significance of the two coefficients, the Bootstrap method is adopted in this paper to calculate the mediating effect size, so as to determine whether the mediating effect changes with the moderator. Referring to Edwards and Lambert (2007), the mean plus or minus one standard deviation was selected as both ends of the value of the moderator to test whether the difference in mediating effect under the condition value was significant. The results are shown in Table 7, the moderated mediating effect value at the proportion of low-slope cropland area is – 1.916, and high-slope cropland area is – 1.083. The confidence interval at the 95 % level of difference BC-Bootstrap method does not contain 0, which was further confirmed that the moderated mediating effect was significant and the effect size was of practical significance, and Hypothesis H3 was confirmed.

In order to reveal more clearly how topographic condition, regulate the relationship between capital substitution and farmland yield, in SPSS 22.0, topographic conditions are divided into high and low groups according to "Mean ± 1SD" for simple slope graph test. The results showed that capital substitution positively predicted farmland productivity when the ratio of sloping farmland area (7–25°) to total farmland area was low (mean-1 SD) (β = 0.788, t = 13.135, p < 0.001). When the ratio of sloping farmland area (7–25°) to total farmland area was high (Mean + 1SD), the positive prediction effect of capital substitution on farmland yield was weakened (β = 0.401, t = 11.627, P < 0.001, β = 0.788 reduced to β = 0.401) (Fig. 8).

## 4.4.2. Planting type

Heterogeneous effects of planting type: cash crops and food crops; considering the differences of peasants' planting structure and the different growth cycles and input requirements of different crops, the non-agricultural labor transfer may have heterogeneous effects on farmland yield within different planting types. In this paper, according to the planting types of crops, they are divided into cash crops and food crops, of which cash crops mainly planted is apple, and food crops mainly planted are corn and wheat. The dependent variable in the regression is expressed in terms of the amount of agricultural land per unit of each type of crop. The empirical results are presented in regressions (17) and (18) of Tables 5–9, where there are differences in the magnitude of the effect of labor off-farm transfer on the output rate per unit area and its significance level for both types of crops. At the significance level of 10 %, non-agricultural labor transfer has a negative impact on unit area yield of cash crops, but has no significant impact on food crops. The reason is that the production of cash crops requires larger number of labor and has a long cycle, which is labor-intensive product (Qiu et al., 2020). Therefore, the non-agricultural labor transfer has a more obvious impact on cash crops.

## 4.4.3. Land transfer

Heterogeneous effect of land transfer: land transfer and non-land transfer; participation in land circulation is an important embodiment of peasants' rational allocation of production factors. Both the allocation of labor factors and the allocation of land factors have a direct impact on agricultural production. Therefore, the non-agricultural labor transfer may have a heterogeneous impact on agricultural land yield under different circumstances. The empirical results are shown in Table 11 regression (19) and (20). The effect of non-agricultural labor transfer on output rate per unit area is negative for both groups, but the negative effect of non-agricultural labor transfer on output rate of farmland is not significant for farmers in the land transfer group and significant for farmers in the non-transfer group. The possible reason is that peasants involved in land transfer are often able to reasonably allocate resources (Tongwei et al., 2020), and at the same time, it is easier to realize large-scale land operation, which is conducive to improving the productivity per unit area.

## 4.4.4. Labor distance

Heterogeneity effect of working location: within county, outside county and inside city, outside city; in the practice of non-agricultural labor transfer in China, peasants often make full use of their family labor resources and choose the form of part-time employment to maximize their interests, that is, to maximize their family income by taking into account agricultural operation while out-migrating for work. In view of the spontaneity and dispersity of non-agricultural labor transfer, the spatial distance difference is obvious, which can be divided into three regions: within county, outside county and inside city, outside city according to the distance. The empirical results are shown in Table 12 regression (21)–(23). The longer the distance of non-agricultural labor transfer, the greater the negative impact of non-agricultural labor transfer on farmland productivity. The possible reasons lie in the following two aspects: on the one hand, from the perspective of peasants themselves, short-distance migrant labor force has obvious characteristics of concurrent employment. On the other hand, the agricultural income effect and labor loss effect of different distance degree are compared. Generally speaking, the income effect of short-distance work is lower than that of long-distance work, but the labor loss caused by long-distance work is greater than that of short-distance work. Especially in the "rush to seed, rush to harvest" agricultural busy period, those crops with low mechanization degree often need a lot of labor input, the cost of short-distance migrant labor returning home is low, while the opportunity cost of long-distance migrant labor returning home is high, unable to timely input enough labor, which is not conducive to agricultural production.

## 5. Conclusion and recommendations

Based on the data of 935 peasant households in the Loess Plateau region in 2018 and 2019, this paper systematically explores the relationship between non-agricultural labor transfer and farmland productivity. The result of research shows that: (1) Non-agricultural labor transfer has a significant negative impact on farmland output-rate, that is, for part-time peasants, the higher the proportion of non-agricultural labor transfer, the lower the farmland yield.

(2) Labor substitution factor input plays a partial mediating role in the relationship between non-agricultural labor transfer and agricultural land yield rate, and its mediating effect is – 1.552, accounting for 53.073 % of the total effect. This result is contrary to the research hypothesis, the possible reason lies in the relatively backward degree of agricultural modernization in the Loess Plateau region, and the capital constraint of peasants basically does not exist. The increase of income brought by non-agricultural labor transfer will only increase the consumption investment of peasants, or reduce the importance of agricultural production in family operation, and then have a negative impact on productive investment. For the part-time peasants in Loess Plateau region, it is necessary to optimize the input structure of production factors and improve the yield of farmland by promoting labor substitution input.

(3) Topographic conditions moderated the relationship between labor substitute input of production factors and agricultural land yield, that is, the higher the proportion of sloping farmland, the less positive effect of capital factor input on agricultural land yield.

(4) Heterogeneity analysis shows that non-agricultural labor transfer has a significant negative impact on the yield of agricultural land with low part-time degree peasants. Non-agricultural labor transfer has a significant negative impact on the yield per unit area of cash crops, but has no significant negative impact on food crops. Compared with peasants who participated in land transfer, peasants who did not participate in land transfer had a significant negative impact on land yield. The longer the distance of labor transfer, the greater the negative effect on farmland output-rate.

Based on the above conclusions, this paper puts forward the following policy recommendations:

Firstly, from the perspective of non-agricultural labor transfer, the government should continue to promote the citizens of migrant workers into the core of reform of linkage, guarantee the stability of the migrant workers in urban residential and employment expectations, optimize the existing population structure, enable peasants to rational allocation of family resources, and enhance factor structure and the suitability of agricultural production efficiency.

Second, given the region natural ecological conditions of vulnerability and modernization level is relatively backward, the government should be moderate to important agricultural production to improve the western subsidies or ecological reserve corresponding standard or adjust the types, to guide peasants in the protection of agricultural production on the basis of gradual differentiation, promoting agricultural production main body structure, improve agricultural inputs and in the process, actively cultivate new professional peasants, accelerate the modernization of peasants in the process of agricultural modernization.

Thirdly, from the perspective of topographic conditions, the government and enterprises should strengthen the development and popularization of small and medium-sized agricultural machinery and tools suitable for hilly and mountainous areas, reduce agricultural production costs, and improve the market supply level of agricultural socialization services in western China.

Finally, the government should improve the land transfer market, encourage peasants to carry out land transfer, optimize the allocation of rural land factors, reduce abandoned farmland, and promote large-scale land management.

A summary of the theoretical contributions as well as the practical implications of this paper is presented:

Firstly, this paper takes the Loess Plateau as the study area, which is characterized by severe soil erosion, frequent natural disasters, obvious population outflow trends, low land productivity, and generally low agricultural output levels. Therefore, a study on the non-agricultural labor transfer and agricultural production in the Loess Plateau region is conducted. It helps to scientifically judge the development stage and development trend of regional agricultural production, adjust the regional agricultural production structure according to local conditions, and support regional agricultural production in a differentiated manner, while providing a basis for decision-making for the government to formulate agricultural and food production support policies, which is of great practical significance to alleviate the contradiction between regional population and resources and ensure regional food security.

Secondly, the Loess Plateau is one of the four major plateaus in China, and its special geographical location and climatic environment have given birth to the unique culture of the Loess Land. Due to its ecological fragility characteristics and its geological environment, the Loess Plateau region has a slow pace of agricultural modernization, and its development has its special barriers compared to the plains. The study of the agricultural development and production behavior of farmers in this region can provide a reference for peasants in the same slow and constrained development region, as well as a reference for other developing countries on rural, agricultural, and farmers' issues.

Thirdly, it is about the theoretical significance of this paper. Based on the New Economics of Labor Migration (NELM) theory, this paper systematically studies the impact of rural non-agricultural labor transfer on output per unit of farmland from the perspective of the overall welfare of farm households and examines the changes in agricultural factor input structure caused by non-agricultural labor transfer, broadening the understanding of the relationship between labor transfer and agricultural production, and improving the understanding of the relationship between labor transfer and agricultural factor structure. At the same time, considering the difficulty of factor substitution under technological progress as an important influencing factor for the adjustment of cropping structure and factor allocation structure, provides a new perspective for studying related issues.
