## ABSTRACT

The sustainable and intensive utilization (SIU) of cultivated land is key to achieving food security and promoting ecological balance at the national level. Agricultural machinery purchase subsidy (AMPS) is one of the agricultural policies with the largest capital investment involving the widest range of subsidies in China. It has both theoretical and practical significance to explore whether AMPS can stimulate the SIU of cultivated land. However, most existing studies investigate the influence of AMPS on agricultural vield and income, while the mechanism by which it promotes the transition to the SIU of cultivated land remains unclear. Given this, based on elasticity theory, this study constructed the impact mechanism of AMPS on the SIU and used the county data of the Huang-Huai-Hai Plain to conduct an empirical test. Emergy analysis showed that high levels of the SIU were concentrated in the central part of Hebei, and the inter-regional differences showed a decreasing trend over the years. Basic regression results indicated that AMPS contributed to improving the utilization ratio of natural resources, the utilization ratio of production factors, and emergy value productivity while reducing environmental load pressure, thus promoting the transition toward the SIU of cultivated land. The conclusion remained valid when the instrumental variables approach was applied for robustness testing after considering the endogeneity issue. The mediation mechanism test showed that AMPS improved the SIU of cultivated land by promoting the development of agricultural machinery socialization services (AMSS), which had a partial mediating effect. This study affirms the importance of fostering AMSS in promoting the SIU of cultivated land through agricultural subsidies. It is expected to provide innovative ideas and practical experience for the agricultural sector in advancing the SIU of cultivated land.

## 1. Introduction

Rapid urbanization is causing continuous compression of cultivated land. Land management has become a key focus within sustainable development goals (SDGs) (Moosavi-Nezhad et al., 2022). Limited land availability and intensified resource competition have created a pressing dilemma. In this context, enhancing land use intensity has become a key strategy to achieve the SDGs and reduce global hunger (Ickowitz et al., 2019). How to enhance the sustainable intensive utilization (SIU) of cultivated land while ensuring grain output and maintaining environmental sustainability has become a research hotspot.

The SIU of cultivated land is the ideal stage in the transformation of rural land use. Pretty (1995) first proposed the concept of the SIU, emphasizing the goal of increasing agricultural production within existing land use while minimizing adverse effects on land resources and the environment. Since then, scholars have engaged in extensive discussions on the SIU of cultivated land. Based on the theory of induced factor substitution, Ke et al. (2024) constructed a framework to identify the transformation toward intensive utilization of cultivated land, and analyzed the transformation process influenced by the input of production factors. Some scholars have focused on the land structure and land morphology involved in the SIU of cultivated land. As a comprehensive treatment method for inefficient and damaged land, land consolidation is effective in reducing land fragmentation, improving land productivity, and restoring the ecological environment (Jiang et al., 2024). Intensive utilization is often accompanied by shifts in farming practices, notably the widespread adoption of mechanization (Belton et al., 2021), and the increased application of fertilizers and pesticides. However, the over-intensive utilization of cultivated land has brought great environmental challenges, and sustainable utilization has become a key change in the transformation of the SIU. Agricultural modernization has promoted the intensive utilization of cultivated land (Huttunen, 2019), but it has also been accompanied by severe non-point source pollution (Zhang et al., 2021). The above studies suggest that the SIU of cultivated land encompasses multiple dimensions, including the rationalization of input factors, the scaling up of land utilization, the efficiency of grain output, and the environmental friendliness. Current research related to the SIU mainly focuses on level measurement and driving factor analysis. In the level measurement, scholars often develop an indicator evaluation system for assessing the SIU. The commonly used methods include data envelopment analysis (Gadanakis et al., 2015), stochastic frontier analysis (Deng and Gibson, 2019), and super-efficiency models (Thi Hoa Sen et al., 2024). Although the selection of indicators is relatively rich, few studies have examined the connotation and mechanisms of the SIU of cultivated land considering the whole process of inputs-utilization-outputs. In terms of driving analysis, social and economic factors have attracted widespread attention, such as changes in age structure (Zulu et al., 2021), economic agglomeration (Hou et al., 2023), and environmental pressure (Scherer et al., 2018). Some scholars have also proposed that the SIU of cultivated land is closely related to changes in agricultural policies (Faye et al., 2025), but empirical studies on this issue remain limited.

Agricultural subsidy policy is usually considered an important policy instrument for increasing farmers' income and promoting agricultural modernization (Walls et al., 2018). China's No.1 Central Document explicitly proposed the strategic goal of enhancing agricultural mechanization. The agricultural machinery purchase subsidy (AMPS) was launched as a pilot program in 66 counties across 16 provinces. By 2009, the subsidized areas were gradually expanded to all counties. The funding for AMPS has also grown rapidly, increasing from 0.7 billion yuan in 2004 to 21.2 billion yuan by 2022 (an increase of more than 300 times). AMPS has drawn significant attention from scholars due to the expansion of its scope and the clarity of its target (Xu et al., 2024). Existing studies have shown that AMPS has remarkable effects on enhancing grain production capacity (Bai et al., 2022), increasing farmers' income (Jena and Tanti, 2023), and reshaping household production behavior (Zou et al., 2020). Peng et al. (2022) further found that the subsidy policy has accelerated the development of agricultural machinery socialization services (AMSS) in China. The research of (Guo et al., 2021) has shown that expanding the scope and intensity of AMPS is expected to reduce the inputs of highly polluting production factors such as pesticides and fertilizers. This suggests that AMPS may drive the transition of intensive land use from relying on high-pollution chemical inputs to a more labor-efficient and sustainable production model. International experiences also provide supporting evidence. Agricultural subsidies in EU countries have contributed to widespread agricultural intensification (Pe'er et al., 2019). Although Japan's direct agricultural subsidies have a limited impact on the sustainable utilization of land, they still demonstrate a positive effects of policy interventions (Takayama et al., 2019). Although the abovementioned studies have proved that AMPS can affect the utilization efficiency of farmland and AMSS, there remains a lack of systematic theoretical explanation and empirical testing on whether the implementation of this policy impacts the SIU through its effect on AMSS.

Overall, the relationship and pathways through which AMPS influence the SIU of cultivated land remain unclear. This may be for the following reasons: (1) Although numerous studies have discussed the institutional changes and policy effects of agricultural subsidies, most of these studies focused on the income increase of farmers and labor transfer, while overlooking the production-oriented role of AMPS. The effects and mechanisms of AMPS on cultivated land use system have received little attention. (2) The SIU of cultivated land has rarely been analyzed within a comprehensive framework that considers the whole process of inputs-utilization-outputs. Existing measurement approaches are mostly confined to the analysis of observed indicators, which makes it difficult to reflect the connotation of the SIU of cultivated land. Give this, based on elasticity theory, we examined the mechanism by which AMPS influences the SIU. The assessment of county-level SIU of cultivated land in the Huang-Huai-Hai Plain was conducted using emergy analysis. Furthermore, we taken a two-way fixed effects model to investigate the impact and pathways through which AMPS influences the SIU. The purposes of this study are as follows: (1) To conceptualize the SIU of cultivated land from a systematic and integrative perspective and to construct a comprehensive measurement framework; (2) To analyze the mechanism by which AMPS influences the SIU of cultivated land and to empirically test the policy effects and transmission pathways.

The innovation of this research is that it broadens the perspective on agricultural policy evaluation. This study starts from the nature of AMPS as a production subsidy. It links AMPS to the SIU of cultivated land, revealing the pathways through which AMPS promotes the SIU. Specifically, first, based on elasticity theory, this study analyzed the mediating role of AMSS in the connection between AMPS and the SIU of cultivated land. It explored the positive effect of AMPS on the SIU from theoretical, hypothetical, and empirical perspectives. By clarifying the intrinsic logic of AMPS affecting cultivated land use, the study makes a significant addition to evaluating the policy effects of agricultural production subsidies on cultivated land use. Second, this research defined the connotation of the SIU of cultivated land, and constructed an evaluation framework for the SIU by emergy analysis, which enriched the theoretical foundation of the cultivated land utilization system. This research enriches both the theoretical analysis and empirical study of agricultural subsidy policies and cultivated land utilization. It also provides empirical evidence for improving agricultural subsidy policies, enhancing the SIU levels, and advancing China's agricultural modernization.

## 2. Theoretical mechanisms

## 2.1. Concept of the SIU of cultivated land

The SIU of cultivated land incorporates the connotations of both sustainability and intensification (Areal et al., 2018). The SIU is not simply the sum of sustainability and intensification. Therefore, when assessing the level of the SIU, we should analyze the dynamic process from the whole process of inputs-utilization-outputs (Fig. 1).

The inputs for cultivated land utilization include both environmental resources and socioeconomic resources. Due to regional differences, environmental resource endowments vary between regions. The inputs of economic and social resources are gradually adapting to the needs of the SIU of cultivated land. Agricultural operators allocate and utilize natural resource endowments and anthropogenic input factors through production activities. Cultivated land output includes both expected outputs and non-expected outputs. Expected outputs are the ultimate purpose of cultivated land system inputs and utilization, and include food, vegetables, and oilseeds. Non-expected outputs manifest primarily as negative externalities to the environment.

The concept of the SIU of cultivated land is defined as: changing the cultivated land utilization pattern by increasing the inputs of efficient and green production factors, with the aim of improving the utilization ratio of natural resources (URNR) and the utilization ratio of production factors (URPF) under the constraint of reducing the environmental load pressure (ELP), so as to maximize the emergy value productivity (EVP) of the cultivated land system.

## 2.2. Impact mechanism of AMPS on the SIU of cultivated land

AMPS affects the AMSS market by changing the supply and demand relationship for agricultural machinery, which subsequently influences URNR, URPF, EVP, and ELP. Based on supply and demand theory (Marshall, 1920; Mondal et al., 2024)and elasticity theory (Hayami and Ruttan, 1985), this study analyzes the intrinsic mechanism by which AMPS affects the SIU by accelerating the development of the AMSS market (Fig. 2).

## 2.2.1. Theoretical mechanisms of AMPS on AMSS

According to the demand theorem, a reduction in price is expected to result in an increase in demand. AMPS is implemented as a fixed-rate subsidy, with a subsidy ratio of approximately 30 % from the central financial fund. This reduces the actual purchase price paid by agricultural operators notably, particularly for large machinery. As a result, the demand from individual farmers and agricultural production organizations expanded. Meanwhile, machinery producers are expected to expand the availability of farming machines to obtain the exchange value of goods. China's basic national condition of “a large country with smallholder farming" determines that privately owned agricultural machinery resources cannot be fully utilized. Therefore, the excess agricultural machinery resources will be channeled into socialized services, and the development of the AMSS market will be gradually improved.

Elasticity measures the degree to which buyers and sellers respond to changes in market conditions (Binswanger, 1974). When the relative price is high, farmers' demand for agricultural machinery is more sensitive to price changes. This means that demand is relatively elastic. When the relative price is low, farmers' demand for agricultural machinery is affected less by the price change, resulting in inelastic demand. Due to the implementation of AMPS, the relative price of agricultural machinery has experienced a continuous decline. This has caused the demand elasticity to shift from highly elastic to unitary elastic, and finally to inelastic (Fig. 3a). When the demand is highly elastic, increasing the supply of agricultural machinery services, which means moving the supply from point A to point B, will lead to an increase in the provider's revenue (quantity × relative price) (Fig. 3b). As a result, the agricultural machinery services provider will increase the supply. When the demand is inelastic, increasing the supply of agricultural machinery services will result in a decrease in the provider's revenue (Fig. 3c), and the agricultural machinery services provider will reduce the supply. As rational economic agents, agricultural machinery services providers will stabilize the quantity of agricultural machinery around the unitary elastic price in the long-term market. At this point, the AMSS market is more fully developed.

## 2.2.2. Theoretical mechanisms of the impact of AMSS on the SIU of cultivated land

The household contract responsibility system implemented in China led to highly decentralized land use (Ye et al., 2024). The unique nature of cultivated land property rights has also made large-scale land transfer difficult to achieve (Li et al., 2018). AMSS enable large-scale production in terms of geographical space and create conditions for economies of scale. Large machinery makes a notable contribution to land consolidation and high-standard farmland construction. It helps integrate resources and thereby improve the URNR in the cultivated land utilization system.

AMSS optimizes the combination of production factors, and deepens agricultural specialization to gradually realize the industrialization of the agricultural production process. Based on the analysis of the theoretical mechanisms, the following hypotheses are proposed:

Hypothesis 1. AMPS is conducive to improving URNR, URPF, and EVP, while reducing ELP, thereby promoting the SIU of cultivated land.

Hypothesis 2. AMPS improves the SIU of cultivated land by expanding the AMSS market.

## 3. Materials and methods materials-methods

## 3.1. Study area

The Huang-Huai-Hai Plain includes major grain-producing provinces such as Hebei, Henan, and Shandong (Fig. 4). It has vast cultivated land, an average elevation of 50 m, and flat terrain. Maize and wheat are the primary crops, making it suitable for mechanized production. As a major agricultural zone in China, the Huang-Huai-Hai Plain plays an essential role in safeguarding food security. The region has implemented a number of policy strategies, such as high-standard farmland construction and land consolidation. These measures aim to transition cultivated land utilization patterns toward more efficient, greener, and more intensive practices. During this process, the scale of AMPS has continued to increase. According to publicly available data from China's provincial machinery subsidy systems, in 2024, the central government allocated 23 % of the total national AMPS funds to Hebei, Henan, and Shandong (Appendix B). The highly intensive production model and the strong implementation of agricultural policies in the Huang-Huai-Hai Plain make it a representative region for this research. The empirical research in this region also provides valuable insights for policy optimization in other agricultural areas.

## 3.2. Data sources

The research data include statistical, raster, and vector data spanning the years 2016–2023 (Table 1). Statistical data were collected at the county level, including information on AMPS funds, input-output data for cultivated land used to analyze emergy value changes, and data related to AMSS. Raster data mainly include sunshine hours, average precipitation, elevation, and land cover data, all of which were counted at the county level. The sunshine hours were used to simulate solar radiation data, based on the calculation method proposed by (Tong et al., 2005). Vector data represent the boundary of the study area.

Fig. 5 illustrates the symbolic language used to show the emergy flow. It describes the comments flow within the cultivated land use system and the structure of the system. Through the analysis of the emergy flow in each component, we define the emergy indices that reflect the SIU of cultivated land. These indices include the utilization ratio of natural resources (URNR), the utilization ratio of production factors (URPF), emergy value productivity (EVP), and the environmental load pressure (ELP). In the emergy analysis spectral diagram, the rectangle in the middle represents the boundaries and scope of cultivated land utilization system. The inner part is the main body of emergy utilization of the system. On the left side are the environmental inputs from nature to the system. On the right side are the economic and social resources inputs from human society, including renewable emergy inputs, such as labor, and non-renewable emergy inputs. The upper side of the system is the expected outputs produced by factor inputs and utilization. The lower side is the non-expected outputs, expressed as negative externalities to the environment. Materials and emergy generate exchanges between the cultivated land, human activities, and the ecological environment. There is also a part for systematic consumption and loss during the flow of emergy values.

To measure the SIU level of cultivated land, we constructed four indicators that capture production factor flow efficiency, input rationality, land productivity improvement, and ecological sustainability. URNR is the emergy value ratio of total outputs to environmental resource input, reflecting the level of resource-saving utilization in cultivated land use. URPF is the emergy value ratio of expected outputs to the socio-economic resource input in the system, reflecting the intensification of production factors in agricultural management. EVP is the emergy value ratio of total outputs to total inputs, reflecting the overall output efficiency level of cultivated land. ELP is the emergy value ratio of non-renewable resource input to the renewable resource input, reflecting the ecological friendliness level.

## 3.4. Variable description and descriptive statistics

The dependent variable is the SIU of cultivated land, represented by URNR, URPF, EVP and ELP.

The core explanatory variable is AMPS. We use AMPS funds per unit of cultivated land as the core explanatory variable.

The theoretical analysis proposed that AMPS affects the SIU of cultivated land by influencing AMSS. The main crops in the study area are maize and wheat, and activities such as deep plowing, sowing, and harvesting are mostly carried out using large and medium-sized agricultural tractors and combine harvesters obtained through AMSS. We used mechanized plowing area (M1), mechanized sowing area (M2), and mechanized harvesting area (M3) per unit of cultivated land to represent the level of AMSS.

Following prior studies (Liang et al., 2022; Fan et al., 2023), we selected some control variables related to the subsidy policy and cultivated land utilization. These included variables associated with socioeconomic resource endowments, such as urbanization rate (UR), number of agricultural laborers (NAL), gross output value of the primary industry (GOPI), rural residents' per capita disposable income (RRDI), and grain yield (GY). Additionally, average precipitation (AP) was included to control for the impact of natural conditions. Multicollinearity tests were conducted on all variables. All variance inflation factors (VIFs) were less than 5. The statistical information of the variables is shown in Table 2.

## 4.2. Analysis of the impact of AMPS on the SIU of cultivated land

## 4.2.1. Base regression

In testing the effect of AMPS on the SIU as proposed in Hypothesis 1, we used the special mixed OLS (LSDV) method and the fixed effects (FE) method for the base regression. In Table 3, the regression analysis demonstrates that, after controlling for other factors, the coefficients of AMPS on URNR, URPF, and EVP are 0.156, 0.087, and 0.037, respectively, and –0.003 for ELP. All of these passed the significance test. This indicates that AMPS improves URNR, URPF, and EVP significantly in the cultivated land system, and they also reduce ELP. Hypothesis 1 is, therefore verified. The regression results prove that AMPS has promoted the SIU of cultivated land. This demonstrates the practical potential of AMPS in improving the URNR, URPF, and EVP, while also reducing the ELP.

## 4.2.2. Robustness checks

We verified the reliability of the regression results through robustness checks including the instrumental variable method, variable replacements, and changes in quantitative scales.

We collected the number of approved applications for AMPS in each county and used it as an instrumental variable. The instrumental variable was linked to the core explanatory variable and satisfied the exogeneity requirement regarding the dependent variables. The coefficient for the instrumental variable to the core explanatory variable is 0.434. This suggests that the instrumental variable has strong explanatory power for the core explanatory variable. Table 4 shows regression results combining the instrumental variables. The Kleibergen-Paap rk LM statistic characterizes whether the instrumental variables can be used to identify endogeneity in the regression analysis. The Kleibergen-Paap rk Wald F statistic serves as a measure to evaluate whether the selected instrumental variables exert substantial explanatory influence on the independent variables. As the results indicate, both statistics are above the critical value (16.38) at the 10 % significance level. This confirms that the number of approved applications for AMPS in each county serves as a valid instrumental variable to address the endogeneity problem. After controlling for endogeneity, the regression coefficients of AMPS on URNR, URPF, EVP and ELP were 0.412, 0.152, 0.063 and —0.009, all of which were statistically significant. It indicates that AMPS still increases URNR, URPF, and EVP significantly while reducing ELP, and Hypothesis 1 is supported again.

## 5. Discussion

Our study defines an evaluation framework for the SIU of cultivated land, and reveals the effect of AMPS in promoting the SIU. It also explores the pathways of impact through AMSS within China's smallholder economy. The study enriches the theoretical foundation and empirical evidence regarding the interplay between agricultural subsidy policies and cultivated land utilization. Although the findings demonstrate the practical significance of AMPS, there are still some issues that need to be further discussed.

## 5.2. Role of AMPS policies in promoting the SIU of cultivated land

Our empirical results demonstrate that AMPS influences the SIU of cultivated land by expanding the AMSS market. The positive impact of AMPS on URNR is the largest, indicating that agricultural mechanization significantly increases the utilization of natural resource. AMSS enhance URPF and EVP by saving labor. This is also confirmed by Belton et al. (2021). AMPS have promoted the use of large-scale machinery, and have reduced inputs of chemical agricultural products. Ning et al. (2022) confirmed this view in their study. They believe that agricultural machinery is a more advanced and environmentally friendly agricultural technology medium. The increase in machinery use driven by AMPS will effectively promote sustainable agricultural development. Other studies have also shown that AMPS have facilitated the development of agricultural technologies, especially in areas with higher land use intensification (Jiang et al., 2023).

China's "big country, smallholder economy" prevents Chinese farmers from purchasing large-scale agricultural machinery as farmers in Europe and the United States do. Otsuka (2012) pointed out that the average cultivated land plot size in China is only 1/3 of that in Japan. The first step to achieve efficient mechanization is a significant expansion of farm size. Therefore, agricultural production in China will go through a long period of low growth in cultivated land use efficiency. However, it has been demonstrated that China has already experienced rapid agricultural mechanization, largely due to the division of labor effects brought about by AMSS. In the early stages of implementing AMPS, EU countries encouraged the promotion of agricultural socialization services through various cooperative organizations. It accelerated the intensive development of agriculture (Lasanta et al., 2017). This offers valuable insights for China. Although China's smallholder economy limits the URNR and URPF to some extent, AMSS has made moderate-scale farming and agricultural modernization a reality. In contrast, countries with distinct smallholder economies, such as Ghana (Houssou et al., 2013), Indonesia (Paman et al., 2016), and Bangladesh (Mottaleb et al., 2017), have seen slower development of agricultural socialization services. As a result, the enhancement in cultivated land use efficiency driven by AMPS has been relatively limited. AMPS have accelerated the market development of AMSS, thereby expanding the benefits of subsidy policies and becoming an important pathway to connect small farmers with agricultural technologies (Sims and Kienzle, 2016). Farmers benefit from technological innovations through leasing services, which promote the SIU of cultivated land collectively. Although our study was conducted in China, it offers valuable policy insights for countries undergoing the transformation to an intensive farming model and agricultural modernization, especially in regions dominated by smallholder economies, such as Sub-Saharan Africa.

Although many studies have suggested that the government should increase AMPS (Xu et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2023), it remains unclear whether AMPS can fully realize their expected production value. China's AMPS has relatively improved the SIU of cultivated land, but the scale effect is limited, especially in mountainous and hilly areas. Some studies suggest that this inefficiency may be related to serious distortions of rural land markets (Meng et al., 2024). For AMPS to fully realize its potential, it needs to be coordinated with other market-oriented reform measures, such as market-based land transfer, agricultural technology innovation and promotion. China's AMPS prioritizes supporting advanced agricultural machinery. While this has promoted the large-scale development and technological innovation of agricultural machinery to some extent, the proportion of benefits to individual farmers is relatively small. As evidenced by the household-level analysis in Huang et al. (2013), only 0.5 % of the farmers in the sample received AMPS, and most of the subsidies went to land contractors and AMSS organizations. This situation poses risks: first, the increase in transaction costs of AMSS, and second, the difficulty in ensuring the operational quality of AMSS due to a lack of supervision over production activities. The existence of these problems is rather detrimental to the SIU of

cultivated land in China.

## 5.3. Implications and recommendations for agricultural subsidy policies

It seems to be an agricultural paradox that China's smallholder economy model has been accompanied by a large number of laborers leaving agricultural production, while grain output has increased year after year. In fact, the growth of grain output is strongly linked to the intensive use of cultivated land. Our study demonstrates that AMPS has transformed China's cultivated land utilization towards a more intensive, efficient, and environmentally sustainable model. This aligns with the purpose of land consolidation and high-standard farmland construction currently underway. Coordinating AMPS with broader agricultural policies and reform measures will make a multiplier effect on cultivated land utilization efficiency. Based on these findings and comparative discussions, we propose the following recommendations.

First, increase the funding for AMPS and optimize the subsidy standards. Currently, the amount of subsidies agricultural machinery in China still has considerable potential for increase when compared to developed countries. While increasing the central financial support, it is necessary to enhance the financial support of provincial and municipal governments. Subsidy standards should be adjusted flexibly. For mountainous and hilly areas with poor mechanized farming conditions, the subsidy for small agricultural machinery should be appropriately increased to enhance the URNR. For major agricultural production areas suitable for mechanized production, the focus should be on the research and development of agricultural machinery. Subsidies for large agricultural machinery and innovative technologies should be expanded to meet the needs of large-scale operations and improve the SIU of cultivated land. For ecologically fragile areas with inefficient land utilization, subsidies for agricultural machinery used in land leveling, pollution control, and other related tasks should be increased to reduce the ELP.

Second, moderately promote the market development of agricultural machinery and improve market supervision mechanisms. The AMSS market should be continuously developed and expanded while simultaneously strengthening its organization, specialization, and scale. AMSS providers should be encouraged to form healthy competition, which can help reduce transaction costs and promote technological innovation. At the same time, the government should prioritize addressing the decline in mechanization efficiency resulting from the opportunistic actions of AMSS providers. Relevant management regulations and reward-and-penalty mechanisms for socialized services should be improved accordingly.

Third, promote the integration of subsidy policies with key agricultural development initiatives to fully leverage the productionsupporting function of AMPS. Although AMPS significantly promote the SIU of cultivated land, this positive effect can only be maximized through coordination with other agricultural policies. Therefore, there is a need to further strengthen policy synergistic and ensure the efficient provision of AMPS and AMSS in the areas of large-scale farming, marketbased land transfer, and green agricultural transformation. By forming coordinated policy efforts, the long-term supporting role of AMPS in promoting the SIU of cultivated land can be better leveraged.

## 5.4. Research limitations and perspectives

Although this study contributes to the evaluation of agricultural policy effects on cultivated land use, it also has certain limitations. First, our case study area is limited in scope, lacking comparisons across different climatic zones, terrain conditions, and crop types. The Huang-Huai-Hai Plain has favorable conditions for mechanized farming and a well-developed market for AMSS. Therefore, the positive effect of AMPS on the SIU is more evident. However, in some mountainous areas with a wide distribution of economic crops, the policy effects of AMPS may be less significant. Therefore, in future research, we plan to select more case study areas covering a wider range of farming conditions and cultivation patterns, so as to comprehensively assess the heterogeneity of agricultural policy effects on cultivated land use. Such efforts will provide more meaningful insights for formulating agricultural development policies according to local conditions.

Second, although we controlled for a series of variables and conducted multiple robustness checks, confirming the mediating role of AMSS, there may be other transmission pathways from the perspective of individual farmers. Moreover, it remains unclear whether AMPS in China is highly concentrated in agricultural mechanization organizations. If so, such a concentration of subsidies may weaken farmers initiative in making decisions about cultivated land use under the influence of agricultural policies. This potential issue deserves further investigation. Therefore, in future research, we plan to carry out extensive field surveys to assess the applicability and effectiveness of agricultural subsidy policies on cultivated land use from the micro perspective of farmers.

## 6. Conclusion

This study constructed an evaluation framework for the SIU of cultivated land from an inputs-utilization-outputs perspective using emergy analysis. Based on elasticity theory, this research examined the impact mechanism of AMPS on the SIU of cultivated land and employed a two-way fixed effects model to clarify its effects and transmission pathways. The key findings are as follows: (1) The regional differences in the SIU of cultivated land in the Huang-Huai-Hai Plain have been narrowing year by year. Trends of URPF, EVP, and ELP indicators are relatively consistent, suggesting that the SIU process of cultivated land reduces ELP. However, it also prevents the improvement of URPF and EVP. (2) Empirical results from the baseline regressions reveal that AMPS exerts a positive and significant influence on the URNR, URPF, and EVP indicators, while demonstrating a negative and significant impact on the ELP indicator. Furthermore, these findings remain robust after undergoing a series of rigorous testing procedures, including instrumental variable tests, variable substitutions, and changes in data scale. (3) After adding AMSS as mechanism variables to the basic regression, the coefficients for AMPS on the SIU of cultivated land decrease. This indicates that AMSS plays a positive mediating role between AMPS and the SIU.

This study examined the impact mechanism of AMPS, which is the most goal-oriented and direct subsidy method, on the level of the SIU. The study not only offered innovative perspectives for advancing the SIU but also formulated targeted agricultural policy recommendations. Subsequent studies should focus on the characteristics of the transformation of the SIU pattern driven by agricultural policy implementation to assess the effectiveness and sustainability of agricultural policies and provide policy references for future food security and ecological protection.
