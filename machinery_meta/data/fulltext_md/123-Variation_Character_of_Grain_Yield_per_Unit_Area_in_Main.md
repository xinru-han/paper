# Variation Character of Grain Yield per Unit Area in Main Grain-producing Area of Northeast China

CHENG Yeqing1, 2, 3, ZHANG Pingyu², ZHANG Huimin²

(1. Institute of Geographic Sciences and Natural Resources Research, Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing 100101, China; 2. Northeast Institute of Geography and Agroecology, Chinese Academy of Sciences, Changchun 130012, China; 3. Graduate University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing 100049, China)

Abstract: Based on the surveys and the statistic data during 1980–2003, the variation character of grain yield per unit area in Northeast China and its main factors have been discussed by the methods of statistics and grey correlation analysis. The results show that: 1) the grain yield per unit area has been taking on an increasing trend in the recent 20 years. It increased from 2519.80kg/ha in 1980 to 4216.11kg/ha in 2003, with an increasing rate of 67.32%; 2) the variation of grain yield per unit area is considerably prominent and its range is also very great, with the maximal increase rate of 42.59% and maximal decrease rate of 21.13%, respectively, which are far above the whole Chinese average level; 3) the variation of main crops' yield per unit area is remarkable, which takes on the character that the yield of corn is much higher than that of soybean and rice; and 4) the grey correlation analysis shows that the most important factors impacting the variation of grain yield per unit area are the total power of agricultural machinery, the consumption of chemical fertilizer and effective irrigated area. However, the influence of natural disaster and income level should not be ignored. Effective ways to improve grain yield per unit area are to construct farmland improvement groundwork, reclaim the middle- and low-yield farmland, etc.

Keywords: grain yield per unit area; grey correlation analysis; Northeast China; main grain-producing area

## 1 Introduction

Grain security, which is one of the global issues that have attracted a lot of attention internationally, is not only the central issue of agricultural development, but also the basic issue in relation to the national economy and social development. Being a country of over a billion population, China's grain production and grain security attracts more and more attention worldwide. Grain security refers to the accessibility of sufficient food for all people at all times for an active and healthy life (Shi et al., 1999). This means that the world must produce enough food to ensure quantitative security and stabilize grain supply to a maximal extent. Furthermore, it must ensure that anybody can buy healthful food to ensure purchase and nutritional security. As we all know, China is a country with huge grain consumption. The grain security condition of China has an important impact on the world's grain security pattern. Under the situations of the country's water and land resources, socio-economic conditions, and macroscopical background of the world's grain security, ensuring the quantity of grain in China for a long period is still the leading issue (Zhang et al., 2004). Related study results showed that the variation of China's grain yield is mainly attributed to the improvement of grain yield per unit area (Dang et al., 1999; Lin, 1995). With the rapid increase of land use for non-agricultural purposes because of industrialization and urbanization, China's cultivated lands have been decreasing dramatically in recent years. Additionally, affected by grain production benefits and agricultural structure adjustment, the proportion of sown area of grain to crop is also decreasing. According to this trend, the sown area of grain shall decrease by 22% in 2030 and 34% in 2050, which shall have a great impact on China's grain security (Cai and Chen, 2004).

Variation and regional differences of China's grain yield per unit area have attracted much attention in the academe (Zhong and Xing, 2004; Lu et al., 2005; Liu et al., 2001; Cheng et al., 1997; Wang et al., 1993; Xie and Liu, 1998). However, most studies paid more attention to regional differences and reasons and rules of variation in grain yield per unit area in the national scale, and few have focused on the main grain-producing area. In this paper, changes of grain yield per unit area in the regional scale will be discussed by taking Northeast China as a case study and combining statistical data from each of the provinces of Northeast China with the help of grey correlation analysis. The purpose of this paper is to provide scientific reference for making policies of grain production and development. Especially, we hope to achieve two objectives: 1) to discover the characteristics and the main factors of variation in grain yield per unit area in Northeast China; and 2) to discuss development orientation of grain yield per unit area and to put forward suggestions on how to improve comprehensive production ability of grain in Northeast China.

## 2 Methodology

## 2.1 Study area

Northeast China is located between 3843'-53°30'N and 115°30'–13520'E, which covers the three provinces of Jilin, Liaoning and Heilongjiang, with an area of about 789× 103km². Its cultivated land per capita was about 0.2ha in 2003, which was twice as much as the country's average level (0.1ha).

Northeast China is a very important commodity grain base in China with abundant water, land resources, and high grain yields. The cultivated land area and the average grain yield were about 16.6% and 14.6% respectively of China's total in 2003, and the per capita grain yield was 266kg higher than the country's average level, but the grain yield per unit area (4216.11kg/ha) was 84.40kg/ha lower than the national average. There were 13.748× 106ha of middle- and low-yield farmlands in Northeast China, which accounted for about 64.6% of the total cultivated lands in 2003.

## 2.2 Data source

The original sources of grain yield and plantation area in this paper are from the China Statistical Yearbooks (National Bureau of Statistics of China, 1981–2004), Statistical Yearbooks of Jilin (Statistical Bureau of Jilin Province, 1981–2004), Statistical Yearbooks of Liaoning (Statistical Bureau of Liaoning Province, 1981–2004)

and Statistical Yearbooks of Heilongjiang (Statistical Bureau of Heilongjiang Province, 1981–2004). The total power of agricultural machinery, effective irrigation area, consumption of chemical fertilizers, and purchasing price indices of grain and other indicators were all taken into consideration in this paper. Almost all of the data were collected from the above yearbooks. The surveyed data's accuracy and reliability are widely accepted.

## 2.3 Methods

By statistical methods, variation rates of grain yield per unit area year after year were calculated. Furthermore, frequency and times of fluctuation of grain yield per unit area were also calculated.

Grey correlation analysis is a very effective method to reveal the relative multi-factors. In this paper, it was used to diagnose the relationship between grain yield per unit area and factors such as total power of agricultural machinery, consumption of chemical fertilizers, effective irrigation area, purchasing price indicies of grain, per capita net income of rural households, etc.

## 3 Variation of Grain Yield per Unit Area

## 3.1 Variation trend

Northeast China is one of the regions with the greatest potential for increasing grain yield in China due to its abundant natural resources and advantageous agricultural production conditions. It also has lots of middle- and low-yield farmlands in the provinces of Liaoning, Jilin and Heilongjiang, which occupy about 63.85%, 60.15% and 67.05% of cultivated farmlands, respectively. With the comprehensive exploitation of agriculture, reconstruction of middle- and low-yield lands, as well as popularization of agricultural technology and new varieties, the grain yield per unit area of Northeast China has increased greatly since the 1980s, which takes on an increasing trend with variation characteristics by and large (Fig. 1).

From Fig. 1, it can be seen that the grain yield per unit area in Northeast China increased from 2519.80kg/ha in 1980 to 5084.62kg/ha in 1998, which was 2.02 times as much as that of 1980. However, affected by agricultural natural disasters and other factors, grain yield per unit area has decreased dramatically in the recent five years, which was 4216.11kg/ha in 2003, being 17.1% lower than that in 1998. As a whole, grain yield per unit area increased by 67.32%, and the total yield increased 78.95% from 1980 to 2003. The variation in grain yield per unit area can be divided into three stages: 1) The first stage is from 1980 to 1989, when the grain yield per unit area varied from 2519.80kg/ha to 2931.25kg/ha in Northeast China, and 2734.36kg/ha to 3578.35kg/ha of the whole country, having average increasing rates of about 1.63% and 3.09%, respectively. However, grain yield per unit area in Northeast China was lower than that of the whole country. 2) The second stage is from 1990 to 1999, when the grain yield per unit area in Northeast China increased rapidly at a rate of about 6.36%, which was 3.99% higher than that of the whole country. Furthermore, Northeast China's grain yield per unit area had always been higher than the national average level. 3) The third stage is from 2000 to 2003, grain yield per unit area in Northeast China was lower than the national average level, but its increasing rate was 2.13% higher than that of the whole country.

## 3.3 Variation of dominated crop yield per unit area

Northeast China has been abundant in soybean, wheat and broomcorn throughout history. Corn, which developed rapidly in the last 30 years, has become one of the most important crops in Northeast China, and the pattern of grain production with corn, soybean and rice in domination has come into being (Cheng and He, 2005). Because of the regional difference of agricultural conditions, the variation characteristics of corn, soybean and rice in yield per unit area are also different. Corn yield per area increased from 3373.14kg/ha in 1980 to 5483.00kg/ha in 2003, soybean from 1258.61kg/ha to 1843.02kg/ha, and rice from 4974.09kg/ha to 6480.50 kg/ha, which increased by 57.70%, 47.44% and 40.82%, respectively. Their variation ranges were also great in 1980–2003 (Fig. 3).

## 4 Main Socioeconomic Factors Impacting Variation of Grain Yield per Unit Area

Grain yield per unit area is affected not only by natural factors, such as climate, water and land resources, but also by socioeconomic factors, such as agricultural technology, policy and market conditions, which are mainly embodied in seeds, water sources, fertilizers, soil fertility, and labor force, etc. In this paper, grey correlation analysis is used to reflect the relationship between the grain yield per unit area and the influencing factors.

Eight key factors, which include total power of agricultural machinery (PAM), consumption of chemical fertilizer (CCF), effective irrigated area (EIA), purchasing price indices of grain (PPG), and price indicies of agricultural means production (PAP), electricity consumed in rural area (ECR), areas affected (AA), and per capita net income of rural households, (PIH), were selected in qualitative analysis. Correlative degree between the grain yield per unit area and the eight factors were calculated by grey correlation analysis (Wang et al., 1989). The correlative degree and correlative order are listed in Table 3.

Northeast China covers the Songnen Plain, Sanjiang Plain and the Liaohe Plain, which have better cultivated conditions, more per capita cultivated lands and an appropriate scale of land management. All of them are favorable to the development of agricultural mechanization. The total power of agricultural machinery kept an increasing trend by and large, which increased from 16.456×106kW in 1980 to 45.806×106kW in 2003, and increased by 1.78 times. Agricultural mechanization level has great impact on grain yield per unit area, reflected in the result of the grey correlation analysis, which states that the correlative degree between the total power of agricultural machinery and the grain yield per unit area is considerably high, but takes on a decreasing trend from 0.9392 in the 1980s to 0.8849 in the early 21st century. On the other hand, their correlative order decreased from the first place to the fifth.

Consumption of chemical fertilizer also has great impact on grain yield per unit area. With the depression of marginal revenues of cultivated lands, the marginal reward rate of per unit consumption of chemical fertilizer also decreased. The correlative degree of consumption of chemical fertilizer and the grain yield per unit area changed from 0.8582 in the 1980s to 0.9680 in the 1990s and 0.9070 in the early 21st, respectively, and the correlative order also changed from the third place to the first, then to the fourth.

With the development of farmland water conservancy works, the effective irrigated area enlarged from 2.16×106ha in 1980 to 5.17×106ha in 2003, which increased about one time, but the correlative degree decreased from 0.8948 in the 1980s to 0.8699 in the early 21st century, with the correlative order decreasing from the second place to the seventh. The influence of farmland water conservancy works on the grain yield per unit area has weakened during these years.

Additionally, the influence of electricity consumed in rural areas on the grain yield per unit area takes on an increasing trend, and has become one of the most important factors influencing the variation of grain yield per unit area. The correlative degree increased from 0.6930 in the 1980s to 0.8890 in the 1990s, and then 0.9141 in the early 21st century, and the correlative order also increased from the seventh place to the second.

## 4.2 Price factors

Northeast China is a main grain-producing area in China and the price factors have had great impact on the grain yield per unit area all through these years. The correlative degree between the purchasing price indices of grain and the grain yield per unit area increased from 0.8559 in the 1980s to 0.9141 in the 1990s, and then 0.9215 in the early 21st century, and the correlative order increased from the fourth place to the first. In recent years, with the decrease of grain purchase price, farmers’ enthusiasm for grain production has been declining. The purchasing price indices of grain have become the uppermost factor among the eight factors.

The influence of price indicies of agricultural mean production on grain yield per unit area also takes on an increasing trend, and the correlative order increased from the fifth place in the 1980s to the third in the early 21st century.

## 4.3 Natural disasters

Natural disasters and farmers' income level are also important factors influencing the variation of grain yield per unit area in Northeast China, but their roles are less than the other factors. The correlative degree between areas affected and the grain yield per unit area increased from 0.7743 in the 1980s to 0.7842 in the 1990s, and then 0.8738 in the early 21st century, but the correlative order decreased from the sixth place to the eighth, and then got back to the sixth.

## 4.4 Income level

The correlative degree between the per capita net income of rural households and the variation of grain yield per unit area increased from 0.5254 in the 1980s to 0.8538 in the 1990s, and then decreased to 0.8206 in the early 21st century, but the correlative order increased from the eighth place to the seventh, and then got back to the eighth.

## 5 Conclusions

(1) The grain yield per unit area takes on a fluctual increasing trend by and large in Northeast China, with an annual increasing rate of 2.87%. The variation range of grain yield per unit area in Northeast China is greater than that of the national average. Additionally, the variation range of corn yield per unit area is greater than that of soybean and rice in the main crops.

(2) The result of the grey correlation analysis indicates that the foremost factors impacting the variation of grain yield per unit area, such as the total power of agricultural machinery, the consumption of chemical fertilizer, effective irrigated area, and the influence of price factors, have been increasing in recent years. The influence of natural disasters and income levels on the variation of grain yield per unit area, however, cannot be ignored.

Being one of the important grain-producing area, Northeast China plays an important role in the strategy of ensuring China's food security. How to make Northeast China become the biggest grain production base and security-ensuring base is a huge scientific issue. With the development of urbanization and industrialization, the demand of non-agriculture land use increases as a whole. Additionally, with agricultural structure adjustment and policy implementation of converting farmlands to forests and grassland, the enlargement of cultivated lands is limited, so it is not a long-term effective strategy to increase grain yields by increasing sown area. We must promote the grain yield per unit area by improving the agriculture production conditions, enhancing the defenses against natural disasters, constructing agricultural infrastructure, and reconstructing the middle- and low-yield farmlands. The final purpose is to improve comprehensive grain productivity and to ensure grain security ability by improving grain yield per unit area.
