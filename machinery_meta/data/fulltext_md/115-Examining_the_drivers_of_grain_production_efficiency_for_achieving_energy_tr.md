## ABSTRACT

Our research examines the critical considerations of driving factors that contribute to eco-efficiency in grain production as a pivotal component of China's energy transition. As China strives to align agricultural practices with sustainable energy objectives, it becomes imperative to identify and understand the key determinants that impact eco-efficiency in grain production. This research unveils the intricate dynamics at play within the intersection of grain production and China's energy transition. It employs super-SBM-windows-DEA model, which accounts for unexpected outputs, to assess the eco-efficiency of grain production across China's 31 provinces spanning the years from 1998 to 2021. Furthermore, it delves into the spatiotemporal evolution characteristics, employing the panel Tobit model to dissect the mechanisms governing various influencing factors. Currently, China's grain production eco-efficiency registers at a relatively low level. The study identifies a pattern where major grain-consuming areas exhibit the highest efficiency, followed by major grain-producing areas, and the lowest efficiency is observed in regions where grain production and consumption are in balance. Over the decade from 1998 to 2021, China's grain production eco-efficiency follows a fluctuating pattern of increase, decrease, and increase, indicative of a generally stable overall trend. The spatial distribution of grain production eco-efficiency showcases a pattern of higher concentrations in peripheral areas and lower concentrations in central areas, and the spatial correlation reveals a randomly dispersed distribution. Several factors wield significant influence: economic development level, agricultural production structure, urban-rural income gap, and labor-to-planting area ratio all exert significant positive effects on grain production eco-efficiency. Conversely, disaster level and replanting index are found to significantly inhibit grain production ecoefficiency. These findings are valuable for policymakers, agricultural stakeholders, and environmental advocates seeking to steer China toward a more sustainable and energy-efficient grain production landscape.

## 1. Introduction

China's ambitious energy transition initiatives have gained global attention as the nation endeavors to transition toward a more sustainable and environmentally responsible future (Devi et al., 2022; Wu and Han, 2022; Liu et al., 2023). Critical to this endeavor is the central sector of grain production, a cornerstone of China's agricultural landscape and food security (Qi et al., 2021a). As China grapples with the challenges associated with meeting the demands of a burgeoning population while addressing environmental concerns and resource constraints, the efficiency and sustainability of grain production play a pivotal role in achieving these dual objectives (Song and Chen, 2019; Zhang et al., 2021).

In this context, the concept of eco-efficiency, which encapsulates the sensible use of resources to produce goods and services while minimizing environmental impacts, has emerged as a central paradigm (Czyzewski et al., 2020; Stepien et al., 2021). Within the broader context of China's energy transition, it becomes increasingly imperative to understand the driving factors that influence eco-efficiency in grain production (Bauman et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2022). This offers the potential to uncover valuable insights and strategies that can simultaneously enhance the sustainability of grain production and contribute to the overarching energy transition goals. This research embarks on a comprehensive exploration of the multifaceted considerations that shape eco-efficiency in grain production within the Chinese context. By delving into the intricate interplay of technological innovations, policy measures, environmental regulations, and agricultural practices, this paper provides a holistic understanding of how these driving factors impact the sustainability and energy efficiency of grain production in China. The insights extracted from this analysis hold the potential to inform policymakers, agricultural stakeholders, and environmentalists alike, thus contributing to the formulation of strategies that foster a more sustainable, efficient, and environmentally responsible grain production landscape in alignment with China's energy transition objectives.

Ecological efficiency, encompassing optimal yield while minimizing the ecological footprint, has drawn substantial attention from researchers due to its critical relevance to regional and national development (Tsionas and Tzeremes, 2022). Scholars have delved into agricultural eco-efficiency since the 1990s, exploring various aspects such as indicator systems (Coluccia et al., 2020; Desli et al., 2021; Liao et al., 2021), measurement methods (Huang et al., 2014; Afrinaldi, 2022; Cheng et al., 2023), and influential factors (Liu et al., 2020a; Czyzewski et al., 2021; Yang et al., 2021), yielding fruitful results. Regarding indicator systems, the primary current debate centers on the selection of indicators for non-desired outputs. Commonly employed indicators in agriculture to assess non-desired outputs include Total Nitrogen (TN), Total Phosphorus (TP), and Chemical Oxygen Demand (COD), all of which significantly contribute to non-point source pollution in this sector (Zeng et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2021). Given the growing severity of climate change, researchers are now addressing the matter of carbon emissions (Cao et al., 2022). Integrating carbon emissions into the measurement of unexpected output in food production expands its scope, providing a comprehensive assessment of the environmental costs associated with food production and exemplifying the concept of sustainable agricultural development (Vlontzos and Pardalos, 2017; Akbar et al., 2021). Yang et al. (2022b) examine the evolutionary patterns of carbon emissions and ecological efficiency in Chinese agriculture, proposing that regions that reduce carbon emissions experience a slower rate of ecological efficiency growth.

In terms of measurement methods, there are primarily two measurement methods for assessing ecological efficiency in the existing literature. One approach is the ratio method proposed by the Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD, 1998), which assesses ecological efficiency by examining the economic performance triggered by variations in unit environmental indicators. The rationality of the ratio method is often questioned due to the reliance on experiential judgment for selecting economic and environmental performance indicators, as well as the lack of unified standards (Huang et al., 2014). An alternative approach is to measure the production frontier, which primarily involves parametric frontier methods (e.g., Stochastic Frontier Analysis - SFA) and non-parametric frontier methods (e.g., Data Envelopment Analysis - DEA). Tone (2002) introduced the super-efficiency slack-based measure (SBM) model, which incorporates two key components: a radial comparison between the target and actual values of inputs and outputs, along with the inclusion of non-radial slack variables for each input and output. This model has gained wide acceptance in the measurement of agricultural ecological efficiency (Vo Hong et al., 2015; Zhong et al., 2020; Liao et al., 2021). Deng and Gibson (2019) employed random frontier analysis to measure the agricultural ecological efficiency of various regions in Shandong, China. Using the Super-SBM model, Yang et al. (2022a) assessed the agricultural ecological efficiency of the city cluster in the middle reaches of the Yangtze River in China. Several scholars have conducted detailed analyses of the regional disparities based on the measurements of agricultural ecological efficiency. According to Liao et al. (2021), the agricultural ecological efficiency is higher in the eastern and western regions of China compared to the northeast region, whereas the central region exhibits the lowest agricultural ecological efficiency. Li et al. (2023b) compared the efficiency of grain green production in three functional areas of grain production in China and observed that the major grain-producing areas and major grain-consuming areas have significantly higher efficiency than the balanced grain production and consumption areas.

In the realm of influential factors, existing research primarily focuses on two perspectives: micro and macro. At the micro level, studies delve into the demographic characteristics of farmers, including gender, age, and education level, as well as factors like training levels, environmental regulations, and environmental awareness (Bonfiglio et al., 2017; Godoy-Duran et al., 2017; Zhang et al., 2018; Grzelak et al., 2019; Xie and Huang, 2021). Macro-level research has a broader scope with hot topics that encompass multiple dimensions including economic development, agricultural industrial structure, technological advancement, level of agricultural mechanization, and urbanization level (Ma et al., 2018; Liu et al., 2020a; Liao et al., 2021; Yang et al., 2021; Xu et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2022b). These diverse studies collectively affirm that agricultural ecological efficiency is influenced by a multitude of factors.

Our findings have highlighted three specific aspects that warrant further attention. First, extensive studies on agriculture, cultivated land, and regional ecological efficiency have been explored (Heidenreich et al., 2022; Li et al., 2023a; Luo and Wang, 2023). Nonetheless, there exists a noticeable gap in the specific focus on grain production. In a narrower context, agriculture encompasses the cultivation of various crops, including grain crops, cash crops, vegetables, fruits, and fruit trees (Zhang et al., 2022; Li et al., 2023b). Different crop categories exhibit significant variations in cultivation systems. Employing ecological efficiency within the broader planting industry to represent grain production might lead to considerable deviations from actual outcomes. Secondly, prior studies on indicators for assessing ecological efficiency have predominantly concentrated on carbon emissions while neglecting the crucial aspect of carbon absorption. Research has underscored that agricultural systems serve as both sources and sinks of carbon (Liao et al., 2021; Hashemi et al., 2022). Carbon absorption during grain production has the potential to enhance ecological efficiency to a certain degree (Liu and Yang, 2021; Yang et al., 2022b). Therefore, the approach to achieving ecological efficiency in grain production should encompass both carbon sources in the form of emissions and carbon sinks. Moreover, previous research has explored the temporal fluctuations and spatial distribution characteristics of agricultural ecological efficiency (Liao et al., 2021). However, there exists a notable absence of analysis concerning alterations in ecological efficiency within major grain-producing areas (MGPA), major grain-consuming areas (MGCA), and regions characterized by balanced grain production and consumption (BGPCA). Furthermore, the factors influencing these variations remain even more obscure.

Our research employs the super-SBM-windows-DEA model, which integrates carbon absorption and carbon emissions, to assess the grain production eco-efficiency (GPEE) in 31 provinces of China between 1998 and 2021. Additionally, the spatiotemporal evolution patterns and driving factors in MGPA, MGCA, and BGPCA of China are examined. The objective is to offer decision-makers guidance on how to enhance GPEE, diminish non-point source pollution and carbon emissions, and foster sustainable grain production within China.

Our study holds the potential to make noteworthy contributions, delineated in the following four key points: First, this study significantly advances the current body of research on agricultural ecological efficiency. It narrows its focus to cereal crops and provides more precise calculations of their ecological efficiency, taking into account their unique characteristics. Furthermore, the research findings enhance the specificity of policy recommendations, thereby augmenting their practical utility. Second, our research introduces different indicators to assess ecological efficiency, which incorporates both desired and undesired outputs. This study specifically integrates carbon sequestration as an expected output and takes into account unintended outputs such as agricultural nonpoint source pollution and carbon emissions, thereby enhancing the evaluation index system for eco-efficiency in food production. Consequently, it presents a more systematic approach to ecological efficiency assessment. Third, we specifically examine the spatial characteristics of ecological efficiency in different provinces based on functional positioning. Additionally, we analyze the ecological efficiency spatial characteristics of grain-producing areas, grain-selling areas, and areas with a balance between production and sales. Therefore, we contribute to the existing literature by unveiling the spatiotemporal distribution characteristics of ecological efficiency. Last, it analyzes factors such as economic development levels, agricultural policies, and natural conditions to discern the mechanisms through which these factors impact GPEE. The study showcases a degree of innovation, and the research findings offer valuable insights for shaping grain production policies in China and other developing nations worldwide.

## 2.1.1. Evaluation variables of GPEE

The GPEE is to optimize economic output while minimizing resource inputs. Our research enhances the conventional Cobb-Douglas production function and develops an evaluation criteria system for assessing GPEE. Input indicators. When analyzing input indicators, it is essential to consider land, capital, and labor as the primary factors (Yang et al., 2021). Land input encompasses the cultivated area dedicated to the cultivation of food crops. Capital input, on the other hand, encompasses a range of factors, including machinery, fertilizers, pesticides, agricultural film, and irrigation. Given the limited availability of statistical data encompassing the total input factors associated with grain production, this study has endeavored to enhance the accuracy of its calculations. Building upon the research conducted by Li et al. (2023b), a weight coefficient denoted as “A" has been established, with A = grain sown area / total crop sown area. This weighting coefficient “A" enables a clear distinction between capital inputs specific to grain production and those associated with agriculture as a whole. The labor input factor is defined as the number of workers involved in grain production. To further refine this factor, a weighting coefficient referred to as “B” has been introduced, calculated as B = A \* (agricultural output value / gross agricultural, forestry, animal husbandry, and fishery output value) based on the insights provided by Li et al. (2023b). This coefficient serves to distinguish laborers specifically engaged in grain production from the broader agricultural workforce.

Output indicators. Inspired by the perspective outlined by Yang et al. (2022a), this paper provides a comprehensive evaluation that encompasses economic benefits, social advantages, and environmental consequences arising from grain production. The evaluation takes into consideration both the desired and undesired outputs, culminating in a comprehensive characterization of the output indicators associated with grain production. This multifaceted assessment explores three dimensions: economic, social, and environmental outcomes. In the realm of economic outcomes, the total value of grain production serves as the primary indicator. To mitigate the influence of price fluctuations, the data was transformed into constant price output, referencing the year 1998. Recognizing the pivotal role of grain production in ensuring food security and social stability, it emerges as a pertinent indicator for social output. ## 2.3. Data sources

The study data encompasses 31 provinces in China, spanning the years from 1998 to 2021. The regions of Hong Kong and Macau were excluded due to data unavailability. The data were gathered from the China Statistical Yearbook and China Rural Statistical Yearbook for the years 1998 to 2021. Employment data for the primary industry, which are pivotal for evaluating agricultural ecological efficiency indicators, were extracted from the provincial statistical yearbooks. In instances where specific data points were absent, interpolation techniques were applied, utilizing the available foundational data to estimate the missing values. For certain equivalent factors and emission coefficients, we drew from existing literature and data from the first national pollution source census, including resources such as the Fertilizer Loss Coefficient Handbook.

## 4. Discussion and implications

## 4.1. Discussion

This study centers on China, the largest developing country globally, with a specific focus on the grain planting industry as the research subject. It establishes an index system to gauge GPEE while incorporating considerations for both carbon absorption and carbon emissions. An extensive analysis of data spanning from 1998 to 2021 across China's 31 provinces is conducted. The findings reveal an oscillating upward trend in China's GPEE throughout the study period, following a pattern of rise-fall-rise since 1998. The overall change in GPEE is relatively gradual. These findings align with those obtained by Yang et al. (2022b), whose research centered on agricultural ecological efficiency in China. However, it contrasts with the results of Liao et al. (2021), who identified a downward trend in agricultural ecological efficiency in China from 2010 to 2018. This variance in results can be attributed to the comprehensive consideration of economic, social, and environmental aspects of grain production in this study, commencing from the inputoutput structure. By integrating carbon absorption, carbon emissions, and non-point source pollution from agriculture into the calculation index system, this study provides more scientifically robust measurement results. In terms of regional trends, previous studies have often classified different provinces of China into eastern, central, and western regions, subsequently examining the ecological efficiency rankings of these regions (Liu et al., 2020a; Akbar et al., 2021). The present study, however, goes a step further by evaluating GPEE across various functional areas, offering insights into its temporal evolution patterns and delivering more targeted conclusions. The overall level of GPEE is relatively low, displaying a pattern of the highest levels in MGCA, intermediate levels in MGPA, and the lowest levels in BGPCA. These findings are consistent with those of Yang et al. (2022b). Nonetheless, Li et al. (2023b) suggested that GPEE is the highest in MGPA, primarily due to their use of the ecological value of food planting as a measure of the environmental impact, which, to a certain extent, overlooks the agricultural carbon sequestration function. In terms of GPEE, Tibet takes the lead in ecological efficiency, followed by Hainan, Shanghai, and Qinghai, while Shanxi, Gansu, and Yunnan exhibit comparatively lower GPEE, consistent with Liao et al. (2021) findings, but in contrast with Li et al. (2023b) results. This variance can be attributed to the choice of indicators for expected output. The shift from a traditional agricultural society to a modern one in China has led to substantial changes in grain production inputs and outputs due to reforms and policies, shaping incentives and constraints. Over time, provincial GPEE has consistently improved (Liu et al., 2020a). However, given differences in regional resource endowments, agricultural productivity, and industrial policies, the temporal and spatial evolution of provincial GPEE displays notable inter-provincial variations. For instance, between 1998 and 2021, Shandong's provincial grain ecological efficiency increased from 0.732 to 1.033, while

Hannan's declined from 1.175 to 1.051, emphasizing the resource conservation and environmental protection challenges of agricultural modernization (Huang and Jiang, 2019). GPEE in China showcases a spatial distribution pattern characterized by higher concentrations in peripheral areas and lower concentrations in central areas, consistent with the observations of Liao et al. (2021). Factors contributing to this dispersion include regional disparities in resource allocation, agricultural productivity, and development strategies. Currently, although a pattern of coordinated development among regions centered around Heilongjiang, Inner Mongolia, Henan, Yunnan, and Gansu has been established, spatial agglomeration's impact remains minimal.

The determinants of GPEE were explored through panel data analysis using the Tobit model. Several factors were found to have a positive impact on GPEE, such as EDL, APS, IG, and RIS. Conversely, DL and RI were found to exert a significantly negative influence on GPEE. This finding is consistent with Liu et al. (2020a) but diverges from previous research by Wang et al. (2023b), which indicated an insignificant relationship between EDL and ecological efficiency. The disparity can be attributed to the different measurements of EDL used in the studies. Wang et al. employed per capita GDP, while this study used rural per capita disposable income, which was found to have a more direct and targeted impact on GPEE. Meanwhile, regions with higher rural per capita disposable income tend to have farmers with greater financial resources, enabling greater investment in organic fertilizers and ecofriendly pesticide inputs. This, in turn, leads to decreased agricultural non-point source pollution, resulting in increased GPEE. Surprisingly, ML was found to have no significant impact on GPEE. This finding differs from previous research by Fu et al. (2022), which indicated a significant relationship between ML and ecological efficiency. The possible reason for the difference may be although mechanization holds the potential to reduce labor costs and enhance production efficiency, it also contributes to the consumption of fossil resources in the form of fuel and increases carbon emissions, thus counteracting its positive effects on ecological efficiency. Hence, the effect of ML on GPEE remains uncertain.

## 4.2. Implications

The analysis presented here sheds light on several critical considerations and driving factors that influence GPEE within the context of China's ongoing energy transition. These insights offer valuable implications for policymakers, agricultural practitioners, and environmental advocates striving to navigate the intricate interplay between grain production and sustainability in China. Technological innovation stands out as a fundamental driving force behind the improvement of GPEE (Wang et al., 2021). The adoption of advanced agricultural technologies, such as precision farming, genetically modified crops, and data-driven decision-making tools, has the potential to significantly enhance resource utilization and reduce environmental impacts (Kamble et al., 2020; Lowenberg-DeBoer et al., 2021; Agarwal, 2023). However, ensuring that these technologies are accessible to all farmers, particularly those in remote or economically disadvantaged regions, remains a challenge (Lin et al., 2021). Policymakers should, therefore, prioritize initiatives that facilitate technology transfer and training for all agricultural communities, ensuring a more equitable adoption of sustainable practices.

Government policies and incentives play a pivotal role in shaping the eco-efficiency landscape of grain production. Subsidies, tax incentives, and support for sustainable farming practices can all encourage farmers to adopt eco-friendly approaches. It is imperative for policymakers to design and implement targeted policies that not only promote resourceefficient techniques but also align with broader energy transition goals. These policies should provide a clear regulatory framework that fosters eco-efficiency without compromising food security. Stringent environmental regulations can act as a powerful driver for enhancing GPEE by imposing stricter standards and incentivizing cleaner agricultural practices (Feng and Li, 2020; Liu et al., 2020b). However, effective enforcement remains challenging, and a one-size-fits-all approach may not accommodate the diversity of agricultural landscapes across China (Devi et al., 2022). Tailored regulations that consider regional variations and local environmental conditions should be developed and enforced, thus striking a balance between environmental protection and agricultural productivity.

The adoption of sustainable agricultural practices, including crop rotation, organic farming, and reduced chemical pesticide usage, emerges as a vital factor in achieving GPEE (Biernat et al., 2020; Saber et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2023c). Encouraging farmers to embrace these practices requires a combination of educational initiatives, financial incentives, and market support for sustainably generated products. Fostering a culture of sustainable farming can lead to reduced resource consumption, minimized environmental impacts, and enhanced crop yields over the long term (Qi et al., 2021b). GPEE can also be influenced by consumer preferences and demands for sustainably produced food (Prom-u-thai and Rerkasem, 2020). As consumers become increasingly conscious of the environmental and health implications of their consumption choices, an opportunity emerges for farmers to respond to market signals by adopting eco-friendly practices (Talanow et al., 2021). Communication and certification schemes that educate consumers about GPEE can stimulate demand for sustainable products, thereby incentivizing farmers to align their practices with environmental goals (Zakaria et al., 2020).

In conclusion, achieving GPEE within the framework of China's energy transition necessitates a multifaceted approach that integrates technological innovation, policy coherence, regional adaptability, sustainable practices, and consumer engagement. Collaborative efforts involving the government, agricultural stakeholders, researchers, and civil society are essential to navigate the complex landscape of grain production and advance sustainability while contributing to China's broader energy transition objectives. Ultimately, striking a harmonious balance between the economic imperatives of food security and the ecological imperatives of sustainability will be key to success in this crucial endeavor.

## 5. Conclusions

The pressing dual objectives of achieving peak carbon emissions and carbon neutrality impose stringent requirements on the sustainable progression of agriculture in China. In light of the critical imperatives of ensuring food security and aligning with carbon reduction targets, the Chinese agricultural sector must swiftly transition toward a high-quality development trajectory. This paper introduces an innovative evaluation index system by incorporating both carbon absorption and carbon emissions. The evaluation spans 31 provinces in China from 1998 to

2021, encompassing MGPA, MGCA, and BGPCA, employing the super-SBM-windows-DEA model. The analysis not only delves into the spatial-temporal evolution patterns but also employs the panel Tobit model to decipher the underlying mechanisms of various influencing factors.

The research findings highlight that the average GPEE across provinces has demonstrated a fluctuating growth trend. Specifically, MGCA displayed the highest level of efficiency, followed by MGPA with an intermediate level, while BGPCA exhibited the lowest GPEE since 1998. Over this period, GPEE in MGPA and MGCA has remained relatively stable, whereas BGPCA displayed more substantial variations. The spatial distribution of GPEE across China reveals a distinct pattern, characterized by high values in peripheral areas and low values in the central region, presenting a random scattered distribution. The research identifies various factors influencing GPEE, including EDL, APS, IG, and RIS, all of which have a positive impact. In contrast, DL and RI have a significant negative effect on GPEE. These findings contribute to enhancing GPEE and provide valuable insights for decision-makers to promote sustainable development within the agricultural sector, particularly in the context of developing nations.

This paper delivers valuable insights; however, it is essential to acknowledge two inherent limitations that present promising avenues for future research. Firstly, the scope of this study encompasses the assessment of GPEE in China from 1998 to 2021, unveiling its temporal and spatial characteristics. To provide a more comprehensive and longterm perspective, future research could consider extending the study period to collect additional data and encompass broader trends. Secondly, due to the constraints of data availability, this study operates at the provincial level. For more targeted and refined policy recommendations, future research could narrow down the focus to specific research objects or entities within the provincial context, thus offering more precise guidance for policymakers and stakeholders.
