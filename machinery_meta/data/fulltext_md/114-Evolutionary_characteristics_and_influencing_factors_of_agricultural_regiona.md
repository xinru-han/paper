Abstract: The rapid expansion of cities seriously threatens the sustainable development of agriculture in China. Exploring the evolution law and influencing mechanism of agricultural regional system in the process of urbanization is of great significance for promoting sustainable development of agriculture in China. This paper takes the Loess Plateau (LP) as an example, and constructs a research framework to study the effect of urbanization on agricultural regional system through the lens of human-earth interaction, aiming at elucidating the evolutionary characteristics of agricultural regional system and revealing the impact law of urbanization. The results show that: (1) The growth trend of the evolution index of the agricultural regional system in the LP was significant, gradually evolving into a spatial pattern of "high in the north and south, low in the east and west". (2) The hot spot and sub-hot spot zones of the agricultural regional system evolution index in the LP were mainly distributed in the south and north, while the cold spot and sub-cold spot

zones were primarily located in the center, east and west. (3) The levels of agricultural mechanization, agricultural land productivity, cropland area, and agricultural labor productivity were the main internal influencing factors of the agricultural regional system in the LP. The obstacle degree of agricultural mechanization level, cropland area, and the proportion of agricultural employees increased over time, while the obstacle degree of agricultural land productivity and grain yield capacity decreased. (4) The impact of population urbanization in the LP showed a spatial pattern of "inhibition in the southeast and promotion in the northwest", the impact of economic urbanization was dominated by inhibition, and the impact of land urbanization showed a spatial pattern of "promotion in the whole and inhibition in the local". This study provides ideas for the comprehensive research on the evolution and influencing factors of agricultural regional system, and offers practical references for achieving sustainable agricultural development in LP.

Keywords: Agricultural regional system; Loess Plateau; Urbanization; Geographically and temporally weighted regression

## 1 Introduction

Agriculture is an important foundation of the national economy and a necessary condition for human survival and development (Liu et al. 2020; Li and Li 2019). Promoting sustainable agricultural development is of great significance in realizing rural revitalization and guaranteeing food security (Schindler et al. 2015). Since the 197os, in order to accelerate the process of realizing socialist modernization, China has vigorously developed its industry and urban construction, and upgraded the level of industrialization and urbanization (Chen et al. 2013; Feng et al. 2019; Fang and Liu 2009). China's urbanization rate exceeded 50% for the first time in 2011 and climbs to 65.72% in 2021, already reaching the world's average level of urbanization (Wu et al. 2024; Zhu et al. 2022). The long-term heavy industryoriented industrial structure and the development model that emphasizes the city over the countryside have prompted a large number of rural populations, resources and capital to flow to the city, leading to unbalanced development in urban and rural areas, and to the imbalance in the relationship between humans and earth in the countryside (Liu 2018; Qu et al. 2019; Feng et al. 2019). The share of agriculture in the regional economy has been declining, and the aging rate of the rural population continues to climb. In 20oo, the population aged 65 and above in rural China accounted for only 7.35% of the total rural population. By 2020, this proportion had increased to 17.72%. The area of cropland resources has also been continuously decreasing. Between 2001 and 2019, China's net arable land area decreased by nearly 35 million acres, accounting for about 1.70% of the total arable land in 2019. The severe issue of "agricultural diseases" has become a key constraint on the sustainable development of agriculture in China (Liu et al. 2016; Chen 2007) Agricultural development should effectively combine social, economic and ecological benefits(Maciasfauria 2018). To address the "three rural issues" (agriculture, rural areas, and farmers), China has successively implemented strategies such as integrated urban-rural development, new rural construction, and new urbanization, achieving phased results. However, many problems and contradictions still exist between urban and rural areas, as well as between industry and agriculture (Liu and Li 2017; Li et al. 2014; Liu 2018). Therefore, how to accelerate the establishment of a new urban-rural relationship and promote sustainable agricultural development driven by urbanization is a key issue that needs to be studied in depth.

The study of the relationship between urbanization and agriculture has progressively gained attention in the academic community in recent years, especially the study of the influence of urbanization on food security, cropland utilization, and labor migration is more abundant (Chen et al. 2023; Fukase and Martin 2016; Brend'Amour et al. 2017; Wu et al. 2019). In discussing the connection between urbanization and food security, some scholars have argued that urbanization is an important cause of the decline in food production, that urbanization has contributed to the exodus of agricultural labour, and that the expansion ofland for urban building land has occupied a large quantity of cropland and exacerbated the non grainization of cultivated land, thereby threatening food security (Hou et al. 2022; Chen et al. 2007; Chen 2007). In contrast, the scholars who hold a contrasting view, contending that urbanization can contribute to enhancing the intensive utilization of cropland and promoting the modernization of agriculture. This, they argue, will ultimately enhance food production efficiency in the long run (Godfray et al. 201o; Luo et al. 2016). Examining the issue from the standpoint of labor, while urbanization has spurred regional economic development, expedited the migration of surplus agricultural labor force, and augmented the economic income of agricultural households, it has also depleted a considerable number of young and robust work force resources, aggravated the aging of the agricultural labor force (Taylor 1999; Liu et al. 201o). Research on the impact of urbanization on cropland has found that urbanization can lead to a rapid loss of cropland resources (Liu and Qiao 2014). However, the transfer of land also provides possibilitiesforlarge-scaleandmechanized agricultural production, helping to improve land production efficiency (Li et al. 2017). In summary, there is a dialectical relationship between urbanization and agriculture. Urbanization has both positive and negative impacts on agricultural development. Current research focuses more on the impact of urbanization on a specific element or aspect of agriculture, such as arable land, labor force, grain output, etc. There is little research that comprehensively analyzes the impact of urbanization on it from a systematic perspective. China's rapid urbanization and the continuously widening urban-rural gap have increased the complexity of agricultural development (Li et al. 2024)

So, the systematic research on the impact of urbanization processes on agriculture needs to be further deepened.

The LP is one of the four major plateaus in China, with complex and varied topography and prominent human-earth conflicts. At the same time, the region is also an important grain-producing area in northern China. Ecological protection, human-earth system coordination and sustainable development have always been the basic propositions for the high-quality development of the LP (Liu et al. 2020). Since the 199os, the region has carried out large-scale Green for Grain Project, and there have been remarkable changes in the regional pattern of agriculture and the quality of the ecological environment (Qu et al. 2020). Currently, the agricultural development of the LP is in a critical transition period (Qu et al. 2022b). Although the agro-ecological environment has improved, urbanization has also developed rapidly since the Western Development Strategy was undertaken, resulting in the formation of a number of national level urban agglomerations, and the enhancement of human activities as well as the rapid loss of agricultural resources have resulted in new challenges to the development of the agriculture of the LP (Fang 2015; Fu et al. 2017; Feng et al. 2022). Therefore, it is urgent to resolve the following scientific issues: What are the evolutionary characteristics of the agricultural regional system in the LP during rapid urbanization? How do changes in the internal elements of agricultural regionalsystemandexternalurbanization perturbations affect the system evolution? Based on this, this paper constructs a research framework for the impact of urbanization on agricultural regional system. Taking 322 districts and counties in the LP as an example, we explore the spatial and temporal evolution characteristics of the agricultural regional system in the process of urbanization and reveal the internal and external influence mechanisms. Ultimately, our goal is to provide decision-making reference for the optimization of management strategies to achieve sustainable development of agriculture in the LP.

## 2 Materials and Methods

## 2.1 Research framework

The human-earth relationship is one of the most fundamental relationships in human society and is an important element of modern human-earth system science research (Liu et al. 2024). In the human-earth relationship, human beings have subjective initiative and can take the initiative to recognize, utilize and transform the geographical environment. The earth is the material basis and spatial carrier on which human beings depend for their survival, and constrains their activities (Liu 2020). Exploring the evolutionary process of human-earth system, its action mechanism and its complex interaction effect is the core content of research on modern human-earth system science.

The agricultural regional system is a complex human-earth system characterized by dissipative structure, openness, and dynamic properties (Cheng 201o). The system is a territorial complex based on a certain space for agricultural activities, characterized by the interaction and spatial-temporal changes in agricultural production activities and the agricultural resource environment, and having multifunctional values such as agricultural production, ecological protection, social security and food security (Zhang 2022). The agricultural regional system can be divided into two aspects: agricultural production activities and the agricultural resource environment (Fig. 1). Agricultural production activities are an important way for human beings to utilize the agricultural resource environment in order to obtain the agricultural products needed for survival. The way of agricultural production activities is directly influenced by the characteristics of social development and the level of economic development. The agricultural resource environment is expressed in the available agricultural resource and the quality of the ecological environment in which they are located. The evolution of agricultural regional system is the result of dynamic changes in internal elements and influence from external factors, with the core outcome being the formation of agriculture-landrelationshipswithdifferent characteristics. In the process of system evolution, agricultural production activities and the agricultural resource environment are in a relationship of mutual utilization and constraint. Social progress and economic development help promote the more efficient use of agricultural resource environment by humans, and the agricultural resource environment is an important foundation for agricultural production activities, directly determining the breadth and depth of agricultural production activities. This paper starts from the perspectives of agricultural production activities and agricultural resource environment, constructing an evaluation system for the agricultural regional system evolution index that includes four dimensions: "society, economy, resources, ecology". The agricultural regional system evolution index is used to describe the spatial-temporal evolutionary characteristics of the system. A higher evolution index indicates a better development state of the agricultural regional system. This method can reveal the development dynamics and trends of agricultural regional system more accurately and systematically.

Urbanization is regarded as an external disturbance to the agricultural regional system. Urbanization is a comprehensive process that can be divided into aspects such as population urbanization, economic urbanization, and land urbanization based on the differences in its impact on agriculture (Li et al. 2015). The essence of population urbanization is the process of rural population concentrating towards cities. This process leads to the loss of agricultural labor force, but it is also an important way to increase farmers' family income. Economic urbanization is the process of the agglomeration of non-agricultural economic elements in urban spaces. The rapid development of secondary and tertiary industries attracts more resources to flow towards towns and cities, affecting the growth of the agricultural economy. Land urbanization is the process of urban land expanding outward, which reduces the amount of arable land and compresses the production space for agriculture, but it also provides the possibility for the intensification of arable land (Chu et al. 2022). Urbanization drives changes in the structure of agricultural regions in various ways, thereby affecting the evolution of agricultural regional system. In view of the above analysis, this paper carries out an in-depth analysis of the evolutionary characteristics and influence mechanism of the agriculture regional system in the LP during the process of urbanization through the logic of "portraying the system evolution pattern - identifying the internal influencing factors - analyzing the influence of the external urbanization - constructing the system regulation path".

## 2.2 Study area

With an overall area of about 650,000 km², the LP of China covers a geographic region that is roughly between 33°41′-41°16'N and 100°54′-114°33′E (Fig. 2). It involves seven provinces and autonomous regions and a total of 34o counties, making it one of China's four major plateaus with extremely complex terrain. The climate varies significantly between the northern and southern regions, with average precipitation ranging from 400-70omm. The average temperature ranges from 3.4°C-14.3°C (Cao et al. 2018). The LP has a long history of agricultural development, with dry farming as the main type and the main crops being wheat, corn, potatoes, etc. (Wang et al. 202ob). This region is a typical ecologically fragile area in China and also the area with the most severe soil erosion in the world (Fu et al. 2023). The harsh ecological environment, complex terrain conditions, and backward production systems have led to low agricultural land production efficiency, which greatly restricts the development of regional agriculture. Simultaneously, the urbanization process has made significant progress under the implementation of the Western Development Strategy, with the urbanization rate growing from 34.9% in 2000 to 63.8% in 2020 (Fu et al. 2023). However, the continuous increase in urbanization rates has further exacerbated agricultural issues in the LP region, including urban growth at the expense of rural decline, loss of agricultural labor, and abandonment of arable land. Due to the unavailability of data from some districts and counties, 322 districts and counties in the LP are used in this paper.

## 2.3 Data sources

The data covered by the study are divided into three periods (2000, 2010 and 2020) and consist mainly of social statistical data and natural environment data. (1) Social statistical data are mainly from China County Statistical Yearbook, statistical yearbooks of provinces, cities and counties, statistical bulletins and government work reports. (2) Land use data at a resolution of 3o m are from the Chinese Academy of Sciences' Resource and Environment Science and Data Centre (https://www.resdc.cn/), 3om resolution DEM data are from the Geospatial Data Cloud Platform (http://www.gscloud.cn/), 30 m resolution NDVI data are from the National Ecological Science Data Centre (http://www.nesdc.org.cn), 1 km resolution meteorological data are from the National Scientific Data Centre for the Tibetan Plateau (https://data.tpdc.ac.cn/).

## 2.4 Construction of the indicator system

Urbanization's impact on agriculture is often accompanied by processes such as population migration, industrial structure transformation, and land-use conversion. The urbanization process is mainly broken down into three components for measurement: population urbanization, economic urbanization, and land urbanization, based on prior research (Shi et al. 202ob; Zhang et al. 2022). Therefore, we choose to use the percentage of urban population, the percentage of secondary and tertiary industries, and the percentage of construction land to measure urbanization (Table 1).

The agricultural regional system is a complex human-earth system closely linked to socio-economic and resource environments, with its main components including agricultural production activities and agricultural resource environments. The intensity and modes of agricultural production activities are determined by the developmental state of agricultural society and agricultural economy (Marc et al. 2017). And the core of the agricultural resource environment is the total volume of agricultural resources and the quality of the agricultural ecological environment. Therefore, this paper chooses to measure the evolution index of agricultural regional system from four dimensions: "society, economy, resources, and ecology". The development level of agricultural social factors is mainly reflected in the agricultural population structure, the level of agricultural modernization, and the development of rural society. Therefore, the proportion of agricultural employees, the level of agricultural mechanization, and the land area of rural settlements per capita are selected for measurement. Rural settlement land is the carrier of rural living space, however, the phenomenon of population exodus from China's countryside is obvious at present. The larger per capita land area of rural settlements indicates the lower degree of intensive use of rural residential land, so it is treated as a reverse indicator (Cheng et al. 2019). The development level of agricultural economic factors is mainly reflected from the perspective of agricultural output. In this paper, agricultural land productivity, agricultural labor productivity and capacity of grain yields are selected to characterize the level of agricultural economic development. Agricultural resource factors mainly include land resources, water resources and climate resources required for agricultural production, which are the basis for the evolution of agricultural regional systems. Therefore, this paper characterizes the status of agricultural resources through the cropland area, annual precipitation, annual average temperature and annual hours of sunshine. Agricultural ecological factors measure the quality of the ecological environment to which agricultural resources belong, and good ecological environment quality is the key to ensure agricultural production. Therefore, in this paper, it is measured by NDVI, cropland elevation and cropland slope. Among them, the higher the elevation of the cropland and the greater the slope, the worse the environment for agricultural production and the more unfavorable it is to carry out agricultural activities, so it is used as a negative indicator (Table 1). The VIF of each indicator is tested to be less than 10, proving that there is no significant multicollinearity.

## 2.5.3 Obstacle Degree Model

System evaluation requires, on the one hand, the portrayal of the evolutionary characteristics of the system and, on the other hand, the identification of the key factors that constrain the evolution of the system in order to facilitate the diagnosis of system problems (Chen et al. 2020). Therefore, this paper introduces an obstacle degree model to identify the key obstacle factors within the agricultural regional system of the LP. The obstacle degree model is a commonly used mathematical model that can accurately identify key factors constraining system development in comprehensive evaluations. The model identifies obstacle factors through the influence degree of the indicator on the objective, the deviation of the indicator and the degree of obstacles (Ou et al. 2017). The influence degree of the indicators on the objectives is expressed by the weight of each objective, the deviation of the indicator is expressed by the difference between each indicator and the optimal value of each indicator, and finally the main obstacle factors in the system are identified by ranking the magnitude of the obstacle degree (Wang et al. 2020a; Zhang et al. 2021). ## 3.2 Evolutionary index hot spot analysis

With hot spot analysis, the spatial clustering properties of high- and low-value locations can be thoroughly investigated. In this study, we calculate the local Gi\* statistics of agricultural regional system evolution index in each district and county of the LP and obtain the Z-value. They were separated into hot spot zones, sub-hot spot zone, transition spot zones, sub-cold spot zones, and cold spot zones from high to low using the natural breakpoint approach. The distribution map and quantity change map of the cold and hot spots of the evolution index of the agricultural geographical system in the LP were drawn (Fig. 4).

The evolution index of the agricultural regional system in the LP from 2000 to 2020 showed obvious spatial clustering characteristics. The hot spot and subhot spot zones were mainly distributed in the plain areas of the southern and northern parts of the LP, which had flat terrain, close proximity to rivers, and good agricultural production conditions. The proportion of districts and counties in the hot spot zones increased from 4% in 2000 to 11% in 2020, with continuous expansion in the southern region. The proportion of districts and counties in the sub-hot spot zones decreased from 34% in 2000 to 28% in 2020, with a significant reduction in the northern region. The cold spot and sub-cold spot zones were mainly distributed in the eastern, western, and central regions of the LP, which were mainly composed of loess hilly and gully region, loess sorghum gully region, and earth-rocky mountainous region. The terrain was relatively complex and the agricultural production conditions were poor. The cold spot zones gradually shifted from the central LP to the eastern and western regions, with the proportion of districts and counties increasing from 15% in 2000 to 19% in 2020. The subcold spot zones expanded in the southeastern region, with the proportion of districts and counties increasing from 24% in 2000 to 34% in 2020.

The characteristics of cold and hot spot spatial changes indicated that the low-value agglomeration areas of the agricultural regional system evolution index on the LP gradually shifted from the central part to the eastern loess sorghum gully region and the western earth-rocky mountainous region, while the high-valueagglomerationareascontinuously expanded in the southern plain.

## 4 Discussion

## 4.1 Evolutionary characteristics of agricultural regional system in the LP

The LP is an important grain-producing area in northern China, but the ecological environment is fragile and soil erosion is serious (Qu et al. 2022a). A large-scale project of returning farmland to forest and grassland has been carried out on the LP since the 199os, resulting in a marked improvement in the quality of the regional ecological environment. Meanwhile, there has been a shift in the distribution of cultivated land resources from high altitude and high slope areas to low altitude and low slope areas. The agricultural regional space is gradually being transformedfromroughly-managedmountain agriculture to intensive plains agriculture (Lv et al 2012; Wang et al. 2016; Shi et al. 2020a). However, the problem of uneven agricultural development in the internal regions of the LP remains severe. The study results show that the agricultural regional system evolution index of Inner Mongolia in the north, Guanzhong Plain and Henan in the south of the LP is higher, and it is also a hot spot area of evolution index. This is mainly because the terrain in this area is flat the altitude is relatively low, and it is close to water sources such as the Yellow River and Weihe River, making the area more suitable for carrying out agricultural production activities. Especially in the southern region of the LP, the climatic conditions can satisfy two or three crops a year, and the agricultural climatic resources are excellent, so there is a comparatively high level of agricultural development. However, the agricultural regional system evolution index in the western and eastern parts of the LP is generally low and the increase is small. This is mainly because this region is composed of loess sorghum gully region, loess hilly gully region and earth-rocky mountainous region, with complex terrain, high fragmentation of cultivated land, serious soil erosion problems. Simultaneously, the region is mainly operated by individual farmers, with low production efficiency and insufficient endogenous driving force, which leads to tensions in agriculture and land relations and slower agricultural development Although the agricultural development in the northern part of Shaanxi in the middle of the LP is relatively backward, it is the main project area for Grain for Green. In the past 20 years, the average elevation of cultivated land has decreased by 15.13m, the average slope has reduced by 1.19°, and the ecological environment has significantly improved. The region has also made significant progress in promoting agricultural modernization and increasing agricultural economic income through the development of characteristicagriculturalindustries,channel agriculture construction, and other methods. Therefore, the agricultural regional system evolution index has been rapidly improved.

## 4.2 Analysis of internal and external influencing factors on the agricultural regional system of the LP

Agricultural regional system is a complex and open system under the interaction between human and earth, and the influence of urbanization on agricultural regional system is complex and variable. Under the systemic perspective, it is more conducive to analyze the influencing factors as internal and external to clarify the influencing mechanism.

The level of agricultural mechanization, agricultural land productivity, cropland area, and agricultural labor productivity are main obstacle factors within the agricultural regional system of the LP. The LP has a dry climate and complex terrain. In particular, the average slope of cultivated land in areas such as the loess hilly and gully region, the loess sorghum gully region, and the earth-rocky mountainous region reaches 9.38 degrees, and the fragmentation is relatively high, making it difficult to implement scaled mechanical production. As a result, in 2020, approximately 45% of the districts and counties had a total agricultural machinery power of less than 150,00o kilowatts, while the total agricultural machinery power in the plain districts and counties of the LP could reach a maximum of more than 1 million kilowatts. Currently, agricultural production in some areas of the LP still continues the traditional lowefficiency farming methods, and this kind of extensive planting and thin-harvesting production method leads to a serious low agricultural production efficiency (Chen et al. 2015). Simultaneously, within the backdrop of the swift advancement of regional urbanization, the depletion of youthful and robust labour in rural areas, the ageing of the agricultural labour force, and other issues are exacerbated. From 2010 to 2020, the number of rural agricultural employees in the provinces and regions of the LP decreased by approximately 33%, and the proportion of the rural elderly population increased from 8.05% to 15.28%. In addition, driven by the Grain for Green Project and urbanization, the total amount of cultivated land resources on the LP decreased by 9.26% from 2000 to 2020, particularly in Gansu, Shaanxi, and Qinghai regions where the total amount of cultivated land resources decreased by more than 14%. The shortage of cultivated land resources in some areas has also limited the enhancement of the agricultural regional system evolution index (Wang et al. 2024; Liu et al. 2022). When conducting a correlation analysis on key obstacle factors, it can also be found that the area of cultivated land resources shows a significant tradeoff relationship with other factors (Fig. 7). Therefore, rationally allocating urban development and ensuring the quantity and quality of cropland are the keys to promoting sustainable agricultural development.

Outside the system, urbanization affects agricultural regional system in various ways. Population urbanization exhibits an inhibitory effect in the southeastern region of the LP. This is primarily due to the region's flat terrain and humid climate, which offer relatively favorable agricultural resource conditions. Especially in the Guanzhong Plain area, the level of population urbanization is higher, and urbanrural transportation is convenient. This facilitates many rural laborers to work in cities, leading to restricted agricultural production. However, the socioeconomic level and agricultural conditions in the northwestern region are relatively underdeveloped. Except for the Hetao Plain, cultivated land in other areas is dry land with a low land output rate. The increase in the proportion of urban population has stimulated urban domestic demand, improved living standards and consumption capacity, promoted agricultural development, and provided possibilities for specialized production. Therefore, population urbanization in this region mainly shows a promoting effect.

Economic urbanization in the LP region is mainly manifested as an inhibitory effect. Agriculture's place in the regional economy has steadily decreased as society and the economy have grown. The secondary and tertiary industries centered on cities will bring greater economic benefits (Lin and Ho 2003). The increase in the proportion of secondary and tertiary industries means that non-agricultural industries will attract more labor, capital and other agricultural resources,thus restricting thesustainable development of agriculture. Particularly in regions such as Inner Mongolia, Shanxi, and Shaanxi, which are rich in mineral resources and have a developed secondary industry, the inhibitory effect of economic urbanization is more pronounced.

Land urbanization is mainly a process of continuous expansion of construction land, which can provide infrastructure support for agricultural development and strengthen inter-district and intercounty linkages and division of labor. Additionally, land urbanization also provides the possibility for the scaling and specialization of cultivated land use. Especially in the southern plains areas where arable land resources are abundant and the cultivation of cash crops is relatively developed. The continuous progression of land urbanization has enhanced the development of related infrastructure, strengthened the circulation of agricultural products, and also promoted the scaling and specialization of agricultural production, significantly improving agricultural production efficiency, and therefore mainly manifesting as a facilitating effect. However, the terrain in the western region is relatively complex, with a scarcity and high fragmentation of quality arable land resources. The expansion of urban land use encroaches upon some areas with prime production conditions. As urban land continues to expand, it increasingly threatens farmland resources, hence the inhibitory effect of land urbanization is continually growing.

## 4.3 Regulation mechanism and insufficient research

The results of this study indicate that in the process of urbanization, the evolution index of the agricultural regional system in the LP has gradually increased, but there are significant differences in the evolutionary characteristics and influencing factors among regions. This also provides important insights for the future sustainable development of agriculture in the LP: (1) Scientifically implementing the Green for Grain Project, and strengthening the supporting role of ecological environment on agricultural regional system. Driven by the Green for Grain Project, the ecological environment quality of the LP has been significantly improved, and the evolution index of the agricultural regional system has been effectively enhanced. Therefore, on the basis of adhering to the principle of "adapting measures to local conditions," we should continue to carry out the project of returning farmland to forests and grasslands, while also strengthening the protection of existing forest lands and improving forest quality (Wang and Zhang 2017). To provide a good natural resource base for agricultural development. (2) Improve the level of specialization and mechanization in agriculture on the LP. The level of agricultural mechanization is a major obstacle to the evolution of the agricultural regional system in places like Shanxi Shaanxi, and Inner Mongolia. Therefore, the region should adopt various methods to promote land transfer, encourage the centralized operation of land on a larger scale, and thereby achieve the mechanization of agricultural cultivation on a larger scale (Li et al. 2024). In addition, to be widely applicable to complex agricultural farming terrains such as hills and mountains, the use of small agricultural machinery should also be vigorously promoted. (3) Adhere to the red line of cropland. The rapid reduction of slope cropland in the LP over the past two decades has led to increased constraints on land resources (Zhang et al. 2020). The degree of obstacles to cropland resources in Qinghai, Gansu, and Shaanxi has significantly increased, and the inhibitory effect of land urbanization on the agricultural regional system is also continuously strengthening. Firstly, the strictest cultivated land protection policy should be implemented to maintain a generally stable grain planting area, comprehensively address the issue of abandoned farmland, and gradually improve the quality of cultivated land resources. Secondly, we should focus on optimizing the spatial layout, reasonably divide the areas suitable for returning farmland and the areas of cultivated land that need to be protected according to the terrain characteristics and ecological conditions of different regions, to maximize both food security and ecological environment. Moreover, the government should comprehensively consider issues such as population size, cultivated land pattern, and ecological protection, reasonably plan the expansion of construction land and reduce the occupation of cultivated land resources (Hu et al. 2023). (4) The economic urbanization of the LP region has had a significant inhibitory effect on the evolution of the agricultural regional system, especially in the northern and eastern regions. Therefore, these regions should strongly support the development of distinctive agricultural industries, actively promote the upgrading of agricultural industries, and form a modernagriculturalindustrialsystemwith coordinated development of primary, secondary, and tertiary industries. At the same time, we should promote the extension of urban infrastructure, public services, and modern management methods to rural areas, promote the integrated construction of urban and rural areas, and avoid the unidirectional loss of agricultural and rural resources.

This paper is only the beginning of an investigation into how urbanization process affects the agricultural regional system, more studies will be conducted in the future. Recently, the rapid development of urbanization has led to a large influx of agricultural resources into cities, seriously threatening the stability and sustainability of agricultural development. How to promote the sustainable development of agriculture under the background of rapid urbanization and build a new urban-rural working-peasantry relationship is a hot topic of concern in academic circles. This article constructs a research framework from the viewpoint of humanearthinteraction,explorestheevolution characteristics and internal and external influencing factors of the agricultural regional system in the LP under the background of urbanization, and due to the availability of data, only panel data from three years are selected for research. In order to better guide regional agricultural development, future research should select longer time series data for related research. Secondly, this study is based on county scale, and different scales of research may show different impact results, future research should investigate the variations in the effects of urbanisation at different scales as well as the underlying mechanisms.

## 5 Conclusions

(1) From 2000 to 2020, the evolution index of the agricultural regional system in the LP showed a significant growth trend, gradually evolving into a spatial pattern of "high in the north and south, low in the east and west". The districts and counties with a larger increase in the evolutionary index were mainly concentrated in the central part of the LP, while those with a smaller increase or decline were mainly distributed in the western and southeastern parts of the LP.

(2) Evolution index of agricultural regional system in the LP showed obvious spatial clustering characteristics. The hot spot and sub-hot spot zones were mainly distributed in the south and north. The cold spot and sub-cold spot zones were mainly distributed in the east, center and west. Hot spot zones expanded in the south, while cold spot and sub-cold spot zones gradually shifted to the east and west.

(3) The level of agricultural mechanization, agricultural land productivity, cropland area, and agricultural labor productivity were the main obstacle factors. With time, the obstacle degrees of agricultural mechanization level, cropland area, and the proportion of agricultural employees increased, while the obstacle degrees of agricultural land productivity and capacity of grain yields decreased.

(4) The impact of population urbanization on the agricultural regional system in the LP presented a spatial pattern of "promotion in the northwest and inhibition in the southeast". The economic urbanization showed increasing inhibition, while the impact of land urbanization exhibited a spatial pattern of "overall promotion and local inhibition".
