# Reviewer Comments (extracted from "Reviewer comments.rtf")

> Manuscript: *Alternative pathways for China's diet transition: Implications for food security, nutrition and greenhouse gas emissions to 2050* (manuscript 0608.docx)

Overall: original and important gap, but execution falls short — unclear methodology, inadequately documented data sources, presentation fails to communicate findings.

## General comments

1. **Methodology not clearly explained.** Equations only cover the nutrition/emissions conversion of CASM outputs; the market-equilibrium model mechanism is never explained or cited.
2. **CASM not referenced** — readers cannot examine the specification independently.
3. **Data sources not clearly identified** (e.g., food composition tables attributed only to "national nutrition authorities").
4. **Out-of-home consumption extension undocumented** — how was it done, with what data?
5. **Trade vs. emissions inconsistency**: model incorporates international trade but emissions use only Chinese production coefficients; not acknowledged anywhere, including limitations.
6. **No in-text references to appendices**, though they contain essential information.
7. **Scenario codes A1–C6 not defined in main text** — several passages uninterpretable.
8. **Healthy diet benchmark quantities never tabulated**; base-year consumption data not shown — reader cannot assess starting point.
9. **Presentation**: only structurally identical tables of absolute quantities; add percentage-change tables and figures (bar charts etc.).
10. **Results section monotonous** — needs more varied, engaging presentation.
11. Citations supporting one idea should appear in ascending order of year.

## Specific comments

- p4 r1: "this transition has improved food availability" does not follow logically; rewrite.
- p4 r12–14: energy intake declined while fat share rose — needs explanation (fat has more kcal/g).
- p6 r7: colon required after "follows".
- p6 r13: citation needed for CASM.
- p6 r29: why exchange rates? Does the model capture imports (e.g., beef from AUS/NZ)? How much of each category is imported? Clarify.
- p7 r3–5: database update for out-of-home consumption, urban–rural differences, supply-use consistency — how, and with what datasets?
- p7 r26: "food income elasticities", not "demand elasticities".
- p8 r12–14: population projection figures (1.364 bn 2035, 1.254 bn 2050; urbanisation 75.4%/81.4%) need citation.
- p8 Table 1: A6/B6/C6 "ageing considered" undefined in text; appendix not referenced.
- p9 r18–23: rationale for combining dietary recommendation systems into the healthy benchmark; detail in appendix needed.
- p10 r1: list the food groups under analysis.
- p10 data sources: first paragraph incomplete; all sources need clear references for replicability.
- p11: long enumerations of numbers difficult to follow; rewrite with more varied sentence styles (applies throughout results).
- p11 r16/22 etc.: B1, C1 etc. never defined.
- p11 r21: which foods count as "micronutrient-rich"? Explain.
- p12 Table 2: notes must be standalone — spell out all abbreviations (all tables).
- p12 Table 2: processed foods, sweets, sugary drinks not included — expected to rise with income/urbanisation; no mention.
- p12 r7: aggregate demand more sensitive to population than urbanisation is a mathematical given — reframe.
- p14 r6: "C6 remains below C1" uninterpretable without scenario definitions.
- p20 limitations: acknowledge (a) no processed/sugary foods; (b) whether trade is in the model, and how emission estimates vary with domestic vs. imported production coefficients.
