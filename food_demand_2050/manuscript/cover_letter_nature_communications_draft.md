# Cover letter draft: Nature Communications

**Internal status: HOLD - do not submit until the publication-promotion conditions in Supplementary Note 3 are met.**

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **"China's dietary transition redistributes agricultural production and farm-gate emissions across global economies,"** for publication in *Nature Communications*.

Dietary policy is usually evaluated within national borders, even when a large importing and producing economy can transmit consumption change through global prices. We connect China-specific dietary pathways to a global agricultural partial-equilibrium system resolving 193 economy accounts and 31 products. This enables us to identify not only a world total, but also the distribution of production and production-attributed greenhouse-gas responses across regions, income groups and individual economy-product pairs.

Three findings motivate broad interest. First, China's composite-guideline pathway creates opposing global channels: the central SSP2 experiment lowers pigmeat prices by 46% but raises fluid-milk and whole-milk-powder prices by 19% and 23%. Second, 81% of the model-covered farm-gate GHG reduction occurs outside mainland China, concentrated in Asia and the Americas. Third, paired results are stable across five socioeconomic pathways but materially sensitive to demand-system structure; the poultry price response changes sign and the pigmeat effect narrows substantially in a nested-demand specification. This comparison shows that apparent scenario robustness can coexist with structural uncertainty.

The manuscript is explicit about its boundaries. Six food categories in the source China pathway are outside the world model; trade is a net-balance identity rather than bilateral flows; GHG accounting uses frozen 2023 attributed biological farm-gate factors; and no health, welfare, lifecycle or land-use claim is made. Code, mapped inputs, derived outputs, figures, numerical audits and a claim registry accompany the paper.

We believe the study fits *Nature Communications* because it connects consumer-side food policy to global environmental and distributional consequences, resolves where adjustment occurs, and makes model-form uncertainty part of the main result.

This manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and all authors have approved its submission. The authors declare no competing interests.
